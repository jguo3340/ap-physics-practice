stem: |
  A heavy flywheel spins on a frictionless axle. A light rope is attached to
  the flywheel's hub and to a bucket resting on the floor. As the flywheel spins,
  the rope winds onto the hub and lifts the bucket. The bucket rises with a
  constant upward deceleration, and the rope exerts a constant force on the
  hub. While the bucket rises, which describes the torque the rope exerts on the
  flywheel and the flywheel's angular momentum?
choices:
  A: 'Torque: decreasing. Angular momentum: decreasing.'
  B: 'Torque: constant. Angular momentum: constant.'
  C: 'Torque: zero. Angular momentum: decreasing.'
  D: 'Torque: constant. Angular momentum: decreasing.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The rope''s force is constant and acts at the hub''s fixed radius, so its torque does not change. Only the angular momentum shrinks.'
  B: 'A nonzero torque always changes angular momentum. The rope''s torque opposes the spin, so the flywheel slows down.'
  C: 'The rope pulls at the edge of the hub, not through the axle, so it has a lever arm and exerts a torque.'
---

The rope pulls on the hub at a fixed radius $r$ with a constant force $T$, so its torque about the axle is constant:

$$
\tau = rT
$$

That torque opposes the flywheel's rotation (the flywheel is doing the work of lifting the bucket). A constant opposing torque makes the angular momentum drop at a steady rate:

$$
\frac{\Delta L}{\Delta t} = -rT
$$

So the torque is constant and the angular momentum decreases. The flywheel's rotational energy is going into lifting the bucket.

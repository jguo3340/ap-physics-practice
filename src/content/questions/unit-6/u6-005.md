stem: |
  A grinding wheel has a rotational inertia of $0.50\ \text{kg·m}^2$. While it
  is spinning at 10 rad/s, a motor exerts an average torque of
  $3.0\ \text{N·m}$ on it in the direction it is spinning, for 6.0 s. What is
  the change in the wheel's angular momentum?
choices:
  A: '$5.0\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}$'
  B: '$18\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}$'
  C: '$23\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}$'
  D: '$36\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is the wheel''s angular momentum before the torque acts, $I\omega_0$. The question asks for the change.'
  C: 'That is the wheel''s final angular momentum, $5.0 + 18$. The change is only the part the torque added.'
  D: 'That is $\tau\Delta t / I$, which is the change in angular speed (in rad/s), not the change in angular momentum.'
---

The angular impulse equals the change in angular momentum:

$$
\Delta L = \tau \Delta t = (3.0\ \text{N·m})(6.0\ \text{s}) = 18\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}
$$

The wheel's starting speed doesn't matter for the change. (It would matter for the final angular momentum: $I\omega_0 + \Delta L = 5.0 + 18 = 23\ \text{kg·m}^2/\text{s}$.)

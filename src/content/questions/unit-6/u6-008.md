stem: |
  The graph shows the angular momentum $L$ of a spinning wheel as a function of
  time $t$. What is the magnitude of the net torque on the wheel at
  $t = 3\ \text{s}$?
choices:
  A: '$1.2\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$'
  B: '$1.3\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$'
  C: '$2.0\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$'
  D: '$4.0\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This spreads the change in $L$ over the whole 5 s. But $L$ stays constant until 2 s, so the change happens in only 3 s.'
  B: 'This divides the value of $L$ at 3 s by the time, 3 s. Torque is the rate of change of $L$, the slope, not $L/t$.'
  D: 'This is the angular momentum at 3 s, read straight off the graph. The torque is the slope of the graph there.'
---

Net torque is the rate of change of angular momentum, which is the **slope** of the $L$–$t$ graph:

$$
\tau = \frac{\Delta L}{\Delta t}
$$

At $t = 3\ \text{s}$ the graph is on the straight piece from 2 s to 5 s, where $L$ rises from $2$ to $8\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m}^2/\text{s}$:

$$
\tau = \frac{8 - 2}{5 - 2} = \frac{6}{3} = 2.0\ \text{N}\cdot\text{m}
$$

Before 2 s, the graph is flat, so the net torque was zero then.

stem: |
  A comet moves in a long elliptical orbit around the Sun. As the comet moves
  from the far end of its orbit toward its closest point to the Sun, what
  happens to its rotational inertia about the Sun and to its angular speed about
  the Sun?
choices:
  A: 'Rotational inertia decreases, and angular speed increases.'
  B: 'Rotational inertia decreases, and angular speed decreases.'
  C: 'Rotational inertia stays the same, and angular speed increases.'
  D: 'Rotational inertia increases, and angular speed stays the same.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'Angular momentum $I\omega$ stays constant, so if $I$ drops, $\omega$ must rise to make up for it.'
  C: 'The comet''s mass is fixed, but its rotational inertia about the Sun is $mr^2$, which depends on its distance from the Sun.'
  D: 'The comet is getting closer to the Sun, so $r$ and its rotational inertia $mr^2$ get smaller, not larger.'
---

Treat the comet as a point mass at distance $r$ from the Sun, so its rotational inertia about the Sun is

$$
I = m r^2
$$

As the comet moves closer, $r$ shrinks, so $I$ **decreases**.

The Sun's gravity points straight at the Sun, so it exerts no torque about the Sun. The comet's angular momentum is conserved:

$$
I\omega = \text{constant}
$$

With $I$ getting smaller, the angular speed $\omega$ must **increase**. That is why comets whip quickly around the Sun at their closest approach and move slowly far away.

stem: |
  A merry-go-round spins counterclockwise, as seen from above, on a
  frictionless axle. A child runs along a straight line that just grazes the
  rim, moving opposite to the direction the rim is moving at that point, and
  jumps onto the edge. How does the angular momentum of the child–merry-go-round
  system just after the child lands compare with its angular momentum just
  before?
choices:
  A: 'It is the same, because no external torque acts on the system about the axle.'
  B: 'It is greater, because the child adds to the rotational inertia.'
  C: 'It is less, because the child''s angular momentum is opposite to the merry-go-round''s.'
  D: 'It is less, because the merry-go-round slows down when the child lands.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'More rotational inertia doesn''t mean more angular momentum. With no external torque, the total can''t change, so the rotation slows instead.'
  C: 'The child''s opposite angular momentum is already part of the total before the landing. It lowers the total, but it does so before and after equally.'
  D: 'The merry-go-round does slow down, but its lost angular momentum is exactly the child''s opposite angular momentum being cancelled. The system''s total stays the same.'
---

Choose the system to be the child plus the merry-go-round. During the landing, the child and the merry-go-round push on each other, but those forces are internal and their torques cancel. The axle is frictionless and its force acts through the axis, so no external torque acts about the axle.

With no external torque, the system's angular momentum is conserved:

$$
L_{\text{before}} = L_{\text{merry-go-round}} - L_{\text{child}} = L_{\text{after}}
$$

The child's angular momentum is in the opposite sense, so it counts as negative, both before and after. The merry-go-round does end up turning more slowly, but the system's total angular momentum doesn't change.

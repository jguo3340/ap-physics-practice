stem: |
  A puck on a level, frictionless table is tied to a string that passes down
  through a small hole in the center of the table. The puck moves in a circle of
  radius $2r$ around the hole with angular speed $\omega_0$. A student below the
  table slowly pulls the string until the puck moves in a circle of radius $r$.
  What is the puck's new angular speed?
choices:
  A: '$2\omega_0$'
  B: '$4\omega_0$'
  C: '$8\omega_0$'
  D: '$16\omega_0$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Doubling is right for the puck''s linear speed, $v$. The angular speed is $v/r$, and $r$ was also halved, so $\omega$ doubles again.'
  C: 'Eight times is the change in the string''s tension, not in the angular speed.'
  D: 'This squares the factor of 4. Angular momentum is $I\omega$ with $I = mr^2$, and the radius was halved, so $I$ drops by a factor of 4 and $\omega$ rises by 4.'
---

The string pulls the puck toward the hole, along the radius, so it exerts no torque about the hole. The puck's angular momentum about the hole is conserved:

$$
I_0 \omega_0 = I \omega \qquad\text{with}\qquad I = m r^2
$$

$$
m(2r)^2 \omega_0 = m r^2 \omega
$$

$$
\omega = 4\omega_0
$$

Halving the radius cuts the rotational inertia to one quarter, so the angular speed becomes four times larger.

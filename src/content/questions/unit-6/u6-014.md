stem: |
  A solid ball rolls without slipping up a ramp, slowing down as it goes. Which
  of the following correctly describes the forces exerted on the ball while it
  rolls up the ramp?
choices:
  A: 'Gravity at the center, a normal force at the contact point, and static friction at the contact point pointing down the ramp, opposite the motion'
  B: 'Gravity at the center, a normal force at the contact point, and a forward force along the ramp at the center'
  C: 'Gravity at the center and a normal force at the contact point only'
  D: 'Gravity at the center, a normal force at the contact point, and static friction at the contact point pointing up the ramp'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Friction opposes slipping, not motion. Without friction, gravity would slow the ball''s travel but not its spin, so the bottom of the ball would slide backward, down the ramp. Friction opposes that slip by pushing up the ramp.'
  B: 'Nothing pushes the ball forward. It keeps moving up the ramp because of its existing motion, not because of a force.'
  C: 'Gravity and the normal force both pass through the center, so they exert no torque about it. Without friction, nothing could slow the ball''s spin as it slows down.'
---

**Gravity** acts at the center of the ball, and the **normal force** acts at the contact point, perpendicular to the ramp. Neither exerts a torque about the center.

But the ball is slowing down, and to keep rolling without slipping its spin must slow down too. Something must exert a torque about the center, and the only candidate is **friction** at the contact point.

Gravity slows the ball's motion up the ramp. If there were no friction, the spin would stay the same while the center slowed. The bottom of a rolling ball moves backward relative to its center at speed $\omega r$, and that would now be faster than the center moves forward, so the bottom would slide backward, down the ramp. Static friction prevents that slip by pushing the contact point **up the ramp**. That force slows the spin, and it also slows the ball a little less than gravity alone would.

Friction points up the ramp whether the ball rolls up or down.

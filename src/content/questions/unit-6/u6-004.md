stem: |
  A comet, whose mass is much less than the Sun's, follows an elliptical orbit
  around the Sun. At the moment described, the comet is moving closer to the
  Sun. How are the gravitational potential energy of the comet–Sun system and
  the magnitude of the comet's angular momentum about the Sun changing?
choices:
  A: 'Potential energy increasing; angular momentum increasing'
  B: 'Potential energy decreasing; angular momentum increasing'
  C: 'Potential energy decreasing; angular momentum staying the same'
  D: 'Potential energy increasing; angular momentum staying the same'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Gravitational potential energy $-GMm/r$ becomes more negative, so it decreases, as the comet gets closer. And the angular momentum can''t change, because gravity exerts no torque about the Sun.'
  B: 'The comet speeds up as it gets closer, but its distance from the Sun shrinks at the same time, and $L = rmv\sin\theta$ stays constant.'
  D: 'Getting closer makes $U_G = -GMm/r$ more negative, so the potential energy decreases. It increases only when the comet moves away.'
---

**Potential energy:** $U_G = -\dfrac{GMm}{r}$. As $r$ shrinks, $U_G$ becomes more negative, so it decreases. (The lost potential energy becomes kinetic energy: the comet speeds up.)

**Angular momentum:** the Sun's gravitational pull on the comet always points straight at the Sun, so its torque about the Sun is zero ($\tau = rF\sin 0^\circ = 0$). With no torque, the comet's angular momentum about the Sun stays the same. Its speed goes up, but its distance goes down in a way that keeps $L$ constant.

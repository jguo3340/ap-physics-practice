stem: |
  A puck moves at a constant speed in a circle of radius $2r$ on a level,
  frictionless table, held by a string that passes through a small hole at the
  circle's center. The string's tension is $T_0$. The string is slowly pulled
  through the hole until the puck circles at radius $r$. What is the tension in
  the string now?
choices:
  A: '$2T_0$'
  B: '$4T_0$'
  C: '$8T_0$'
  D: '$16T_0$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Doubling matches the change in speed, but tension depends on speed squared, $mv^2/r$. And the radius also shrank, which raises the force further.'
  B: 'This squares the doubled speed but forgets that the radius was halved. Dividing by the smaller radius doubles the force again.'
  D: 'This is how much $\omega^2$ grows, since $\omega$ rises by a factor of 4. But $T = m\omega^2 r$, and $r$ was halved, so the tension grows by 8.'
---

**Speed:** the string exerts no torque about the hole, so angular momentum $mvr$ is conserved:

$$
m v_0 (2r) = m v r \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = 2v_0
$$

**Tension:** the string provides the centripetal force:

$$
T_0 = \frac{m v_0^2}{2r} \qquad T = \frac{m (2v_0)^2}{r} = \frac{4 m v_0^2}{r}
$$

$$
\frac{T}{T_0} = \frac{4/r}{1/(2r)} = 8
$$

The tension grows quickly as the puck is pulled in, which is why it gets harder and harder to pull the string.

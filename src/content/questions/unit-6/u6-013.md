stem: |
  Block A, of mass $3m$, sits on a level table and is connected by a light
  string to block B, of mass $m$, which hangs over the table's edge. The string
  runs over a pulley that has rotational inertia $I$ and turns without the string
  slipping. At one instant both blocks move with speed $v$. A student writes the
  kinetic energy of the system of both blocks and the pulley as
  $K = \tfrac12 (3m)v^2 + \tfrac12 m v^2$. Can this equation be used?
choices:
  A: 'Yes, because the pulley does not move from place to place, so it has no kinetic energy.'
  B: 'Yes, because the string has negligible mass.'
  C: 'No, because the system''s kinetic energy is less than the equation gives, since the pulley takes energy from the blocks.'
  D: 'No, because the system''s kinetic energy is greater than the equation gives.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The pulley stays in one place, but it spins. A spinning object has rotational kinetic energy, $\tfrac12 I\omega^2$.'
  B: 'The light string adds no kinetic energy of its own, but the pulley is part of the system and it is spinning.'
  C: 'The pulley does take some energy from the blocks, but that energy is still in the system, as the pulley''s rotational kinetic energy. The system has more kinetic energy than the blocks alone.'
---

The system includes the pulley, and the pulley is spinning. Its rotational kinetic energy is

$$
K_{\text{pulley}} = \tfrac12 I \omega^2 = \tfrac12 I \left(\frac{v}{R}\right)^2
$$

where $R$ is the pulley's radius (the string does not slip, so the rim moves at the string's speed $v$).

The full kinetic energy of the system is

$$
K = \tfrac12 (3m) v^2 + \tfrac12 m v^2 + \tfrac12 I \omega^2
$$

The student's equation leaves out the last term, which is positive, so the real kinetic energy is greater than the equation gives.

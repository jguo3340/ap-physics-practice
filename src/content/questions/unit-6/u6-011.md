stem: |
  A puck slides in a circle on a level, frictionless table, held by a string
  that runs through a small hole at the center of the circle. Someone below the
  table slowly pulls the string, shrinking the circle's radius from $2r$ to $r$.
  Which quantity for the puck stays the same?
choices:
  A: 'Its kinetic energy'
  B: 'Its linear speed'
  C: 'The tension in the string'
  D: 'Its angular momentum about the hole'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The person below the table pulls the string through a distance, so they do work on the puck. Its kinetic energy grows by a factor of 4.'
  B: 'With angular momentum $mvr$ conserved, halving $r$ doubles $v$.'
  C: 'The puck moves faster on a smaller circle, so it needs a much larger centripetal force. The tension grows by a factor of 8.'
---

The string's pull points straight at the hole, so it exerts no torque about the hole. That makes the puck's **angular momentum** about the hole constant.

Everything else changes as the radius halves:

- Linear speed: $mvr$ stays constant, so $v$ doubles.
- Kinetic energy: $\tfrac12 mv^2$ grows by $2^2 = 4$. The energy comes from the work done by the person pulling the string.
- Tension: $mv^2/r$ grows by $\dfrac{2^2}{1/2} = 8$.

stem: |
  Three identical uniform rods, X, Y, and Z, each of length $L$, can rotate on a
  level, frictionless surface about vertical axles. Rod X's axle is at one end,
  rod Y's axle is at its center, and rod Z's axle is $\tfrac13 L$ from one end.
  All three rods rotate with the same angular speed. Which of the following
  correctly ranks their angular momenta $L$ about their axles?
choices:
  A: '$L_X > L_Z > L_Y$'
  B: '$L_X = L_Y = L_Z$'
  C: '$L_Y > L_Z > L_X$'
  D: '$L_X > L_Y > L_Z$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The rods are identical, but rotational inertia also depends on where the axle is. Mass farther from the axle adds more rotational inertia.'
  C: 'This has the ranking backward. Rotating about the center keeps the mass closest to the axle, which gives the smallest rotational inertia.'
  D: 'An axle at $\tfrac13 L$ puts more mass farther away than an axle at the center, so rod Z beats rod Y.'
---

With the same angular speed, angular momentum $L = I\omega$ ranks the same as rotational inertia.

- Rod Y, axle at the center: $I_Y = \tfrac{1}{12} ML^2$.
- Rod X, axle at the end: $I_X = \tfrac13 ML^2$.
- Rod Z, axle $\tfrac13 L$ from the end, which is $\tfrac16 L$ from the center. Use the parallel-axis theorem:

$$
I_Z = \tfrac{1}{12} ML^2 + M\left(\tfrac16 L\right)^2
$$

$$
I_Z = \tfrac{3}{36} ML^2 + \tfrac{1}{36} ML^2 = \tfrac19 ML^2
$$

Compare: $\tfrac13 > \tfrac19 > \tfrac{1}{12}$, so $L_X > L_Z > L_Y$.

stem: |
  A bicycle wheel of known rotational inertia starts at rest on a frictionless
  axle. A string is wrapped around its rim, so pulling the string with a known
  force exerts a known torque on the wheel. Which procedure would best test the
  relationship between the torque applied and the resulting change in the
  wheel's angular momentum?
choices:
  A: 'Pull with one force for several different times and measure the angle the wheel turns through each time.'
  B: 'Pull with five different forces, each for 3 s, and measure the wheel''s final angular speed each time.'
  C: 'Pull with a small force for 2 s and then a larger force for 3 s, and measure the final angular speed once.'
  D: 'Pull with one force for five different times and measure the wheel''s final angular speed each time.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'The angle turned through is not angular momentum. Angular momentum depends on the angular speed, $L = I\omega$.'
  C: 'Mixing two torques in one run gives a single measurement, which can''t show how the change in angular momentum depends on the torque.'
  D: 'That tests how the change in angular momentum depends on time, with the torque held fixed. The question is about the torque, so the torque is what needs to vary.'
---

The relationship to test is $\Delta L = \tau \Delta t$. To see how $\Delta L$ depends on the torque, vary the torque, keep the time the same, and measure $\Delta L$ each time.

Since the wheel starts at rest, $\Delta L = I\omega_f$, and $I$ is known, so measuring the final angular speed gives $\Delta L$. Five different forces give five torques, each applied for the same 3 s. Plotting $I\omega_f$ against $\tau$ should give a straight line through the origin with slope $\Delta t = 3$ s.

stem: |
  A comet travels in a long elliptical orbit around the Sun. Which of the
  following describes the comet's kinetic energy and the total mechanical
  energy of the comet–Sun system over one complete orbit?
choices:
  A: 'The comet''s kinetic energy changes; the system''s total energy stays constant and is positive.'
  B: 'The comet''s kinetic energy stays constant; the system''s total energy stays constant.'
  C: 'The comet''s kinetic energy changes; the system''s total energy changes along with it.'
  D: 'The comet''s kinetic energy changes; the system''s total energy stays constant and is negative.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'A positive total would mean the comet has enough energy to escape and never come back. A comet in a closed orbit is bound, so the total is negative.'
  B: 'In an elliptical orbit the distance to the Sun changes, so the gravitational potential energy changes and the kinetic energy must change to make up for it. The comet moves fastest when it is closest to the Sun.'
  C: 'Only gravity, an internal force, acts within the comet–Sun system, so its total mechanical energy is conserved even though the comet speeds up and slows down.'
---

**The comet's kinetic energy changes.** As the comet falls in toward the Sun, gravity does positive work on it and it speeds up; as it climbs back out, it slows down. It moves fastest at its closest point.

**The system's total energy is constant.** The only force is the gravitational attraction between the comet and the Sun, which is internal to the comet–Sun system. Kinetic and potential energy trade back and forth, but their sum doesn't change.

**The total is negative.** With $U_G = -GMm/r$, the potential energy is negative everywhere. For a bound orbit, the comet never has enough kinetic energy to make the total reach zero, which is what it would take to escape.

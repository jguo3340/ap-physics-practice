stem: |
  A solid ball and a block are released from rest at the same height on two
  identical ramps. The ball rolls down its ramp without slipping. The block
  slides down a frictionless ramp. Which reaches the bottom with the greater
  speed?
choices:
  A: 'The ball, because rolling helps it move along the ramp'
  B: 'The block'
  C: 'They have the same speed, because they start at the same height.'
  D: 'It depends on the masses of the ball and the block.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Rolling does not add energy. Part of the ball''s energy goes into spinning, which leaves less for moving down the ramp.'
  C: 'Both lose the same potential energy per kilogram, but the ball has to share its energy between moving and spinning. The block puts all of it into moving.'
  D: 'Mass cancels for both objects, since both the potential energy and the kinetic energy are proportional to mass. The ball''s shape decides the answer, not its mass.'
---

Each object starts with potential energy $mgh$, and no energy is lost along the way (the ball rolls without slipping, so static friction does no work).

**Block:** all of the energy becomes translational kinetic energy.

$$
mgh = \tfrac12 m v^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = \sqrt{2gh}
$$

**Ball:** the energy is split between moving and spinning. For a solid ball, $I = \tfrac25 mr^2$ and $\omega = v/r$:

$$
mgh = \tfrac12 m v^2 + \tfrac12 \left(\tfrac25 m r^2\right)\left(\frac{v}{r}\right)^2 = \tfrac{7}{10} m v^2
$$

$$
v = \sqrt{\tfrac{10}{7} gh} \approx \sqrt{1.43\, gh}
$$

That is smaller than $\sqrt{2gh}$, so the block is faster at the bottom.

stem: |
  The graph shows the potential energy $U$ of a system as one object in the
  system moves along the $x$-axis while the rest of the system stays put. Six
  evenly spaced points, P through V, are labeled. At point P the object is
  moving in the $+x$ direction, and the system's mechanical energy is 4.0 J.
  No energy enters or leaves the system. In which segment is the magnitude of
  the net force on the object greatest?
choices:
  A: 'PQ'
  B: 'QR'
  C: 'RS'
  D: 'ST'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The force depends on how steeply $U$ changes. From P to Q, $U$ rises by 2 J per segment, while from R to S it drops by 3 J per segment.'
  B: 'The potential energy is highest here, but it is flat. A flat $U$ graph means zero force, no matter how high it is.'
  D: 'The object moves fastest here, but $U$ is flat, so the net force is zero. Speed and force are different things.'
---

For a conservative force, the force is the negative **slope** of the $U$–$x$ graph:

$$
F = -\frac{\Delta U}{\Delta x}
$$

So the steepest segment has the largest force. Read the change in $U$ for each one-segment step:

- PQ: from 1 J to 3 J, a change of 2 J.
- QR and ST: flat, so zero force.
- RS: from 3 J to 0 J, a change of 3 J, the steepest.
- TV: from 0 J to 2 J, a change of 2 J.

The force is greatest in segment RS. Its size is the steepness of the graph, not the height of $U$ or the object's speed.

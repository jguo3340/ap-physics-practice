stem: |
  A 200 g box is released from rest at the top of a rough ramp shaped like a
  quarter of a circle with radius 0.40 m. At the bottom, the ramp joins
  smoothly onto a level, frictionless floor. Half of the box–Earth system's
  initial mechanical energy is dissipated while the box slides down the ramp.
  What is the box's speed as it slides across the floor?
choices:
  A: '1.4 m/s'
  B: '2.0 m/s'
  C: '2.8 m/s'
  D: '4.0 m/s'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This halves the no-friction speed of 2.8 m/s. Losing half the energy halves $v^2$, not $v$.'
  C: 'This is the speed with no friction at all. Half of the energy was dissipated on the ramp.'
  D: 'This forgets the square root. The kinetic energy gives $v^2 = 4\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2$, so $v = 2.0\ \text{m/s}$.'
---

The top of a quarter-circle ramp is one radius above the bottom, so $h = 0.40\ \text{m}$. Take $U = 0$ at the floor.

**Initial mechanical energy:**

$$
U_0 = mgh = (0.200)(10)(0.40) = 0.80\ \text{J}
$$

**After the ramp:** half is dissipated, so the box keeps $0.40\ \text{J}$, all as kinetic energy on the level floor.

$$
\tfrac12 (0.200) v^2 = 0.40 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v^2 = 4.0\ \text{m}^2/\text{s}^2
$$

$$
v = 2.0\ \text{m/s}
$$

The floor is frictionless, so the box keeps that speed.

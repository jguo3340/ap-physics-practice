stem: |
  An object starts at rest on a frictionless surface. A horizontal net force
  then acts on it while it moves from $x = 0$ to $x = 4$ m. The four graphs
  show four different ways the force could vary with position. For which graph
  is the object's final speed the greatest?
choices:
  A: 'Graph 1'
  B: 'Graph 2'
  C: 'Graph 3'
  D: 'Graph 4'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The area under graph 1 is $(4\ \text{N})(4\ \text{m}) = 16$ J, less than the 20 J under graph 4.'
  B: 'Graph 2 has the largest force at the start, but it only acts for 1 m. Its area is 12 J.'
  C: 'The area under graph 3 is $(6\ \text{N})(3\ \text{m}) = 18$ J, close but less than the 20 J under graph 4.'
---

The net work done on the object equals its change in kinetic energy. It starts at rest, so the graph with the **largest area** under it gives the most kinetic energy and the greatest speed.

| Graph | Area (net work) |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | $(4)(4) = 16$ J |
| 2 | $(12)(1) = 12$ J |
| 3 | $(6)(3) = 18$ J |
| 4 | $\tfrac12 (4)(10) = 20$ J |

Graph 4 wins. The trap is graph 2: it has the biggest force, but a big force over a short distance does less work than a moderate force over a long one.

stem: |
  Three small objects lie on a straight line far from any other masses. An
  object of mass $m$ is at $x = 0$, a second object of mass $m$ is at
  $x = r$, and an object of mass $2m$ is at $x = 2r$. What is the total
  gravitational potential energy of the three-object system?
choices:
  A: '$-\dfrac{3Gm^2}{r}$'
  B: '$-\dfrac{4Gm^2}{r}$'
  C: '$-\dfrac{5Gm^2}{r}$'
  D: '$-\dfrac{8Gm^2}{r}$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This counts only the two neighboring pairs. The objects at $x = 0$ and $x = 2r$ also attract each other, and that pair adds energy too.'
  C: 'This uses a separation of $r$ for the outer pair. The objects at $x = 0$ and $x = 2r$ are $2r$ apart.'
  D: 'This counts each pair twice. Every pair of objects contributes its potential energy once.'
---

The gravitational potential energy of a system is the sum over **every pair** of objects, each pair counted once, using $U_G = -\dfrac{G m_1 m_2}{r}$.

There are three pairs:

$$
\underbrace{-\frac{G(m)(m)}{r}}_{0 \text{ and } r} \; \underbrace{-\,\frac{G(m)(2m)}{r}}_{r \text{ and } 2r} \; \underbrace{-\,\frac{G(m)(2m)}{2r}}_{0 \text{ and } 2r}
$$

That is $-\dfrac{Gm^2}{r}\,(1 + 2 + 1) = -\dfrac{4Gm^2}{r}$.

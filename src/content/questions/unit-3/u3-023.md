stem: |
  A student drags a box across a level floor along three straight paths: 4 m
  east, then 3 m north, then 5 m straight back to the starting point. The
  kinetic frictional force on the box is 10 N the whole time. What is the total
  work done on the box by friction?
choices:
  A: '$0$'
  B: '$-50\ \text{J}$'
  C: '$-70\ \text{J}$'
  D: '$-120\ \text{J}$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Ending at the starting point does not cancel friction''s work. Friction always points against the motion, so it does negative work on every part of the trip.'
  B: 'This uses only the last 5 m. Friction acts on all three legs of the trip.'
  C: 'This leaves out the trip back. Friction keeps opposing the motion on the 5 m return leg too.'
---

Kinetic friction always points opposite the direction the box is moving, so on every leg it does negative work equal to the friction force times the distance:

$$
W_f = -f \times (\text{total path length})
$$

$$
W_f = -(10\ \text{N})(4 + 3 + 5\ \text{m}) = -120\ \text{J}
$$

Friction's work depends on the path length, not on the displacement. That is why friction is not a conservative force and has no potential energy.

stem: |
  A simple pendulum is pulled aside until its string makes a $60^\circ$ angle
  with the vertical, then released from rest. Take the gravitational potential
  energy of the bob–Earth system to be zero at the bob's lowest point. Air
  resistance is negligible. At what angle from the vertical are the bob's
  kinetic energy and the system's potential energy equal?
choices:
  A: 'Less than $30^\circ$'
  B: 'Equal to $30^\circ$'
  C: 'Greater than $30^\circ$'
  D: 'It depends on the mass of the bob.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'At $30^\circ$ the bob is already below the halfway height, so the angle where $K = U$ is larger than $30^\circ$, not smaller.'
  B: 'Halfway in angle is not halfway in height. The bob drops slowly near the top of the swing and quickly near the bottom.'
  D: 'Both energies are proportional to the mass, so the mass cancels when you set them equal.'
---

The bob's height above its lowest point, when the string is at angle $\theta$ from the vertical, is

$$
h = L(1 - \cos\theta)
$$

**Start:** $h_0 = L(1 - \cos 60^\circ) = 0.50L$, and all the energy is potential: $E = 0.50\,mgL$.

**Where $K = U$:** each must be half of $E$, so $U = 0.25\,mgL$ and $h = 0.25L$:

$$
1 - \cos\theta = 0.25 \quad\Rightarrow\quad \cos\theta = 0.75
$$

$$
\theta \approx 41^\circ
$$

**Compare with $30^\circ$:** at $30^\circ$, $h = L(1 - 0.87) = 0.13L$, which is below the halfway height. The height does not change in proportion to the angle, so equal energies happen at a larger angle than $30^\circ$.

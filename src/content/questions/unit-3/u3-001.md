stem: |
  Which of the following objects has a kinetic energy that is increasing?
choices:
  A: 'A sled sliding down a frictionless, icy hill'
  B: 'A satellite moving in a circular orbit at a constant speed'
  C: 'An elevator rising at a constant 2 m/s'
  D: 'A hockey puck gliding in a straight line across frictionless ice'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The satellite''s direction keeps changing, but its speed does not. Kinetic energy depends only on speed, so it stays the same.'
  C: 'The elevator''s gravitational potential energy is increasing, but its speed is constant, so its kinetic energy is not changing.'
  D: 'Nothing speeds the puck up or slows it down, so its speed and its kinetic energy stay the same.'
---

Kinetic energy is $K = \tfrac12 mv^2$. It depends on mass and **speed** only, not on direction, so the question is really asking which object is speeding up.

The sled on the frictionless hill is the only one speeding up. Gravity has a component along the slope, and nothing opposes it, so the sled gains speed as it goes down. Gravitational potential energy turns into kinetic energy.

In the other three cases the speed is constant, so the kinetic energy is constant, even for the satellite, whose velocity changes direction all the time.

stem: |
  Two identical 2.0 kg pucks slide to the right at 3.0 m/s on a frictionless,
  level surface, one behind the other. The front puck reaches a rough patch,
  where the coefficient of kinetic friction is 0.15, and comes to rest after
  sliding 3.0 m. At that moment the other puck has not yet reached the rough
  patch. What is the change in the mechanical energy of the two-puck system
  from the start to that moment?
choices:
  A: '$0$'
  B: '$-4.5\ \text{J}$'
  C: '$-9.0\ \text{J}$'
  D: '$-18\ \text{J}$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Friction does negative work on the front puck and turns its kinetic energy into thermal energy. That energy is no longer mechanical.'
  B: 'This is half of the front puck''s kinetic energy. The front puck stopped completely, so it lost all of it.'
  D: 'This counts the kinetic energy of both pucks. The rear puck is still sliding at 3.0 m/s, so it has lost nothing.'
---

The pucks stay on a level surface, so there is no change in potential energy. Only kinetic energy changes.

- **Rear puck:** still at 3.0 m/s, so its kinetic energy has not changed.
- **Front puck:** went from 3.0 m/s to rest.

$$
\Delta K = 0 - \tfrac12 (2.0)(3.0)^2 = -9.0\ \text{J}
$$

You can check it with the work done by friction:

$$
W_f = -\mu_k m g d
$$

$$
W_f = -(0.15)(2.0)(10)(3.0) = -9.0\ \text{J}
$$

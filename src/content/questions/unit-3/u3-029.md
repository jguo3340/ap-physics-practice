stem: |
  A ball falls toward the floor and bounces back up. At time $t_1$, before the
  bounce, the ball is a height $h_1$ above the floor and moving down with speed
  $v_1$. At a later time $t_2$, after the bounce, it is a height $h_2$ above the
  floor and moving up with speed $v_2$. Nothing adds energy to the ball. Which of
  the following could be true?
choices:
  A: '$h_2 > h_1$ and $v_2 > v_1$'
  B: '$h_2 = h_1$ and $v_2 > v_1$'
  C: '$h_2 > h_1$ and $v_2 = v_1$'
  D: '$h_2 < h_1$ and $v_2 = v_1$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Both the potential energy and the kinetic energy would be larger than before. The total mechanical energy cannot grow when nothing adds energy.'
  B: 'Same height means the same potential energy, so a larger speed would mean more total energy than before the bounce.'
  C: 'Same speed means the same kinetic energy, so a greater height would mean more total energy than before the bounce.'
---

Nothing adds energy, so the ball–Earth system's mechanical energy after the bounce can be no larger than before (and is usually smaller, since some energy turns into heat and sound):

$$
mgh_2 + \tfrac12 m v_2^2 \le mgh_1 + \tfrac12 m v_1^2
$$

Check each choice:

- Higher and faster: both terms grow, so the total grows. Impossible.
- Same height, faster: the kinetic term grows. Impossible.
- Higher, same speed: the potential term grows. Impossible.
- Lower, same speed: the potential term shrinks, so the total goes down. This is possible. If the bounce loses a little energy, the ball gets back up to speed $v_1$ only at a point below $h_1$.

stem: |
  A cyclist and bike have a combined mass of 80 kg. The cyclist brakes hard
  from several starting speeds and records the distance needed to stop, as
  shown in the table.

  $\begin{array}{c|c} \text{Speed (m/s)} & \text{Stopping distance (m)} \\ \hline 4 & 2.0 \\ 8 & 8.1 \\ 12 & 17.9 \end{array}$

  Assuming the braking force is about the same each time, its size is closest
  to which of the following?
choices:
  A: '80 N'
  B: '160 N'
  C: '320 N'
  D: '640 N'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This divides $\tfrac12 m v$ by the distance. Kinetic energy depends on $v^2$, not $v$.'
  B: 'This divides the momentum $mv$ by the distance. Work and distance connect to kinetic energy, not momentum.'
  D: 'This uses $mv^2$ and leaves out the $\tfrac12$ in the kinetic energy.'
---

The braking force does negative work that removes all of the kinetic energy:

$$
F d = \tfrac12 m v^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad F = \frac{m v^2}{2d}
$$

Try the first row:

$$
F = \frac{(80)(4)^2}{2(2.0)} = 320\ \text{N}
$$

The other rows agree: about 316 N for 8 m/s and about 322 N for 12 m/s.

Notice the pattern in the table: doubling the speed makes the stopping distance about four times as long, which is exactly what $d \propto v^2$ predicts for a constant force.

stem: |
  A worker uses a rope parallel to a rough ramp to lower a crate down the ramp
  at a constant speed. Let $W_g$ be the work done on the crate by gravity and
  $W_r$ the work done on the crate by the rope. Which of the following is
  correct?
choices:
  A: '$|W_r| = W_g$, because the crate moves at a constant speed.'
  B: '$|W_r| < W_g$, and $W_r$ is negative.'
  C: '$|W_r| > W_g$, because the rope must also overcome friction.'
  D: '$W_r$ is positive, because the rope and the crate move together.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Constant speed means the total work is zero, but three forces do work here, not two. Friction also takes energy away, so the rope does not have to take all of it.'
  C: 'The rope would have to overcome friction if it were pulling the crate up the ramp. Going down, friction helps the rope hold the crate back.'
  D: 'The rope pulls up the ramp while the crate moves down the ramp. A force opposite the motion does negative work.'
---

Three forces do work on the crate as it moves down the ramp (the normal force is perpendicular to the motion, so it does none):

- **Gravity** has a component down the ramp, along the motion: $W_g > 0$.
- **The rope** pulls up the ramp, against the motion: $W_r < 0$.
- **Friction** acts up the ramp, against the motion: $W_f < 0$.

The speed is constant, so the kinetic energy does not change and the total work is zero:

$$
W_g + W_r + W_f = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad |W_r| = W_g - |W_f|
$$

Friction is doing part of the job of holding the crate back, so the rope does less negative work than gravity does positive work.

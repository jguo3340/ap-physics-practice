stem: |
  A worker lifts a crate from the floor onto a shelf at a constant speed.
  Consider the system made of the crate and Earth. Which of the following
  correctly describes the mechanical energy of the system and the work the
  worker does on it?
choices:
  A: 'The mechanical energy stays the same, and the worker does zero work on the system.'
  B: 'The mechanical energy stays the same, and the worker does positive work on the system.'
  C: 'The mechanical energy increases, and the worker does zero work on the system.'
  D: 'The mechanical energy increases, and the worker does positive work on the system.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The crate''s speed is constant, but it ends up higher, so the gravitational potential energy of the system increases.'
  B: 'If the worker does positive work on the system, the system''s energy must go up. Here it goes up as gravitational potential energy.'
  C: 'The net work on the crate alone is zero, because gravity cancels the lift. But gravity is inside this system; the worker is the only outside force, and the worker does positive work.'
---

Pick the right system first. The crate and Earth are inside, so the gravitational force between them is internal and shows up as potential energy, not as work. The worker is outside the system.

- **Mechanical energy:** the speed is constant, so $K$ does not change. The crate ends up higher, so $U_g = mgh$ increases. Mechanical energy increases.
- **Work by the worker:** the worker pushes up, and the crate moves up, so the worker does positive work. That work is exactly the energy that shows up as the increase in $U_g$.

The trap is mixing systems. For the crate alone, the net work is zero (the lift and gravity cancel), which matches its constant kinetic energy. For the crate–Earth system, the worker's positive work raises the mechanical energy.

stem: |
  An object moves in the positive $x$ direction. The graph shows the net force
  on it as a function of its position. How does the object's kinetic energy
  change between $x = 0$ and $x = 4.0$ m?
choices:
  A: 'It decreases by 3 J.'
  B: 'It decreases by 15 J.'
  C: 'It increases by 3 J.'
  D: 'It increases by 27 J.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'That is only the negative work from 1.5 m to 4.0 m. The force did $+12$ J of work first.'
  C: 'The sign is backward. The negative area (15 J) is bigger than the positive area (12 J).'
  D: 'This adds the two areas as if both were positive. Below the axis the force points opposite the motion, so that work is negative.'
---

The change in kinetic energy equals the net work, which is the area between the graph and the axis, counting area below the axis as negative.

$$
W_{0 \to 1.5} = (+8\ \text{N})(1.5\ \text{m}) = +12\ \text{J}
$$

$$
W_{1.5 \to 4.0} = (-6\ \text{N})(2.5\ \text{m}) = -15\ \text{J}
$$

The total is $12 - 15 = -3$ J, so the kinetic energy decreases by 3 J. The object still has some kinetic energy left, as long as it started with more than 3 J.

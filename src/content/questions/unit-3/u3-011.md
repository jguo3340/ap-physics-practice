stem: |
  A 0.40 kg ball is dropped from rest 2.5 m above the floor. During its bounce,
  the ball–Earth system loses 3.0 J of mechanical energy. Air resistance is
  negligible. How high does the ball rise after the bounce?
choices:
  A: '0.75 m'
  B: '1.75 m'
  C: '2.50 m'
  D: '3.25 m'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is how much height the ball loses, not how high it rises.'
  C: 'That would mean no energy was lost. The bounce takes 3.0 J away, so the ball cannot return to where it started.'
  D: 'This adds the 3.0 J to the system instead of taking it away.'
---

Measure height from the floor. At the start, all the mechanical energy is gravitational potential energy:

$$
E_i = mgh = (0.40)(10)(2.5) = 10\ \text{J}
$$

After the bounce the system has $10 - 3.0 = 7.0$ J left. At the top of the bounce the ball is momentarily at rest, so all 7.0 J is potential energy again:

$$
h = \frac{7.0\ \text{J}}{(0.40)(10)} = 1.75\ \text{m}
$$

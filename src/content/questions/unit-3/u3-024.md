stem: |
  A crane lifts a crate at a constant speed through a height $h$ in time $t$,
  doing work at an average rate $P_1$. Later the crane lifts the same crate at
  a constant speed through a height $3h$ in time $2t$, doing work at a rate
  $P_2$. What is $P_2$?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac23 P_1$'
  B: '$\tfrac32 P_1$'
  C: '$3 P_1$'
  D: '$6 P_1$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This has the ratio upside down. Power is work divided by time, so the larger height goes on top.'
  C: 'This accounts for the extra work but not the extra time. The second lift takes twice as long.'
  D: 'This multiplies by the time instead of dividing by it.'
---

At constant speed, the crane's force equals the crate's weight, so the work done is $mgh$. Power is work divided by time.

$$
P_1 = \frac{mgh}{t}
$$

$$
P_2 = \frac{mg(3h)}{2t} = \frac32 \cdot \frac{mgh}{t} = \frac32 P_1
$$

Three times the work spread over twice the time is 1.5 times the power.

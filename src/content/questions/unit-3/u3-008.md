stem: |
  A constant net force acts on a 2.0 kg cart on a level track. During the
  first 2.0 s, the cart's speed increases from 1.0 m/s to 4.0 m/s. If the same
  net force keeps acting, by how much does the cart's kinetic energy increase
  during the next 2.0 s?
choices:
  A: '9 J'
  B: '15 J'
  C: '33 J'
  D: '49 J'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This squares the change in speed, $\tfrac12 m(\Delta v)^2$. The change in kinetic energy is $\tfrac12 m(v_f^2 - v_i^2)$, which is not the same thing.'
  B: 'That is the gain during the first 2.0 s. The force is the same, but the cart covers more distance in the second interval because it is moving faster, so the force does more work.'
  D: 'That is the cart''s total kinetic energy at the end, not the increase during the last 2.0 s.'
---

A constant force gives a constant acceleration:

$$
a = \frac{4.0 - 1.0}{2.0} = 1.5\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

After two more seconds the speed is $4.0 + (1.5)(2.0) = 7.0$ m/s. The increase in kinetic energy over the second interval is

$$
\Delta K = \tfrac12 (2.0)(7.0^2 - 4.0^2) = 49 - 16 = 33\ \text{J}
$$

Compare that with the first interval, $\tfrac12(2.0)(4.0^2 - 1.0^2) = 15$ J. Equal times do **not** give equal energy gains. The work is $Fd$, and the cart moves farther in the second interval (11 m versus 5 m), so the same force does more work.

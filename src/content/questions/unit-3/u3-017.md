stem: |
  A 0.50 kg block is attached to the bottom of a vertical spring with spring
  constant 100 N/m. The block is held so the spring is at its natural length,
  then released from rest. It bounces up and down, and air resistance slowly
  brings it to rest 0.050 m below where it was released. What is the change in
  the total mechanical energy of the block–spring–Earth system?
choices:
  A: '$-0.250$ J'
  B: '$-0.125$ J'
  C: '$0$ J'
  D: '$+0.125$ J'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is only the change in gravitational potential energy. The spring also stored energy as it stretched.'
  C: 'Mechanical energy would be conserved without air resistance. Air resistance is what made the bouncing die out, and it took energy away.'
  D: 'That is only the energy stored in the spring. The gravitational potential energy also decreased.'
---

The block starts and ends at rest, so the kinetic energy does not change. Add up the changes in the two kinds of potential energy, with $x = 0.050$ m:

$$
\Delta U_g = -(0.50)(10)(0.050) = -0.250\ \text{J}
$$

$$
\Delta U_s = \tfrac12 kx^2 = \tfrac12 (100)(0.050)^2 = +0.125\ \text{J}
$$

So $\Delta E = 0 - 0.250 + 0.125 = -0.125$ J. The missing 0.125 J went to the air as thermal energy while the block bounced.

Check that $0.050$ m is the resting point: there $kx = (100)(0.050) = 5.0$ N, which balances the block's weight, $(0.50)(10) = 5.0$ N.

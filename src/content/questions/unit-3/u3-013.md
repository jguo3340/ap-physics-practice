stem: |
  Two identical crates are moved from the ground onto a loading dock 1.2 m
  high. Crate 1 is lifted straight up by a hoist at a constant speed. Crate 2
  is pushed up a frictionless ramp at a constant speed. How does the work done
  on crate 1 by the hoist compare with the work done on crate 2 by the person
  pushing it?
choices:
  A: 'The two amounts of work are equal.'
  B: 'More work is done on crate 1, because the hoist must exert a larger force.'
  C: 'More work is done on crate 2, because it moves a longer distance.'
  D: 'Less work is done on crate 2, because the ramp supports part of its weight.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The hoist does push harder, but over a shorter distance. Force times distance comes out the same.'
  C: 'Crate 2 does move farther, but the push needed along a frictionless ramp is smaller. Force times distance comes out the same.'
  D: 'The ramp''s normal force is perpendicular to the motion, so it does no work. It lowers the force needed, not the work.'
---

Both crates start and end at rest, at the same height, so the work done on each by the hoist or the person equals the same increase in gravitational potential energy:

$$
W = mg\Delta y = mg(1.2\ \text{m})
$$

You can also see it with force and distance. On a frictionless ramp at angle $\theta$, the push is only $mg\sin\theta$, but the crate moves $\dfrac{1.2}{\sin\theta}$ along the ramp. The product is still $mg(1.2)$. A ramp trades a smaller force for a longer distance; it does not save work.

stem: |
  A sled of mass $M$ starts at rest on level, frictionless ice. A child pulls it
  with a rope that makes an angle $\theta$ above the horizontal, using a
  constant force of magnitude $F$. After the sled has moved a distance $d$, its
  speed is $v$. Which expression is equal to the work done on the sled by the
  rope?
choices:
  A: '$Fd$'
  B: '$Fd \sin\theta$'
  C: '$Mgd$'
  D: '$\tfrac12 M v^2$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'This would be true only if the rope pulled straight along the ice. Only the horizontal part of the pull, $F \cos\theta$, does work here.'
  B: 'The sine gives the vertical part of the pull, which is perpendicular to the motion and does no work.'
  C: 'The sled stays on level ice, so it never moves in the direction of gravity. This expression has nothing to do with the rope.'
---

The rope's work is $W = Fd\cos\theta$, but that is not one of the choices. Use the work–energy theorem instead.

On frictionless, level ice, the forces on the sled are gravity, the normal force, and the rope's pull. Gravity and the normal force are vertical, perpendicular to the motion, so they do no work. That makes the rope's work the **net** work:

$$
W_{\text{rope}} = W_{\text{net}} = \Delta K = \tfrac12 M v^2 - 0
$$

So $Fd\cos\theta = \tfrac12 Mv^2$, and both describe the rope's work.

stem: |
  A 5.0 kg box slides up a rough ramp. At the bottom of the ramp its speed is
  8.0 m/s. At a point 1.5 m higher (measured vertically), its speed is
  2.0 m/s. How much work does friction do on the box between those two points?
choices:
  A: '$-225$ J'
  B: '$-150$ J'
  C: '$-75$ J'
  D: '$0$ J'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This subtracts the gain in gravitational potential energy instead of adding it. Part of the lost kinetic energy went into height, not into friction.'
  B: 'That is the whole loss of kinetic energy. Only some of it went to friction; the rest became gravitational potential energy.'
  D: 'If friction did no work, the box would keep all the kinetic energy it did not use to rise. It lost more than the rise accounts for, so friction did negative work.'
---

Use the box–Earth system. Friction is the only force from outside the system that does work, so it accounts for the change in mechanical energy:

$$
W_f = \Delta K + \Delta U_g
$$

The change in kinetic energy is

$$
\Delta K = \tfrac12 (5.0)(2.0^2 - 8.0^2) = -150\ \text{J}
$$

The change in gravitational potential energy is

$$
\Delta U_g = mg\Delta y = (5.0)(10)(1.5) = +75\ \text{J}
$$

So $W_f = -150 + 75 = -75$ J. The box lost 150 J of kinetic energy: 75 J went into height, and friction took the other 75 J.

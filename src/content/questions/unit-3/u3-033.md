stem: |
  A student swings a small ball of mass $m$ on a string in a vertical circle of
  radius $R$. The string breaks just as the ball passes the top of the circle,
  where it is moving horizontally with speed $v$. Air resistance is negligible.
  What is the ball's kinetic energy when it has fallen to the height of the
  bottom of the circle?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac12 m v^2$'
  B: '$2mgR$'
  C: '$\tfrac12 m(v^2 + 2gR)$'
  D: '$\tfrac12 m(v^2 + 4gR)$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'This is the kinetic energy at the top. The ball then falls and speeds up, gaining kinetic energy.'
  B: 'This is the kinetic energy the ball gains on the way down. It also keeps the kinetic energy it had when the string broke.'
  C: 'This uses a drop of $R$. The top of the circle is a full diameter, $2R$, above the bottom.'
---

After the string breaks, only gravity acts on the ball, so the ball–Earth system's mechanical energy is conserved. The path does not matter, only the drop in height.

The top of the circle is $2R$ above the bottom:

$$
K_{\text{bottom}} = K_{\text{top}} + mg(2R) = \tfrac12 m v^2 + 2mgR
$$

Factor out $\tfrac12 m$:

$$
K_{\text{bottom}} = \tfrac12 m\left(v^2 + 4gR\right)
$$

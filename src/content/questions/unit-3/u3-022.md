stem: |
  A ball of mass $m$ is thrown straight up from the ground with speed $v_0$ at
  time $t = 0$. Air resistance is negligible, and the gravitational potential
  energy of the ball–Earth system is zero at the ground. Which pair of
  expressions gives the ball's kinetic energy $K$ and the system's potential
  energy $U$ as functions of time while the ball is in the air?
choices:
  A: '$K = \tfrac12 m(v_0^2 - g^2t^2)$ and $U = mg(v_0 t - \tfrac12 gt^2)$'
  B: '$K = \tfrac12 m(v_0 - gt)^2$ and $U = mg(v_0 t + \tfrac12 gt^2)$'
  C: '$K = \tfrac12 m(v_0 - gt)^2$ and $U = mg(v_0 t - \tfrac12 gt^2)$'
  D: '$K = \tfrac12 m(v_0^2 + g^2t^2)$ and $U = \tfrac12 mg^2t^2$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This squares $v_0$ and $gt$ separately, but the speed is $v_0 - gt$, and the whole difference must be squared. Adding these two expressions does not give a constant total.'
  B: 'The height is $v_0 t - \tfrac12 gt^2$. The acceleration points down, opposite the launch, so the $\tfrac12 gt^2$ term subtracts.'
  D: 'These describe a ball that falls from rest, with speed $gt$. This ball starts upward at $v_0$, so it slows down first.'
---

**Velocity and height:** with up as positive, the acceleration is $-g$, so

$$
v = v_0 - gt \qquad y = v_0 t - \tfrac12 g t^2
$$

**Energies:**

$$
K = \tfrac12 m v^2 = \tfrac12 m (v_0 - gt)^2
$$

$$
U = mgy = mg\left(v_0 t - \tfrac12 g t^2\right)
$$

**Check:** expand $K$ and add $U$:

$$
K = \tfrac12 m v_0^2 - m v_0 g t + \tfrac12 m g^2 t^2
$$

$$
K + U = \tfrac12 m v_0^2
$$

The total stays constant, as it must with no air resistance.

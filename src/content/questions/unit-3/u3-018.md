stem: |
  The graph shows the size of the force needed to compress a spring as a
  function of the spring's total length. How much elastic potential energy is
  stored in the spring when its length is 0.15 m?
choices:
  A: '2.5 J'
  B: '3.75 J'
  C: '5.0 J'
  D: '7.5 J'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'This uses the spring''s length, 0.15 m, as if it were the compression. The compression is how far it is from its natural length.'
  C: 'This multiplies the force by the compression and leaves out the $\tfrac12$. The force grows from zero, so the stored energy is the area of a triangle.'
  D: 'This multiplies the force by the length, 0.15 m. Neither the length nor a full rectangle is right.'
---

First read the natural length: the force is zero at 0.25 m, so that is the spring's length when it is neither stretched nor compressed.

At a length of 0.15 m the spring is compressed by $\Delta x = 0.25 - 0.15 = 0.10$ m, and the graph shows a force of 50 N.

The stored energy is the area under the force graph from the natural length to that compression, a triangle:

$$
U_s = \tfrac12 F\,\Delta x = \tfrac12 (50\ \text{N})(0.10\ \text{m}) = 2.5\ \text{J}
$$

The same answer comes from the spring constant: $k = 50/0.10 = 500$ N/m, and $\tfrac12 k\,\Delta x^2 = \tfrac12 (500)(0.10)^2 = 2.5$ J.

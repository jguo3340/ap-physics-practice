stem: |
  Two identical pucks slide on a level, frictionless table. Each puck is
  attached to one end of an identical spring whose other end is fixed to a pin
  in the table, and each puck moves in a circle around its pin at a constant
  speed. Puck 2 moves faster than puck 1, on a larger circle. How do the works
  $W_1$ and $W_2$ done by the springs on the pucks during one full revolution
  compare?
choices:
  A: '$W_2 > W_1 > 0$'
  B: '$W_1 > W_2 > 0$'
  C: '$W_1 = W_2 > 0$'
  D: '$W_1 = W_2 = 0$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Puck 2''s spring is stretched more and pulls harder, but force alone does not mean work. Work needs a component of force along the motion, and these forces have none.'
  B: 'Neither spring does any work. Each spring''s pull is always perpendicular to its puck''s velocity.'
  C: 'The springs do pull on the pucks, but always toward the center while the pucks move along the circle. A force perpendicular to the motion does zero work.'
---

Each spring pulls its puck toward the pin, along the radius of the circle. The puck moves along the circle, tangent to it. The radius and the tangent are always at $90^\circ$:

$$
W = F d \cos 90^\circ = 0
$$

You can also check with energy. Each puck moves at a constant speed, so its kinetic energy does not change. Each spring keeps the same stretch, so its elastic energy does not change either. No energy is transferred, so no work is done, no matter how hard the springs pull.

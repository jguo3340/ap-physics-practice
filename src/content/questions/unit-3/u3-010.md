stem: |
  A spring with spring constant 400 N/m has already been stretched 3.0 cm
  from its natural length. How much more work must be done to stretch it an
  additional 3.0 cm?
choices:
  A: '0.18 J'
  B: '0.36 J'
  C: '0.54 J'
  D: '0.72 J'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is the work for the first 3.0 cm. The second 3.0 cm takes more work, because the spring pulls back harder the more it is stretched.'
  B: 'This just doubles the first 3.0 cm, as if the force were constant. The spring force keeps growing, so the second stretch takes more.'
  D: 'That is the total energy stored at 6.0 cm. The first 0.18 J was already stored before this stretch began.'
---

The work needed equals the increase in the spring's potential energy, $U_s = \tfrac12 kx^2$. Convert to meters first.

$$
W = \tfrac12 k\left(x_2^2 - x_1^2\right) = \tfrac12 (400)\left(0.060^2 - 0.030^2\right)
$$

$$
W = 200\,(0.0036 - 0.0009) = 0.54\ \text{J}
$$

Because energy goes as $x^2$, doubling the stretch makes the stored energy four times as big: 0.18 J at 3.0 cm and 0.72 J at 6.0 cm. The extra 3.0 cm costs the difference, three times as much as the first 3.0 cm.

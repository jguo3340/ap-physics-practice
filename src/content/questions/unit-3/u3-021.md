stem: |
  Two carts, of mass $m$ and $2m$, are joined by a light rigid rod and roll
  together at speed $v_0$ along a level track. The lighter cart is in front. The
  front cart rolls up a short ramp, and at one instant it is a height $h$ above
  the track while the heavier cart is still on the level part. Friction and the
  wheels' rotation are negligible. What is the kinetic energy of the two-cart
  system at that instant?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac32 m v_0^2 - mgh$'
  B: '$\tfrac32 m v_0^2 - 3mgh$'
  C: '$\tfrac12 m v_0^2 - mgh$'
  D: '$\tfrac32 m v_0^2$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'This lifts both carts by $h$. Only the front cart has gone up the ramp; the heavier cart is still on the level track.'
  C: 'This starts with the kinetic energy of the front cart only. The rod makes both carts move together, so the system starts with $\tfrac12 (3m) v_0^2$.'
  D: 'Some of the kinetic energy has turned into gravitational potential energy, because the front cart has risen.'
---

Take the system to be both carts, the rod, and Earth. With no friction, the mechanical energy stays the same.

**At the start:** both carts move at $v_0$, and both are on the level track (take $U = 0$ there).

$$
K_0 = \tfrac12 (m + 2m) v_0^2 = \tfrac32 m v_0^2
$$

**At the instant described:** only the front cart (mass $m$) has risen, so

$$
U = mgh
$$

Energy conservation gives

$$
K = K_0 - U = \tfrac32 m v_0^2 - mgh
$$

The rod keeps the carts moving at the same speed, so this kinetic energy is shared by both carts.

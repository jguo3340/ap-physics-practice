stem: |
  A student pulls a sled across level snow using a spring scale held
  horizontally, so that the sled moves at a constant velocity. The student
  wants to find the work done on the sled by friction from the snow. Which of
  the following measurements are enough to find it?
choices:
  A: 'The mass of the sled and the distance it moves'
  B: 'The speed of the sled and the time it moves'
  C: 'The mass of the sled, its speed, and the time it moves'
  D: 'The reading on the spring scale and the distance the sled moves'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The mass gives the sled''s weight, but the friction force also depends on the coefficient of friction, which is unknown.'
  B: 'Speed times time gives the distance, but nothing here tells you the size of the friction force.'
  C: 'The kinetic energy stays constant, so mass and speed tell you nothing about friction. You still need the friction force.'
---

Work done by friction is $W = F_f d\cos\theta$, so you need the size of the friction force and the distance.

The key is the constant velocity. The net force on the sled is zero, so the horizontal pull from the spring scale exactly balances friction. The scale reading **is** the size of the friction force.

Friction points opposite to the motion, so

$$
W_f = -F_{\text{scale}}\, d
$$

The mass alone cannot work, because the friction force also depends on the coefficient of friction, which the student does not know.

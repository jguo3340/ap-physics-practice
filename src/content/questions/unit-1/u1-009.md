stem: |
  A cart rolls along a level track, speeding up at a constant rate. A launcher
  fixed to the cart fires a ball straight up relative to the cart, and the cart
  keeps speeding up while the ball is in the air. Air resistance is negligible.
  When the ball falls back to the height of the launcher, where is it?
choices:
  A: 'Behind the launcher, because the ball keeps the forward speed the cart had when it fired, while the cart keeps gaining speed'
  B: 'Right at the launcher, because the ball moves forward with the cart the whole time'
  C: 'Ahead of the launcher, because the speeding-up cart gave the ball an extra forward push as it fired'
  D: 'Directly above the spot on the track where it was fired, because nothing pushes the ball forward once it leaves the launcher'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'That would be right if the cart moved at a constant speed. Here the cart keeps speeding up after the ball leaves, so the cart moves forward farther than the ball does.'
  C: 'Once the ball leaves the launcher, nothing pushes it forward any more, so it cannot gain forward speed. It cannot get ahead of a cart that keeps speeding up.'
  D: 'The ball leaves with the same forward velocity as the cart and keeps it, because no horizontal force acts on it. It does move forward, just not as far as the cart.'
---

Look at it from the ground. At the moment of firing, the ball and the cart have the same forward velocity, $u$.

- **The ball** has no horizontal force on it once it leaves, so it keeps moving forward at $u$. In time $t$ it goes $x_{\text{ball}} = u t$.
- **The cart** keeps accelerating, so in the same time it goes $x_{\text{cart}} = u t + \tfrac12 a t^2$.

The cart gets ahead by $\tfrac12 a t^2$, so the ball comes down behind the launcher.

From the cart's point of view, the ball seems to drift toward the back while it rises and falls. If the cart moved at a constant speed instead ($a = 0$), the ball would land right back in the launcher.

stem: |
  A car starts from rest and speeds up with constant acceleration along a
  straight road. It travels a distance $d$ during the first time interval $T$.
  How far does it travel during the next time interval $T$?
choices:
  A: '$d$'
  B: '$2d$'
  C: '$3d$'
  D: '$4d$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'That would be true at a constant speed. The car is speeding up, so it covers more ground in the second interval than in the first.'
  B: 'That assumes the car''s average speed doubles. Its speed goes from 0 to $v$ in the first interval and from $v$ to $2v$ in the second, so the average speeds are $\tfrac12 v$ and $\tfrac32 v$: three times as much.'
  D: '$4d$ is how far the car goes in both intervals together. The question asks only about the second one, so take away the first $d$.'
---

Starting from rest with constant acceleration, the distance grows with the **square** of the time:

$$
x = \tfrac12 a t^2
$$

- After time $T$: $x = \tfrac12 a T^2 = d$.
- After time $2T$: $x = \tfrac12 a (2T)^2 = 4 \cdot \tfrac12 a T^2 = 4d$.

So during the second interval alone, the car travels $4d - d = 3d$.

Another way to see it: the car's speed rises steadily from 0 to $v$ in the first interval and from $v$ to $2v$ in the second. Its average speed in the second interval, $\tfrac32 v$, is three times its average in the first, $\tfrac12 v$, and both intervals last the same time.

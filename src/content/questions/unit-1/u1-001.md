stem: |
  A cart starts from rest and speeds up at a constant 2.0 m/s². How far does
  it travel in the first 3.0 s?
choices:
  A: '3.0 m'
  B: '6.0 m'
  C: '9.0 m'
  D: '18 m'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Halving $a \times t$ gives 3.0, but distance depends on $t^2$, so this drops a factor of $t$.'
  B: 'That is the final speed, $v = at = 6.0$ m/s, not the distance traveled.'
  D: 'This leaves out the ½ in $\Delta x = \tfrac12 a t^2$.'
---

The cart starts from rest, so $v_0 = 0$. With constant acceleration,

$$
\Delta x = v_0 t + \tfrac12 a t^2 = 0 + \tfrac12 (2.0\ \text{m/s}^2)(3.0\ \text{s})^2 = 9.0\ \text{m}
$$

A quick check: the average speed is halfway between 0 and the final 6.0 m/s, so 3.0 m/s for 3.0 s, which is 9.0 m.

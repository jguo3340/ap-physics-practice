stem: |
  Two identical balls, P and Q, are tied together with a light string. A
  student holds ball P so that ball Q hangs straight down below it, then lets go
  at $t = 0$. Air resistance is negligible. Which statement about the speed of
  the two-ball system's center of mass at time $t$, while both balls are still
  falling, is correct?
choices:
  A: 'It is $gt$ relative to the ground and $gt$ relative to ball P.'
  B: 'It is zero relative to the ground, because the balls are tied together.'
  C: 'It is $gt$ relative to the ground and zero relative to ball P.'
  D: 'It is $\tfrac12 gt$ relative to the ground, because the motion is shared by two balls.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Ball P falls with the same velocity as the center of mass, so relative to P the center of mass is not moving at all.'
  B: 'The string joins the balls to each other, but nothing holds the system up. Gravity is the only outside force, so the whole system accelerates downward.'
  D: 'Each ball has its own weight, so the total force is $2mg$ on a total mass of $2m$. The center of mass still accelerates at $g$.'
---

**Relative to the ground:** once released, the only outside force on the two-ball system is gravity, $2mg$, acting on a total mass of $2m$. So the center of mass accelerates at $g$ and, starting from rest, has speed $gt$ after time $t$.

In fact, each ball falls freely at $g$. The string stays slack, because nothing makes one ball fall faster than the other.

**Relative to ball P:** ball P also has speed $gt$, in the same direction. The center of mass sits a fixed distance below P and moves along with it, so relative to P its speed is $gt - gt = 0$.

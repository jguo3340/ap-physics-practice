stem: |
  A small puck rests on the smooth, level floor of a train car that is moving
  forward at a constant speed. Friction between the puck and the floor is
  negligible. The train then begins to speed up. While the train is speeding up,
  how would a person standing beside the track and a passenger sitting in the
  train each describe the puck's motion?
choices:
  A: 'Person beside the track: it keeps moving forward at a constant velocity. Passenger: it speeds up toward the back of the car.'
  B: 'Person beside the track: it speeds up toward the front. Passenger: it stays at rest.'
  C: 'Person beside the track: it speeds up toward the back. Passenger: it speeds up toward the back.'
  D: 'Person beside the track: it slows down. Passenger: it stays at rest.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'Nothing pushes the puck forward. The floor is frictionless, so the train cannot drag the puck along with it.'
  C: 'The person beside the track sees no horizontal force on the puck, so the puck cannot speed up in their frame. Only the passenger, who is accelerating, sees it move backward.'
  D: 'No horizontal force acts on the puck, so in the ground frame its velocity cannot change at all. And the passenger sees the train move out from under the puck, so the puck does not stay at rest relative to the car.'
---

**Ground frame (person beside the track):** with a frictionless floor, no horizontal force acts on the puck. By Newton's first law it keeps the velocity it had, moving forward at the train's original constant speed.

**Train frame (passenger):** the train is now speeding up, so it pulls ahead of the puck. The passenger sees the car's floor move forward under the puck, which looks like the puck sliding **toward the back** of the car, faster and faster.

Both descriptions are correct in their own frames. The difference comes from the passenger's frame accelerating forward. Relative to an accelerating frame, an object with no forces on it appears to accelerate the opposite way.

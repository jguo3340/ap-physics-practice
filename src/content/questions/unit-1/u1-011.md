stem: |
  A ball is rolled up a long, straight incline. Take $+x$ to point up the
  incline. At $t = 0$ the ball's velocity is $+3.0$ m/s, and its acceleration is
  a constant $1.2\ \text{m/s}^2$ directed down the incline. Which statement
  describes the ball's motion at $t = 4.0$ s?
choices:
  A: 'It is moving in the $+x$ direction and slowing down.'
  B: 'It is moving in the $+x$ direction and speeding up.'
  C: 'It is moving in the $-x$ direction and slowing down.'
  D: 'It is moving in the $-x$ direction and speeding up.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'That describes the ball only until $t = 2.5$ s, when it stops. After that it rolls back down the incline.'
  B: 'The acceleration points down the incline, so the ball can never speed up while it moves up the incline.'
  C: 'Once the ball is rolling back down, its velocity and acceleration both point in the $-x$ direction, so it speeds up, not down.'
---

With constant acceleration, $v = v_0 + a t$. Down the incline is the $-x$ direction, so $a = -1.2\ \text{m/s}^2$:

$$
v = 3.0\ \text{m/s} + (-1.2\ \text{m/s}^2)(4.0\ \text{s}) = -1.8\ \text{m/s}
$$

The negative sign means the ball is moving in the $-x$ direction, back down the incline. (It stopped at $t = 3.0 / 1.2 = 2.5$ s and turned around.)

At 4.0 s the velocity and the acceleration both point in the $-x$ direction. When they point the same way, the object speeds up.

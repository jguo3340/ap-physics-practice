stem: |
  The graph shows the velocity of a toy car moving along a straight line. From
  $t = 0$ to $t = 5$ s, what are the car's average velocity and its average
  speed?
choices:
  A: 'Average velocity 1 m/s, average speed 1 m/s'
  B: 'Average velocity 1 m/s, average speed 2.6 m/s'
  C: 'Average velocity 2.6 m/s, average speed 2.6 m/s'
  D: 'Average velocity 1 m/s, average speed 5 m/s'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Average speed uses the total distance, not the size of the displacement. The car goes 9 m forward and then 4 m back, so it travels 13 m in all.'
  C: 'The area below the time axis is displacement in the −x direction, because the car is moving backward then. Only distance adds the two areas as positive.'
  D: 'That averages the starting and ending speeds (6 m/s and 4 m/s). Averaging the end values works for velocity when the acceleration is constant, but not for speed once the car turns around.'
---

The area between the line and the time axis is the displacement. Area above the axis is motion forward, and area below it is motion backward.

- From 0 to 3 s the car moves forward: $\tfrac12 (3\ \text{s})(6\ \text{m/s}) = 9\ \text{m}$.
- From 3 to 5 s it moves backward: $\tfrac12 (2\ \text{s})(4\ \text{m/s}) = 4\ \text{m}$.

**Average velocity** uses the displacement, a vector, so the backward part subtracts:

$$
\bar v = \frac{9\ \text{m} - 4\ \text{m}}{5\ \text{s}} = 1\ \text{m/s}
$$

**Average speed** uses the total distance, a scalar, so both parts add:

$$
\frac{9\ \text{m} + 4\ \text{m}}{5\ \text{s}} = 2.6\ \text{m/s}
$$

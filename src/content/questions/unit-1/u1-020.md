stem: |
  A marble is released from rest at the top of a ramp of length $L$. The ramp
  is rough, and the amount of friction is unknown. The marble rolls down the
  ramp, across a level shelf of length $s$, and off the edge of the shelf. It
  falls a vertical distance $H$ and lands a horizontal distance $D$ from the
  edge. Air resistance is negligible. Which two measurements, used together with
  no other data, are enough to find the marble's speed as it leaves the shelf?
choices:
  A: 'The ramp length $L$ and the height of the top of the ramp'
  B: 'The fall height $H$ and the shelf length $s$'
  C: 'The landing distance $D$ and the ramp length $L$'
  D: 'The fall height $H$ and the landing distance $D$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Energy conservation on the ramp would need the friction losses, and the friction is unknown. The ramp cannot tell you the launch speed here.'
  B: 'The fall height gives the time in the air, but the shelf length says nothing about the speed. You need a horizontal distance covered during that time.'
  C: 'The landing distance helps, but without the fall time you cannot turn it into a speed. The ramp length does not give that time.'
---

Once the marble leaves the shelf it is a projectile launched horizontally, so the two directions can be handled separately.

**Vertical:** it starts with no vertical speed and falls $H$, so the time in the air is

$$
H = \tfrac12 g t^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = \sqrt{\frac{2H}{g}}
$$

**Horizontal:** nothing acts horizontally, so the marble covers $D$ at its launch speed $v$:

$$
v = \frac{D}{t}
$$

So $H$ and $D$ together give the launch speed. Anything on the ramp is useless here, because the unknown friction means you cannot use energy conservation to find the speed at the bottom.

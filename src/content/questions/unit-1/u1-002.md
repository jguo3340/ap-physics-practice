stem: |
  A runner completes exactly one lap of a 400 m circular track in 100 s,
  finishing at the starting line. What are the runner's average speed and the
  magnitude of the runner's average velocity for the lap?
choices:
  A: 'Average speed 4.0 m/s, average velocity 4.0 m/s'
  B: 'Average speed 4.0 m/s, average velocity 0'
  C: 'Average speed 0, average velocity 4.0 m/s'
  D: 'Average speed 0, average velocity 0'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Average velocity uses displacement, not distance. After a full lap the displacement is zero.'
  C: 'This swaps the two. Speed uses the 400 m traveled; velocity uses the zero displacement.'
  D: 'Average speed uses the total distance traveled, 400 m, which is not zero.'
---

Average speed is a scalar: distance traveled over time.

$$
\text{average speed} = \frac{400\ \text{m}}{100\ \text{s}} = 4.0\ \text{m/s}
$$

Average velocity is a vector: displacement over time. The runner ends at the starting line, so the displacement is zero, and so is the average velocity.

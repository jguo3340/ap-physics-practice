stem: |
  At $t = 0$ a ball is thrown upward at an angle, toward a smooth vertical wall.
  At time $t_1$, while the ball is still rising, it bounces off the wall. The
  bounce is perfectly elastic and the wall exerts no friction. Which row of
  graphs could show the ball's horizontal velocity $v_x$ and vertical velocity
  $v_y$ as functions of time?
choices:
  A: 'Row 1'
  B: 'Row 2'
  C: 'Row 3'
  D: 'Row 4'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Row 1 ignores the bounce. The wall pushes the ball horizontally, so $v_x$ must reverse at $t_1$.'
  C: 'Row 3 is what a bounce off a ceiling would look like. A wall pushes horizontally, so it reverses $v_x$, not $v_y$.'
  D: 'A smooth wall can only push straight out from its surface, which is horizontal. It cannot change $v_y$, so $v_y$ has no jump.'
---

Think about the direction of the force from the wall. A smooth (frictionless) wall can only push perpendicular to its surface, and a vertical wall's surface faces sideways. So during the bounce the wall pushes the ball **horizontally**.

- **Horizontal velocity:** before the bounce, nothing acts horizontally, so $v_x$ is constant. The elastic bounce reverses it to the same size in the opposite direction. After that it is constant again.
- **Vertical velocity:** the wall exerts no vertical force, so gravity alone controls $v_y$ the whole time. It decreases in a straight line with slope $-g$, with no jump at $t_1$.

That is row 2.

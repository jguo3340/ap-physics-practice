stem: |
  An engineer records the velocity $v$ of a robot moving along a straight track
  once every second. The data are shown in the graph. During which time
  interval does the robot return to the position it had at $t = 0$?
choices:
  A: '$3\ \text{s} \le t < 5\ \text{s}$'
  B: '$6\ \text{s} \le t < 8\ \text{s}$'
  C: '$8\ \text{s} \le t < 10\ \text{s}$'
  D: '$10\ \text{s} \le t < 12\ \text{s}$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'At 4 s the velocity is zero, which is where the robot turns around. That is its farthest point from the start, not its return.'
  C: 'This assumes the trip back takes as long as the trip out (4 s each). The robot moves back faster, at up to 2 m/s, so it returns sooner.'
  D: 'By this time the negative area already far exceeds the positive area, so the robot has passed its starting point and kept going.'
---

Displacement is the area between the velocity graph and the time axis. The robot is back at its start when the negative area equals the positive area.

**Positive area (0 to 4 s):** a triangle with base 4 s and height 2 m/s:

$$
\tfrac12 (4)(2) = 4\ \text{m}
$$

**Negative area, adding up:**

- 4 to 6 s: a triangle, $\tfrac12 (2)(2) = 2\ \text{m}$ back.
- After 6 s: a rectangle at $-2\ \text{m/s}$, so 2 m back each second.

The robot needs 4 m back. It has 2 m by $t = 6\ \text{s}$, and the next 2 m takes one more second. So it returns at $t = 7\ \text{s}$, which is in the interval from 6 s to 8 s.

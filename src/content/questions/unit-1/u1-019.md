stem: |
  A ball is thrown straight up from a balcony. The graph shows the ball's
  speed (not velocity) as a function of time for the first 1.2 s. What is the magnitude of
  the ball's displacement from the launch point at $t = 1.2\ \text{s}$?
choices:
  A: '0.8 m'
  B: '2.4 m'
  C: '3.2 m'
  D: '4.0 m'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This is only the distance the ball falls back down after the top. The ball first rose 3.2 m.'
  C: 'This is the height of the top of the path. By 1.2 s the ball has already fallen back 0.8 m.'
  D: 'This adds the two areas, which gives the total distance traveled. A speed graph loses the direction, so the downward part has to be subtracted.'
---

The graph shows speed, which is always positive, so the area under it gives **distance**. To get displacement, use the direction of motion for each part.

**0 to 0.8 s (going up, slowing down):**

$$
d_{\text{up}} = \tfrac12 (0.8\ \text{s})(8\ \text{m/s}) = 3.2\ \text{m}
$$

**0.8 to 1.2 s (coming down, speeding up):**

$$
d_{\text{down}} = \tfrac12 (0.4\ \text{s})(4\ \text{m/s}) = 0.8\ \text{m}
$$

The ball went up 3.2 m and came back down 0.8 m:

$$
|\Delta y| = 3.2 - 0.8 = 2.4\ \text{m}
$$

The ball is 2.4 m above the launch point. You can check the graph: the speed drops by 8 m/s in 0.8 s and then grows by 4 m/s in 0.4 s, both at $10\ \text{m/s}^2$, as expected for free fall.

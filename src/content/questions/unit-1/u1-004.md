stem: |
  A boat moves at 3.0 m/s relative to the water and points straight across a
  river. The river flows at 4.0 m/s relative to the shore. What is the speed of
  the boat relative to the shore?
choices:
  A: '1.0 m/s'
  B: '3.0 m/s'
  C: '5.0 m/s'
  D: '7.0 m/s'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Subtracting the speeds would be right only if the boat pointed straight upstream, against the current.'
  B: 'That is the speed relative to the water. The current also carries the boat downstream.'
  D: 'Adding the speeds would be right only if the boat pointed straight downstream, with the current.'
---

The boat's velocity relative to the shore is its velocity relative to the water plus the water's velocity relative to the shore:

$$
\vec v_{\text{boat, shore}} = \vec v_{\text{boat, water}} + \vec v_{\text{water, shore}}
$$

The two are at right angles (across the river and downstream), so they add like the sides of a right triangle:

$$
v = \sqrt{(3.0\ \text{m/s})^2 + (4.0\ \text{m/s})^2} = 5.0\ \text{m/s}
$$

stem: |
  Ball 1 is launched horizontally with speed $v_0$ from the top of a cliff of
  height $h$ and lands a distance $D$ from the base of the cliff. Ball 2 is
  launched horizontally with speed $\tfrac12 v_0$ from the top of a cliff of
  height $4h$. Air resistance is negligible. How far from the base of its cliff
  does ball 2 land?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac12 D$'
  B: '$D$'
  C: '$2D$'
  D: '$4D$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That counts only the slower launch speed. The taller cliff also keeps ball 2 in the air longer.'
  C: 'That counts only the longer time in the air and forgets the slower launch speed. (It is also what you get if you assume a cliff 4 times as tall takes 4 times as long to fall from.)'
  D: 'That assumes a cliff 4 times as tall takes 4 times as long to fall from, and it forgets the slower launch speed. The fall time grows with the square root of the height.'
---

Treat the vertical and horizontal motions separately.

**Vertical: how long each ball is in the air.** Both balls start with no vertical velocity, so

$$
h = \tfrac12 g t^2 \quad\Rightarrow\quad t = \sqrt{\frac{2h}{g}}
$$

The time grows with the square root of the height. A cliff 4 times as tall gives $\sqrt{4} = 2$ times the time.

**Horizontal: how far each ball goes.** Nothing changes the horizontal velocity, so the distance is speed × time:

$$
x_2 = \left(\tfrac12 v_0\right)(2t_1) = v_0 t_1 = D
$$

Half the speed for twice as long: ball 2 lands the same distance out as ball 1.

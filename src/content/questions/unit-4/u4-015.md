stem: |
  Cart A, of mass 3.0 kg, moves right at 2.0 m/s toward cart B, which moves left
  at 4.0 m/s. After they collide, cart A moves left at 2.0 m/s and cart B moves
  right at 4.0 m/s. The track is level and frictionless. What is the mass of
  cart B?
choices:
  A: '0.75 kg'
  B: '1.5 kg'
  C: '3.0 kg'
  D: '6.0 kg'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This divides by an extra factor of 2. Each cart''s momentum changes by twice its starting momentum, but that factor appears on both sides and cancels.'
  C: 'Equal masses are not required for both carts to keep their speeds. Here cart B is faster, so it must be lighter to carry the same size of momentum.'
  D: 'This flips the ratio. The faster cart has to be the lighter one for the momenta to balance.'
---

Take right as positive. Before the collision the momenta are $(3.0)(+2.0) = +6.0$ for cart A and $-4.0\,m_B$ for cart B. After it they are $-6.0$ for cart A and $+4.0\,m_B$ for cart B. Momentum is conserved:

$$
6.0 - 4.0\,m_B = -6.0 + 4.0\,m_B
$$

$$
12 = 8.0\,m_B \quad\Rightarrow\quad m_B = 1.5\ \text{kg}
$$

A shortcut: the total momentum after is the negative of the total before. That can only happen if the total momentum is zero, so $(3.0)(2.0) = m_B(4.0)$.

Bonus check: the kinetic energy is 18 J before and 18 J after, so this collision is elastic.

stem: |
  A 1.0 kg block is released from rest at the top of a frictionless ramp
  inclined at 30°. It slides 1.6 m along the ramp, onto a level frictionless
  floor, and then collides with and sticks to a 3.0 kg block at rest. What is
  the speed of the two blocks after the collision?
choices:
  A: '1.0 m/s'
  B: '1.4 m/s'
  C: '2.0 m/s'
  D: '4.0 m/s'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'This uses 1.6 m as the drop in height. The block slides 1.6 m along the slope, but it only drops $1.6 \sin 30° = 0.80$ m.'
  C: 'This shares the kinetic energy between the blocks. In a collision where the blocks stick, momentum is conserved, not kinetic energy.'
  D: 'That is the 1.0 kg block''s speed just before the collision. The collision shares its momentum with the 3.0 kg block.'
---

This problem has two stages, and each one uses a different conservation law.

**Down the ramp (energy).** The block drops $h = (1.6\ \text{m})\sin 30° = 0.80$ m. With no friction,

$$
v = \sqrt{2gh} = \sqrt{2(10)(0.80)} = 4.0\ \text{m/s}
$$

**The collision (momentum).** The blocks stick, so kinetic energy is not conserved, but momentum is:

$$
(1.0)(4.0) = (1.0 + 3.0)\,v_f
$$

so $v_f = 1.0$ m/s.

stem: |
  An 8.0 kg sled slides across level snow at 5.0 m/s. The coefficient of
  kinetic friction between the sled and the snow is 0.15. What is the size of
  the sled's change in momentum during the next 2.0 s?
choices:
  A: '12 kg·m/s'
  B: '24 kg·m/s'
  C: '40 kg·m/s'
  D: '160 kg·m/s'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is the size of the friction force in newtons. The change in momentum is the force multiplied by the 2.0 s it acts.'
  C: 'That is all of the sled''s starting momentum, which would mean it stops. Friction only slows it to 2.0 m/s in 2.0 s.'
  D: 'This multiplies the sled''s weight by the time. Only the friction force changes the horizontal momentum.'
---

Friction is the only horizontal force. Its size is

$$
F_f = \mu_k F_N = \mu_k mg = (0.15)(8.0)(10) = 12\ \text{N}
$$

The change in momentum equals the impulse:

$$
|\Delta p| = F_f\,\Delta t = (12\ \text{N})(2.0\ \text{s}) = 24\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

It is worth checking that the sled is still moving after 2.0 s, since friction stops acting once it stops. Its momentum goes from 40 to 16 kg·m/s, so its speed is 2.0 m/s: still sliding.

stem: |
  Two carts of equal mass are joined by a stretchy cord on a level track.
  The graph shows the velocity of each cart over 5 s. Which of the
  following statements about the two-cart system is correct?
choices:
  A: 'The system''s momentum decreases, so a net external force must act on it.'
  B: 'The system''s kinetic energy stays the same, so no forces act on either cart.'
  C: 'The faster cart loses momentum, so the cord must pull harder on it than on the slower cart.'
  D: 'The system''s momentum stays the same, so no net external force is needed to explain the motion.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'One cart loses momentum, but the other gains exactly as much. The total stays the same.'
  B: 'The kinetic energy actually decreases, from 10 units ($3^2 + 1^2$) to 8 units ($2^2 + 2^2$). And the carts'' velocities change, so forces do act on them.'
  C: 'The cord pulls on the two carts with equal and opposite forces (Newton''s third law). That is why one cart''s loss in momentum equals the other''s gain.'
---

Because the carts have the same mass, the total momentum is proportional to the sum of the velocities. Read the graph at the start and the end:

- At $t = 0$: $3 + 1 = 4$ m/s.
- At $t = 5$ s: $2 + 2 = 4$ m/s.

In fact, the two curves are mirror images about $v = 2$ m/s, so the sum is 4 m/s at every moment. The total momentum never changes, which means the net external force on the system is zero. The cord's forces on the carts are internal: equal in size and opposite in direction.

Kinetic energy is not conserved, though. Some of it is stored in the stretched cord or turned into thermal energy.

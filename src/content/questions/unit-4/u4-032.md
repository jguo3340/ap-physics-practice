stem: |
  On a track with small but noticeable friction, a student starts cart 1 moving
  toward cart 2, which is at rest. At $t = 4\ \text{s}$, cart 1 hits cart 2 and
  they stick together. Motion sensors record each cart's velocity from
  $t = 0$ to $t = 8\ \text{s}$. Which velocities should the student use to test
  whether the two-cart system's momentum changes during the collision?
choices:
  A: 'Each cart''s velocity just before and just after $t = 4\ \text{s}$'
  B: 'Each cart''s average velocity from 0 to 4 s and from 4 s to 8 s'
  C: 'Each cart''s velocity at $t = 0$ and at $t = 8\ \text{s}$'
  D: 'Each cart''s velocity just before $t = 4\ \text{s}$ and its average velocity from 4 s to 8 s'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'Friction slows the carts during those long intervals. Averages over 4 s include that slowing, which has nothing to do with the collision.'
  C: 'Between 0 and 8 s, friction has been taking momentum away for the whole time. The difference would mostly measure friction, not the collision.'
  D: 'The average after the collision is lowered by friction over the next 4 s, so it is smaller than the speed just after the collision.'
---

Friction is an outside force, so it changes the system's momentum over time. But the collision itself lasts only a moment. During that short time, friction has almost no effect compared with the large forces between the carts.

To isolate the collision, compare the momentum **just before** and **just after** it:

$$
m_1 v_{1,\text{before}} + m_2 v_{2,\text{before}} \quad\text{vs.}\quad (m_1 + m_2)\,v_{\text{after}}
$$

Any measurement spread over seconds before or after mixes in the slowing caused by friction.

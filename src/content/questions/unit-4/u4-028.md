stem: |
  A 45 kg cart starts at rest on a level, frictionless track. The graph shows
  the net horizontal force on the cart as a function of time. What is the
  cart's speed at $t = 4\ \text{s}$?
choices:
  A: '1.3 m/s'
  B: '2.7 m/s'
  C: '4.0 m/s'
  D: '5.3 m/s'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This divides the peak force by the mass, which gives an acceleration, not a speed. The speed comes from the impulse, the area under the graph.'
  B: 'This uses only the flat middle part of the graph, 120 N·s. The sloped parts at the start and end add area too.'
  D: 'This treats the whole graph as a 60 N rectangle for 4 s. The sloped ends are triangles, with half the area of a rectangle.'
---

The impulse is the area under the force–time graph. Split the graph into pieces:

- 0 to 1 s: a triangle, $\tfrac12 (1)(60) = 30\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$.
- 1 to 3 s: a rectangle, $(2)(60) = 120\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$.
- 3 to 4 s: a triangle, $\tfrac12 (1)(60) = 30\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$.

$$
J = 30 + 120 + 30 = 180\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}
$$

The cart starts at rest, so its final momentum equals the impulse:

$$
v = \frac{J}{m} = \frac{180}{45} = 4.0\ \text{m/s}
$$

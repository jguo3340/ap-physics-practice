stem: |
  An object starts at rest. The net force on it is $+6$ N from $t = 0$ to
  $t = 2$ s, zero from 2 s to 4 s, and $-3$ N from 4 s to 6 s. Which of the
  following best describes the object's momentum?
choices:
  A: 'It is constant from 0 to 2 s, zero from 2 s to 4 s, and negative from 4 s to 6 s.'
  B: 'It increases from 0 to 2 s, drops to zero at 2 s and stays there until 4 s, then becomes negative.'
  C: 'It increases from 0 to 2 s, stays constant from 2 s to 4 s, then decreases from 4 s to 6 s but stays positive.'
  D: 'It increases from 0 to 2 s, stays constant from 2 s to 4 s, then decreases to zero at 6 s.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This describes the force, not the momentum. The force tells you how fast the momentum changes.'
  B: 'Zero net force means the momentum stops changing, not that it becomes zero. The object keeps moving.'
  D: 'The negative force is half as big as the positive one and lasts the same time, so it removes only half of the momentum: 6 of the 12 kg·m/s.'
---

The net force is the rate of change of momentum, so each interval's impulse $F\,\Delta t$ is the change in momentum during it:

| Interval | Impulse | Momentum at the end |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 to 2 s | $(6)(2) = +12$ | $12\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ |
| 2 s to 4 s | $0$ | $12\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ |
| 4 s to 6 s | $(-3)(2) = -6$ | $6\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ |

The momentum rises steadily, stays at 12 kg·m/s while there is no net force, and then falls to 6 kg·m/s. It is still positive, so the object is still moving forward at 6 s.

stem: |
  A 4.0 kg block and a 2.0 kg block sit side by side, touching, on a level,
  frictionless surface. A constant horizontal force of 12 N pushes on the
  4.0 kg block, so that both blocks start from rest and move together. What
  is the change in momentum of the 4.0 kg block during the first 3.0 s?
choices:
  A: '12 kg·m/s'
  B: '24 kg·m/s'
  C: '36 kg·m/s'
  D: '72 kg·m/s'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is the change in momentum of the 2.0 kg block, not the 4.0 kg block.'
  C: 'This uses the full 12 N as the net force on the 4.0 kg block. The 2.0 kg block pushes back on it, so the net force on it is only 8 N.'
  D: 'This multiplies the force by the final speed instead of by the time.'
---

Treat both blocks as one system first. The 12 N is the only horizontal external force:

$$
a = \frac{12\ \text{N}}{6.0\ \text{kg}} = 2.0\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

After 3.0 s, both blocks move at $v = at = (2.0)(3.0) = 6.0$ m/s.

The 4.0 kg block goes from rest to 6.0 m/s, so

$$
\Delta p = (4.0\ \text{kg})(6.0\ \text{m/s}) = 24\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

The impulse view gives the same answer. The 2.0 kg block needs $(2.0)(2.0) = 4.0$ N to accelerate, so it pushes back on the 4.0 kg block with 4.0 N. The net force on the 4.0 kg block is $12 - 4.0 = 8.0$ N, and $(8.0\ \text{N})(3.0\ \text{s}) = 24\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$.

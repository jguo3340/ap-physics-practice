stem: |
  A ball hangs from a string. It is pulled aside and released, and at the bottom
  of its swing, moving horizontally at speed $v$, it hits a box of equal mass
  that sits at rest on a level, frictionless floor. Which of the following
  outcomes of the collision is impossible?
choices:
  A: 'The ball stops, and the box slides away at speed $v$.'
  B: 'The ball and box move forward together at speed $\tfrac12 v$.'
  C: 'The ball keeps moving forward more slowly, and the box slides forward faster than the ball.'
  D: 'The ball bounces backward, and the box slides forward.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'This is a perfectly elastic collision between equal masses. Momentum and kinetic energy are both conserved, so it can happen.'
  B: 'This is a perfectly inelastic collision. Momentum is conserved, $mv = 2m\left(\tfrac12 v\right)$, and some kinetic energy is lost, which is allowed.'
  C: 'This is a partly elastic collision. It conserves momentum and loses a little kinetic energy, which is allowed.'
---

Every possible outcome must conserve momentum, and kinetic energy can stay the same or decrease, but never increase.

Check choice D. Say the ball bounces back at speed $u$. Momentum (forward positive) gives the box's speed:

$$
mv = -mu + m v_{\text{box}} \quad\Rightarrow\quad v_{\text{box}} = v + u
$$

The box would move **faster** than the ball was going, and the ball would still be moving too:

$$
K_{\text{after}} = \tfrac12 m u^2 + \tfrac12 m (v + u)^2 > \tfrac12 m v^2
$$

That creates kinetic energy out of nothing, so it cannot happen. A ball bounces back only when it hits something heavier than itself.

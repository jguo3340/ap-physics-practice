stem: |
  Identical disks X and Y are on a level, frictionless air table. Disk X slides
  with velocity $\vec{v}_0$ and hits disk Y, which is at rest, off-center. The
  collision is perfectly elastic, and the disks move away at different angles.
  Which of the following must be true after the collision?
choices:
  A: 'The velocity of the two-disk system''s center of mass points in the direction of $\vec{v}_0$.'
  B: 'Each disk has half of the original kinetic energy.'
  C: 'The two disks have momenta of equal magnitude.'
  D: 'Disk X is at rest.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The kinetic energy is shared between the disks, but not necessarily equally. How it splits depends on how off-center the hit is.'
  C: 'The momenta must add up to the original momentum, but they need not be equal in size. A glancing hit gives disk Y only a small momentum.'
  D: 'Disk X stops only in a head-on collision. In an off-center hit, both disks keep moving.'
---

No outside horizontal force acts on the two-disk system, so its total momentum is conserved:

$$
\vec{p}_{\text{after}} = \vec{p}_{\text{before}} = m\vec{v}_0
$$

The center of mass velocity is the total momentum divided by the total mass:

$$
\vec{v}_{\text{cm}} = \frac{m\vec{v}_0}{2m} = \tfrac12 \vec{v}_0
$$

So the center of mass keeps moving in the direction of $\vec{v}_0$ at half the speed, before, during, and after the collision. How the momentum and energy split between the two disks depends on the details of the hit.

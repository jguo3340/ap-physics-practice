stem: |
  A 3.0 kg cart rolling at 4.0 m/s on a level, frictionless track collides with
  a 1.0 kg cart at rest, and the two carts stick together. Which of the
  following correctly describes the total kinetic energy of the two carts and
  their speed after the collision?
choices:
  A: 'The kinetic energy decreases, and the carts move at 3.0 m/s.'
  B: 'The kinetic energy stays the same, and the carts move at 3.0 m/s.'
  C: 'The kinetic energy stays the same, and the carts move at about 3.5 m/s.'
  D: 'The kinetic energy decreases, and the carts move at 2.0 m/s.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The speed is right, but carts that stick together always lose kinetic energy. Check it: 24 J before, 18 J after.'
  C: 'That speed comes from keeping kinetic energy constant. In a collision where the objects stick, momentum is conserved but kinetic energy is not.'
  D: 'That averages the two speeds without weighting by mass. The 3.0 kg cart carries most of the momentum.'
---

There are no outside horizontal forces, so momentum is conserved:

$$
(3.0)(4.0) + (1.0)(0) = (4.0)\,v
$$

so $v = 3.0$ m/s.

Now compare kinetic energies:

$$
K_i = \tfrac12 (3.0)(4.0)^2 = 24\ \text{J}
$$

$$
K_f = \tfrac12 (4.0)(3.0)^2 = 18\ \text{J}
$$

The kinetic energy decreases. A collision where the objects stick together is perfectly inelastic: some kinetic energy turns into thermal energy, sound, and deformation of the carts.

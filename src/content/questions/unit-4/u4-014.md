stem: |
  A 0.15 kg baseball moves toward a bat at 20 m/s. The graph shows the force
  the bat exerts on the ball, in the direction opposite the ball's original
  motion, during the hit. Ignore all other forces during the hit. What is the
  ball's speed just after it leaves the bat?
choices:
  A: '20 m/s'
  B: '40 m/s'
  C: '60 m/s'
  D: '100 m/s'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'That is the change in velocity, $\Delta v = J/m$. The ball first has to lose its original 20 m/s before it moves the other way.'
  C: 'This takes the area as a full rectangle, 12 N·s. The graph is a triangle, so the impulse is half of that.'
  D: 'This uses a rectangle for the area and also adds the original speed instead of subtracting it.'
---

The impulse is the area under the force–time graph, a triangle with a base of 0.010 s and a height of 1200 N:

$$
J = \tfrac12 (0.010\ \text{s})(1200\ \text{N}) = 6.0\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}
$$

Take the bat's push as the positive direction. The ball starts with momentum $(0.15)(-20) = -3.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$. The impulse adds $+6.0$:

$$
p_f = -3.0 + 6.0 = +3.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

$$
v_f = \frac{3.0}{0.15} = +20\ \text{m/s}
$$

The ball leaves at 20 m/s in the opposite direction. Half of the impulse went into stopping it, and the other half into sending it back.

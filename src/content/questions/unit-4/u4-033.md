stem: |
  Two pucks slide on a level, frictionless surface. A 4 kg puck moves east at
  1.5 m/s, and a 2 kg puck moves north at 3 m/s. They collide and stick
  together. In which direction does the combined puck move after the collision?
choices:
  A: 'Exactly northeast, $45^\circ$ between east and north'
  B: 'Closer to north than to east'
  C: 'Closer to east than to north'
  D: 'Due east'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The 2 kg puck is faster, but direction depends on momentum, which also counts mass. Its momentum is $2 \times 3 = 6\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$, the same as the other puck''s.'
  C: 'The 4 kg puck is heavier, but it moves more slowly. Its momentum is $4 \times 1.5 = 6\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$, which only ties the other puck.'
  D: 'The north-moving puck brings northward momentum into the collision, and that momentum is conserved. The combined puck must move partly north.'
---

Momentum is conserved in each direction separately.

- **East:** only the 4 kg puck moves east: $p_x = (4)(1.5) = 6\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$.
- **North:** only the 2 kg puck moves north: $p_y = (2)(3) = 6\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$.

After the collision, the combined puck has the same total momentum, with equal east and north parts. So it moves at $45^\circ$, exactly northeast.

Its speed is $\dfrac{\sqrt{6^2 + 6^2}}{6} \approx 1.4\ \text{m/s}$, though the question only asks for the direction.

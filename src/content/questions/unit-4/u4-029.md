stem: |
  A 2 kg cart moving at 6 m/s to the right has a perfectly elastic collision
  with a 4 kg cart at rest on a level, frictionless track. Just after the
  collision, the 4 kg cart moves at 4 m/s to the right. What is the velocity of
  the 2 kg cart just after the collision?
choices:
  A: 'Zero'
  B: '2 m/s to the right'
  C: '2 m/s to the left'
  D: '4 m/s to the left'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'A light cart stops only when it hits an equal mass. If the 2 kg cart stopped, the total momentum afterward would be 16 kg·m/s instead of 12.'
  B: 'Then the total momentum afterward would be $4 + 16 = 20\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$, more than the 12 kg·m/s before.'
  D: 'Then the total momentum afterward would be $-8 + 16 = 8\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$, less than the 12 kg·m/s before.'
---

Momentum is conserved. Take right as positive:

$$
(2)(6) + (4)(0) = (2)v + (4)(4)
$$

$$
12 = 2v + 16 \quad\Rightarrow\quad v = -2\ \text{m/s}
$$

The 2 kg cart bounces back at 2 m/s to the left.

**Check that it is elastic:** kinetic energy before is $\tfrac12 (2)(6)^2 = 36\ \text{J}$. After, it is $\tfrac12 (2)(2)^2 + \tfrac12 (4)(4)^2 = 4 + 32 = 36\ \text{J}$. It matches, as it should.

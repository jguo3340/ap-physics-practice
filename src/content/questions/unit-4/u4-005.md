stem: |
  A cart of mass $2m$ moving at speed $v_0$ collides head-on with a cart of
  mass $m$ at rest on a level, frictionless track. The collision is
  perfectly elastic. Which pair gives the velocities of the $2m$ cart and the
  $m$ cart after the collision, in that order? (Positive is the $2m$ cart's
  original direction.)
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac12 v_0$ and $v_0$'
  B: '$\tfrac13 v_0$ and $\tfrac43 v_0$'
  C: '$\tfrac23 v_0$ and $\tfrac23 v_0$'
  D: '$0$ and $2v_0$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This conserves momentum, but the total kinetic energy drops from $m v_0^2$ to $\tfrac34 m v_0^2$, so the collision would not be elastic.'
  C: 'Equal velocities mean the carts move together, as if they stuck. That collision is perfectly inelastic and loses kinetic energy.'
  D: 'A moving cart stops only when it hits an equal mass. Here the total kinetic energy would double to $2mv_0^2$, which is impossible.'
---

An elastic collision conserves both momentum and kinetic energy. Call the final velocities $v_1$ (the $2m$ cart) and $v_2$ (the $m$ cart).

**Momentum:** $2m v_0 = 2m v_1 + m v_2$, so $2v_0 = 2v_1 + v_2$.

**Kinetic energy:** for a head-on elastic collision this is equivalent to the carts separating as fast as they approached: $v_2 - v_1 = v_0$.

Substitute $v_2 = v_1 + v_0$ into the momentum equation: $2v_0 = 3v_1 + v_0$, so $v_1 = \tfrac13 v_0$ and $v_2 = \tfrac43 v_0$.

Check the energy: $\tfrac12(2m)(\tfrac13 v_0)^2 + \tfrac12 m(\tfrac43 v_0)^2 = \tfrac19 m v_0^2 + \tfrac89 m v_0^2 = m v_0^2$, the same as before.

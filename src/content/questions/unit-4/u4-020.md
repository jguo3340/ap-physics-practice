stem: |
  Two carts collide on a level, frictionless track and bounce apart. A student
  knows both masses, both velocities before the collision, and the velocity of
  cart 1 after the collision. Which of the following can the student use to
  find the velocity of cart 2 after the collision?
choices:
  A: 'Conservation of momentum only'
  B: 'Conservation of kinetic energy, because the carts bounce apart'
  C: 'Both conservation of momentum and conservation of kinetic energy are needed.'
  D: 'Neither; the time the carts are in contact must also be known.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'Bouncing apart does not make a collision elastic. Some kinetic energy may still be lost, so the student cannot assume it is conserved.'
  C: 'There is only one unknown, so one equation is enough. And kinetic energy might not be conserved anyway.'
  D: 'The contact time would matter for finding the force, but not for finding a velocity. Momentum conservation does not need it.'
---

There is no net external force along the track, so momentum is conserved in every collision, whether the carts stick, bounce, or something in between:

$$
m_1 v_{1i} + m_2 v_{2i} = m_1 v_{1f} + m_2 v_{2f}
$$

Every quantity except $v_{2f}$ is known, so this one equation gives the answer.

Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision. "Bouncing apart" does not guarantee that: real carts usually lose some kinetic energy even when they bounce. Once the student has $v_{2f}$, they could check whether kinetic energy was conserved.

stem: |
  A cart of mass $3m$ rolls into a cart of mass $m$ that is at rest. During the
  collision, how do the forces the carts exert on each other compare, and how
  do the sizes of their changes in momentum compare?
choices:
  A: 'The $3m$ cart exerts the larger force, and it has the larger change in momentum.'
  B: 'The forces are equal in size, and the $m$ cart has the larger change in momentum.'
  C: 'The forces are equal in size, and the $3m$ cart has the larger change in momentum.'
  D: 'The forces are equal in size, and the changes in momentum are equal in size.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Newton''s third law says the two forces are always equal in size, no matter which object is heavier or moving.'
  B: 'The $m$ cart has the larger change in velocity, but the change in momentum is force times time, the same for both.'
  C: 'The $3m$ cart has more momentum to begin with, but it loses exactly as much as the $m$ cart gains.'
---

By Newton's third law, the two carts push on each other with forces that are equal in size and opposite in direction, at every instant of the collision.

Each force acts for the same time (the carts are touching for the same interval), so the impulses are equal in size and opposite in direction:

$$
\Delta p = F\,\Delta t \quad\text{for both carts}
$$

So the changes in momentum are equal in size and opposite in direction, which is exactly why the total momentum is conserved.

What differs is the change in **velocity**: the $m$ cart's velocity changes three times as much, because $\Delta v = \Delta p / m$.

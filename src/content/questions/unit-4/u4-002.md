stem: |
  A bumper car of known mass hits a padded wall and bounces back. During the
  collision, one sensor records the force the wall exerts on the car as a
  function of time, and a second sensor records the car's velocity as a
  function of time. Which data could be used to find the impulse the wall
  delivers to the car?
choices:
  A: 'Only the force–time data'
  B: 'Only the velocity–time data'
  C: 'Either the force–time data or the velocity–time data'
  D: 'Neither, unless the collision time is also measured separately'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The force–time data works, but so does the velocity data: impulse equals the change in momentum, and the mass is known.'
  B: 'The velocity data works, but so does the force data: the area under a force–time graph is the impulse.'
  D: 'The collision time is already in both data sets, since both are recorded as functions of time.'
---

Impulse can be found two ways, and each data set gives one of them.

- **Force–time data:** the impulse is the area under the force–time graph, which you can estimate by counting squares.
- **Velocity–time data:** the impulse equals the change in momentum, $J = m\,\Delta v$. Read the velocity before and after the collision and multiply the change by the known mass.

Both should give the same answer. Watch the signs with the velocity data: if the car arrives at $-3$ m/s and leaves at $+2$ m/s, then $\Delta v = +5$ m/s, not $-1$ m/s.

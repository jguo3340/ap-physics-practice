stem: |
  A cart of mass $2m$ rolls with kinetic energy $K_i$ on a level, frictionless
  track and collides with a cart of mass $m$ that is at rest. The carts stick
  together. What is the kinetic energy of the two carts just after the
  collision?
choices:
  A: '$K_i$'
  B: '$\tfrac23 K_i$'
  C: '$\tfrac12 K_i$'
  D: '$\tfrac13 K_i$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Momentum is conserved, but kinetic energy is not. When carts stick together, some kinetic energy always turns into other forms.'
  C: 'Half is what remains when equal masses stick. Here the moving cart is heavier, so less of its energy is lost.'
  D: 'One third is the amount lost, not the amount left.'
---

**Momentum** is conserved in the collision. Let the first cart's speed be $v$:

$$
(2m)v = (3m)v_f \quad\Rightarrow\quad v_f = \tfrac23 v
$$

**Kinetic energy** before and after:

$$
K_i = \tfrac12 (2m) v^2 = m v^2
$$

$$
K_f = \tfrac12 (3m)\left(\tfrac23 v\right)^2 = \tfrac12 (3m)\cdot\tfrac49 v^2 = \tfrac23 m v^2
$$

So $K_f = \tfrac23 K_i$. A shortcut: for sticking collisions with one object at rest, the fraction of kinetic energy left is $\dfrac{m_{\text{moving}}}{m_{\text{total}}} = \dfrac{2m}{3m}$.

stem: |
  Cart 1 (1.0 kg) moves at $+2.0$ m/s toward cart 2 (1.0 kg), which is at rest
  on a level, frictionless track. Cart 1 carries a compressed spring that is
  released when the carts touch, pushing them apart. Which of the following
  could be the velocities of carts 1 and 2 after they separate?
choices:
  A: 'Cart 1: $0$ m/s; cart 2: $+2.0$ m/s'
  B: 'Cart 1: $+1.0$ m/s; cart 2: $+1.0$ m/s'
  C: 'Cart 1: $-1.0$ m/s; cart 2: $+2.0$ m/s'
  D: 'Cart 1: $-0.5$ m/s; cart 2: $+2.5$ m/s'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Momentum works, but the kinetic energy stays at 2.0 J. The released spring adds energy, so the total kinetic energy must increase.'
  B: 'Momentum works, but the kinetic energy drops to 1.0 J, and the carts would not separate. The spring adds energy, it does not remove it.'
  C: 'The total momentum here is $+1.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$, but it must stay $+2.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$.'
---

Any possible outcome has to pass two tests.

1. **Momentum is conserved.** The spring's forces are internal, so the total stays $(1.0)(2.0) = +2.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$.
2. **Kinetic energy increases.** The carts start with $\tfrac12 (1.0)(2.0)^2 = 2.0$ J, and the spring releases its stored energy into the carts.

| Choice | Total momentum | Total kinetic energy |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A | $+2.0$ | $2.0$ J |
| B | $+2.0$ | $1.0$ J |
| C | $+1.0$ | $2.5$ J |
| D | $+2.0$ | $3.25$ J |

Only D conserves momentum and gains energy: $\tfrac12(1.0)(0.5)^2 + \tfrac12(1.0)(2.5)^2 = 0.125 + 3.125 = 3.25$ J.

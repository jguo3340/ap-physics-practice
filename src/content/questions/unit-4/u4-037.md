stem: |
  A 90 kg astronaut and a 30 kg crate float at rest next to each other in deep
  space. The astronaut shoves the crate, and afterward the crate moves away with
  kinetic energy $K$. What is the astronaut's kinetic energy afterward?
choices:
  A: '$3K$'
  B: '$K$'
  C: '$\tfrac13 K$'
  D: '$\tfrac19 K$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The astronaut is heavier, so the astronaut moves more slowly and ends up with less kinetic energy, not more.'
  B: 'The momenta are equal in size, but the kinetic energies are not. Kinetic energy depends on speed squared, and the speeds differ.'
  D: 'This squares the mass ratio. The astronaut''s speed is $\tfrac13$ of the crate''s, which brings in a factor of $\tfrac19$, but the astronaut''s mass is 3 times larger, which brings back a factor of 3.'
---

**Momentum:** everything starts at rest, so the total momentum is zero before and after. The astronaut and crate move apart with equal and opposite momenta, $p$.

**Kinetic energy in terms of momentum:**

$$
K = \tfrac12 m v^2 = \frac{p^2}{2m}
$$

Both have the same $p$, so kinetic energy is inversely proportional to mass:

$$
\frac{K_{\text{astronaut}}}{K_{\text{crate}}} = \frac{m_{\text{crate}}}{m_{\text{astronaut}}} = \frac{30}{90} = \frac13
$$

The astronaut has $\tfrac13 K$.

stem: |
  A block of mass $3m$ slides at speed $v_0$ to the right across a level,
  frictionless floor toward a small ball of mass $m$ that hangs at rest from a
  light string. The block collides elastically with the ball. Which of the
  following describes the block's velocity just after the collision and the
  ball's speed a short time later, as it swings upward?
choices:
  A: 'Block: $\tfrac12 v_0$ to the left. Ball: slightly less than $\tfrac32 v_0$.'
  B: 'Block: $\tfrac12 v_0$ to the right. Ball: slightly less than $\tfrac32 v_0$.'
  C: 'Block: at rest. Ball: slightly less than $v_0$.'
  D: 'Block: $\tfrac12 v_0$ to the right. Ball: slightly less than $\tfrac12 v_0$.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'A heavy object hitting a lighter one keeps going forward. Bouncing back happens when a light object hits a heavier one.'
  C: 'The moving object stops only when the two masses are equal. Here the block is three times heavier than the ball.'
  D: 'Then the block and ball would move off together, and momentum would not be conserved. In an elastic collision the light ball flies off much faster than the block.'
---

**Just after the collision:** use the elastic-collision results for a moving object of mass $m_1$ hitting a resting object of mass $m_2$:

$$
v_{1} = \frac{m_1 - m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_0 = \frac{3m - m}{4m}\,v_0 = \tfrac12 v_0
$$

$$
v_{2} = \frac{2m_1}{m_1 + m_2}\,v_0 = \frac{6m}{4m}\,v_0 = \tfrac32 v_0
$$

Check momentum: $3m\left(\tfrac12 v_0\right) + m\left(\tfrac32 v_0\right) = 3mv_0$. It matches.

**A short time later:** the ball is on a string, so it swings upward along an arc. As it rises, gravity takes away some of its kinetic energy, so its speed is slightly less than $\tfrac32 v_0$. The block keeps sliding at $\tfrac12 v_0$ on the frictionless floor.

stem: |
  Two balls, of mass $m$ and $3m$, are dropped from rest at the same moment.
  Air resistance is negligible. The momentum of the lighter ball increases at
  a rate of $4.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$ every second. What is the size of
  the total momentum of the two-ball system 2.5 s after they are dropped?
choices:
  A: '10 kg·m/s'
  B: '20 kg·m/s'
  C: '30 kg·m/s'
  D: '40 kg·m/s'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is only the lighter ball''s momentum. The system includes both balls.'
  B: 'This assumes both balls gain momentum at the same rate. The heavier ball has three times the weight, so it gains momentum three times as fast.'
  C: 'That is only the heavier ball''s momentum. Add the lighter ball''s 10 kg·m/s.'
---

The rate of change of momentum equals the net force: $\dfrac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = F_{\text{net}}$. For a falling ball the net force is its weight, so the lighter ball's weight is 4.0 N.

The heavier ball has three times the mass, so its weight is 12 N. The net external force on the system is the total weight, 16 N, and it acts the whole time:

$$
\Delta p = F_{\text{net}}\,\Delta t = (16\ \text{N})(2.5\ \text{s}) = 40\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

Both balls started at rest, so the total momentum is 40 kg·m/s, downward. Both balls fall with the same acceleration, but not the same momentum.

stem: |
  A toy cannon is fixed to a cart that rolls at a constant velocity along a
  level, frictionless track. The cannon fires a ball forward, parallel to the
  track. Consider the cart, cannon, and ball as one system. Immediately after
  the ball leaves the cannon, how does the velocity of the system's center of
  mass compare with its velocity just before firing?
choices:
  A: 'It is greater, because the ball is pushed forward.'
  B: 'It is smaller, because the cart recoils backward.'
  C: 'It is the same.'
  D: 'It is zero, because the forward and backward momenta cancel.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The ball does speed up, but the cart slows down by exactly enough to keep the total momentum the same.'
  B: 'The cart does slow down, but the ball speeds up by exactly enough to keep the total momentum the same.'
  D: 'The system had momentum before the firing, and internal forces cannot remove it. Only the changes in the ball''s and cart''s momenta cancel.'
---

The firing is an internal interaction: the cannon pushes the ball forward and the ball pushes the cannon (and cart) backward with an equal and opposite force. Internal forces cannot change the total momentum of a system.

Along the track there are no external forces (it is level and frictionless), so the total momentum is the same just before and just after firing. The velocity of the center of mass is the total momentum divided by the total mass, so it is unchanged too.

The ball speeds up and the cart slows down, but the center of mass carries on exactly as before.

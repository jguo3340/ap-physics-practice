stem: |
  A space probe moves in the $+x$ direction with momentum $p_0$. A thruster
  then pushes on it in the $-x$ direction. The size of the thrust decreases
  steadily from $F_0$ to zero over a time $t_1$. No other forces act. What is
  the probe's momentum after the thruster stops?
choices:
  A: '$p_0 + \tfrac12 F_0 t_1$'
  B: '$p_0 - \tfrac12 F_0 t_1$'
  C: '$p_0 - F_0 t_1$'
  D: '$p_0$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'The thrust points in the $-x$ direction, opposite the momentum, so it reduces the momentum.'
  C: 'This assumes the thrust stays at $F_0$ the whole time. It decreases steadily to zero, so its average is $\tfrac12 F_0$.'
  D: 'The thruster''s force is an external force on the probe, so it changes the probe''s momentum.'
---

The change in momentum equals the impulse, which is the area under the force–time graph. A force that falls steadily from $F_0$ to zero makes a triangle:

$$
J = \tfrac12 F_0 t_1 \quad\text{in the } -x \text{ direction}
$$

So

$$
p = p_0 - \tfrac12 F_0 t_1
$$

Equivalently, the average force is $\tfrac12 F_0$, and the impulse is the average force times the time.

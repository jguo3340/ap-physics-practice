stem: |
  The graph shows the position $x_{\text{cm}}$ of the center of mass of a system
  of two blocks as a function of time. What can be concluded about the net
  external force on the system during this time?
choices:
  A: 'It points in the $+x$ direction.'
  B: 'It points in the $-x$ direction.'
  C: 'It is zero.'
  D: 'Nothing can be concluded without knowing the masses of the blocks.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'The center of mass is moving in the $+x$ direction, but it is slowing down. A force along the motion would speed it up.'
  C: 'With zero net external force, the center of mass would move at a constant velocity, a straight line on this graph. The curve shows its velocity changing.'
  D: 'The direction of the net external force matches the direction of the center of mass''s acceleration. The masses would only be needed for the force''s size.'
---

The slope of the $x_{\text{cm}}$–$t$ graph is the velocity of the center of mass.

- The slope is positive the whole time, so the center of mass moves in the $+x$ direction.
- The slope gets smaller and reaches zero at 4 s, so the center of mass is **slowing down**.

Slowing down while moving in $+x$ means the acceleration points in $-x$. For a system,

$$
F_{\text{net, ext}} = M_{\text{total}}\, a_{\text{cm}}
$$

so the net external force points in the $-x$ direction. Forces between the two blocks are internal and cannot change how the center of mass moves.

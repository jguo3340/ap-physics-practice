stem: |
  A cart sits at rest on a level, frictionless track. A student moves the cart
  by pulling and pushing on it with a force sensor. The graph shows the force
  the sensor exerts on the cart as a function of time. How does the cart's
  momentum change from $t = 0$ to $t = 18\ \text{s}$?
choices:
  A: 'It increases in magnitude the whole time.'
  B: 'It decreases in magnitude the whole time.'
  C: 'It increases in magnitude at first, then decreases without reversing direction.'
  D: 'It increases in magnitude at first, then decreases and reverses direction.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'After 12 s the force points the other way, opposite the cart''s motion, so the cart slows down.'
  B: 'The cart starts at rest, so its momentum can only grow at first. For the first 12 s, the force speeds it up.'
  C: 'The backward impulse, $-48\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$, is larger than the forward impulse, $+36\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$. The cart stops and then moves the other way.'
---

The change in momentum is the area under the force–time graph.

**0 to 12 s:** the force is $+3\ \text{N}$, so the impulse is $(3)(12) = +36\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$. The cart speeds up in the positive direction.

**12 to 18 s:** the force is $-8\ \text{N}$, so the impulse is $(-8)(6) = -48\ \text{N}\cdot\text{s}$. The cart slows down.

The momentum reaches zero when the backward impulse equals 36 N·s, which takes $36/8 = 4.5\ \text{s}$, at $t = 16.5\ \text{s}$. After that the force keeps pushing in the negative direction, so the cart moves backward. At 18 s its momentum is

$$
p = 36 - 48 = -12\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

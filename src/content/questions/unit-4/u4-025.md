stem: |
  A ball of mass $m$ moving horizontally at speed $v$ hits a padded wall and
  bounces straight back with speed $\tfrac12 v$. The ball is in contact with the
  wall for a time $\Delta t$. What is the magnitude of the average force the
  wall exerts on the ball?
choices:
  A: '$\dfrac{mv}{2\,\Delta t}$'
  B: '$\dfrac{mv}{\Delta t}$'
  C: '$\dfrac{3mv}{2\,\Delta t}$'
  D: '$\dfrac{2mv}{\Delta t}$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This subtracts the speeds, $v - \tfrac12 v$. Velocity has direction, and the ball reverses, so the change is $v + \tfrac12 v$.'
  B: 'This is the force needed just to stop the ball. The wall also has to send it back at $\tfrac12 v$.'
  D: 'This would be correct if the ball bounced back at its full speed $v$. It comes back at only half that.'
---

The average force is the change in momentum divided by the contact time. Take the direction the ball bounces back as positive.

$$
p_i = m(-v) = -mv
$$

$$
p_f = m\left(+\tfrac12 v\right) = +\tfrac12 mv
$$

$$
\Delta p = \tfrac12 mv - (-mv) = \tfrac32 mv
$$

$$
F_{\text{avg}} = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{3mv}{2\,\Delta t}
$$

Reversing direction always makes the change in momentum larger than either momentum alone.

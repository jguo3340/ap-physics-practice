stem: |
  The graph shows the position of a 4.0 kg object moving along a straight line
  from $t = 0$ to $t = 4.0$ s. What is the object's change in momentum over
  those 4.0 s?
choices:
  A: '$-20\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$'
  B: '$-4\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$'
  C: '$-2\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$'
  D: '$+20\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'This adds the two velocities, $(4.0)(2 + (-3))$. A change is final minus initial: $-3 - 2 = -5$ m/s.'
  C: 'This uses the average velocity over the whole 4.0 s. The change in momentum depends on the velocities at the start and end, not the average.'
  D: 'The size is right, but the object ends up moving in the negative direction, so its momentum decreased.'
---

On a position–time graph, the slope is the velocity.

- From 0 to 2.0 s: the position rises from 0 to 4 m, so $v_i = \dfrac{4}{2.0} = +2$ m/s.
- From 2.0 s to 4.0 s: it falls from 4 m to $-2$ m, so $v_f = \dfrac{-6}{2.0} = -3$ m/s.

Then, using $\Delta p = m(v_f - v_i)$,

$$
\Delta p = (4.0)(-3 - 2) = -20\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

The object turned around, so the change in momentum is bigger than either momentum alone: it had to lose its $+8$ and then gain $-12$.

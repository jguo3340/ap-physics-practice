stem: |
  Two frictionless lab carts start from rest on a level track. A student pushes
  each one with the same constant 4.0 N force for 1.5 s. One cart has a mass of
  0.5 kg, and the other has a mass of 2.0 kg. Which cart has more kinetic energy
  at the end of the push?
choices:
  A: 'The 0.5 kg cart'
  B: 'The 2.0 kg cart'
  C: 'Both carts have the same kinetic energy.'
  D: 'It cannot be determined without knowing how far each cart moved during the push.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'Both carts end with the same momentum, $6.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$. The lighter cart has to move faster to carry that momentum, and kinetic energy grows with speed squared.'
  C: 'The same force for the same time gives the same momentum, not the same kinetic energy. Equal kinetic energy would need the same force over the same distance.'
  D: 'The distances are not given, but they are not needed. The momentum and mass of each cart are enough to find its kinetic energy.'
---

Same force, same time, so both carts get the same impulse and end with the same momentum:

$$
p = F\,\Delta t = (4.0)(1.5) = 6.0\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

Write kinetic energy in terms of momentum:

$$
K = \frac{p^2}{2m}
$$

$$
K_{0.5} = \frac{6.0^2}{2(0.5)} = 36\ \text{J}
$$

$$
K_{2.0} = \frac{6.0^2}{2(2.0)} = 9.0\ \text{J}
$$

The lighter cart moves farther during the push (it is going faster), so the same force does more work on it.

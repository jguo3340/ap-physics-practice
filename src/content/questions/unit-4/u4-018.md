stem: |
  A firework shell launched from level ground would land a distance $D$ from
  the launch point if it did not explode. At the top of its path it breaks
  into two pieces, which move horizontally right after the break and land at
  the same time. The piece with one-third of the mass lands $2D$ from the
  launch point. Air resistance is negligible. Where does the other piece land?
choices:
  A: 'A distance $D$ behind the launch point'
  B: 'At the launch point'
  C: 'A distance $\tfrac12 D$ from the launch point'
  D: 'A distance $D$ from the launch point'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This swaps the masses, giving the far piece two-thirds of the mass. The heavier piece must land closer to the center of mass.'
  B: 'This treats the pieces as equal halves, landing equally far on either side of $D$. The heavier piece moves a smaller distance from the center of mass.'
  D: 'The heavier piece cannot follow the original path, because the lighter piece has moved ahead of it. The center of mass follows the original path.'
---

The explosion is internal, and gravity acts the same on the pieces as it did on the whole shell. So the **center of mass** follows the original path and lands at $D$.

Both pieces land at the same moment, so at that moment the center of mass is at

$$
x_{cm} = \frac{\tfrac13 M (2D) + \tfrac23 M\,x}{M} = D
$$

$$
\tfrac23 D + \tfrac23 x = D \quad\Rightarrow\quad x = \tfrac12 D
$$

Check: the light piece is $D$ beyond the center of mass and the heavy piece is $\tfrac12 D$ short of it. Twice the mass, half the distance.

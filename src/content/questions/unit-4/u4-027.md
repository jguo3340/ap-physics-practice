stem: |
  A student standing on a skateboard throws a 2 kg ball forward. While throwing,
  the student pushes on the ball with an average force of 40 N for 0.25 s. The
  student and skateboard have a combined mass of 50 kg. What is the magnitude of
  the change in momentum of the student and skateboard?
choices:
  A: '$0$'
  B: '$0.2\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$'
  C: '$10\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$'
  D: '$20\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The student pushes on the ball, but the ball pushes back on the student just as hard, for the same time. That backward force changes the student''s momentum.'
  B: 'This is the student''s change in velocity, $10/50$, in m/s. The question asks for the change in momentum.'
  D: 'This counts the impulse twice. The student gets the same size impulse as the ball, not double.'
---

By Newton's third law, the ball pushes back on the student with a force of the same size, 40 N, for the same 0.25 s. So the student gets an impulse of the same size as the ball, in the opposite direction:

$$
|\Delta p_{\text{student}}| = F\,\Delta t
$$

$$
|\Delta p_{\text{student}}| = (40\ \text{N})(0.25\ \text{s}) = 10\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

The masses do not matter for the change in momentum. They only decide how fast each object ends up moving: the ball gains $10/2 = 5\ \text{m/s}$, while the student gains only $10/50 = 0.2\ \text{m/s}$ backward.

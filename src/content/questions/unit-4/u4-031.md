stem: |
  A cart rolls into a stiff rubber bumper at the end of a track and comes to
  rest. A force sensor records the force the bumper exerts on the cart as a
  function of time. The stiff bumper is replaced by a softer bumper of the same
  size, and the cart is sent in at the same speed. It again comes to rest. How
  does the force–time graph for the soft bumper compare with the graph for the
  stiff bumper?
choices:
  A: 'Same peak force, lasting for a longer time'
  B: 'Lower peak force, lasting for the same time'
  C: 'Higher peak force, lasting for a shorter time, with the same area under the curve'
  D: 'Lower peak force, lasting for a longer time, with the same area under the curve'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'A longer time at the same peak force would give a larger area, meaning a larger impulse. The cart''s change in momentum has not changed.'
  B: 'A lower peak over the same time would give a smaller area, which is too little impulse to stop the cart.'
  C: 'A softer bumper squishes more, so it takes longer to stop the cart, not less time.'
---

The cart arrives at the same speed and ends at rest both times, so its change in momentum is the same. That change equals the impulse, which is the **area under the force–time graph**. So the area must be the same for both bumpers.

A softer bumper squishes farther, so the stopping takes **longer**. To keep the same area over a longer time, the force must be **smaller**. That is the whole idea behind padding, airbags, and bending your knees when you land.

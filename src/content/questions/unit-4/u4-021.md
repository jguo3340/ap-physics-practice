stem: |
  Two carts, both moving, collide on a level, frictionless track and stick
  together. For the two-cart system, how do the total kinetic energy and the
  speed of the center of mass just after the collision compare with their
  values just before?
choices:
  A: 'The kinetic energy stays the same, and the center-of-mass speed stays the same.'
  B: 'The kinetic energy stays the same, and the center-of-mass speed decreases.'
  C: 'The kinetic energy decreases, and the center-of-mass speed decreases.'
  D: 'The kinetic energy decreases, and the center-of-mass speed stays the same.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The center-of-mass speed is right, but carts that stick together always lose kinetic energy.'
  B: 'Both parts are backward: kinetic energy is lost, and the center-of-mass speed does not change.'
  C: 'Kinetic energy is lost, but the center of mass keeps its speed, because the total momentum is conserved.'
---

**Center of mass.** The collision forces are internal, and there is no net external force along the track, so total momentum is conserved. The center-of-mass velocity is $p_{\text{total}}/m_{\text{total}}$, so it does not change.

**Kinetic energy.** When objects stick together, the collision is perfectly inelastic. Some kinetic energy becomes thermal energy, sound, and deformation, so the total kinetic energy decreases.

The kinetic energy that cannot be lost is the energy of the center of mass's motion, $\tfrac12 m_{\text{total}} v_{cm}^2$. The carts can lose only the energy of their motion relative to each other, and sticking removes all of it.

stem: |
  A 2000 kg space probe is moving through deep space at 300 m/s. It fires its
  thrusters, which push it opposite its direction of motion. When the thrusters
  shut off, the probe is moving at 300 m/s in the opposite direction from where
  it started. What was the average force exerted on the probe by the thrusters?
choices:
  A: '0 N, because the probe ends with the same speed it started with'
  B: '$6.0 \times 10^5\ \text{N}$'
  C: '$1.2 \times 10^6\ \text{N}$'
  D: 'It cannot be determined without knowing how long the thrusters fired.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The speed is the same, but the velocity reversed. Reversing direction takes a large change in momentum, so the force cannot be zero.'
  B: 'This is the size of the probe''s starting momentum, in kg·m/s, not a force. It is also only half of the change in momentum.'
  C: 'This is the change in momentum, $1.2 \times 10^6\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}$. To get a force, it has to be divided by the time the thrusters fired.'
---

The change in momentum can be found. Take the original direction as positive:

$$
\Delta p = (2000)(-300) - (2000)(300)
$$

$$
\Delta p = -1.2 \times 10^6\ \text{kg}\cdot\text{m/s}
$$

The change in momentum equals the impulse, $F_{\text{avg}}\,\Delta t$. Without the time $\Delta t$, there is no way to split the impulse into a force and a time. A small force for a long time and a large force for a short time give the same impulse.

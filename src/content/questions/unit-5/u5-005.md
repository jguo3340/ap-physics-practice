stem: |
  A potter's wheel of known rotational inertia $I$ is spinning freely at a
  known angular speed $\omega_0$. The potter then presses a wet sponge against
  the rim with a steady force, and the wheel slows to a stop. Which one
  additional measurement allows the potter to find the torque the sponge exerts
  on the wheel?
choices:
  A: 'The time the wheel takes to stop'
  B: 'The force with which the potter presses the sponge against the rim'
  C: 'The radius of the wheel'
  D: 'The mass of the sponge'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The torque comes from the friction force along the rim, not the pressing force. Without the coefficient of friction, the pressing force doesn''t give the friction force.'
  C: 'The radius is only half of $\tau = rF$. The friction force is still unknown.'
  D: 'The sponge''s mass doesn''t affect the torque on the wheel. What matters is the friction force it exerts and where.'
---

With a steady force, the torque is constant, so the angular acceleration is constant. Knowing the time $t$ to stop gives it:

$$
\alpha = \frac{\Delta\omega}{\Delta t} = \frac{0 - \omega_0}{t}
$$

Then Newton's second law for rotation gives the torque: $|\tau| = I|\alpha| = I\omega_0 / t$.

(Counting the number of turns before stopping would also work, using $\omega^2 = \omega_0^2 + 2\alpha\Delta\theta$, but that isn't one of the options.)

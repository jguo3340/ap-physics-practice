stem: |
  A rod lies on a level, frictionless table and can rotate about a fixed pivot
  through its center. The rod starts at rest. A force of constant magnitude and
  fixed direction is exerted on one end of the rod, starting out perpendicular to
  the rod. The rod rotates through $60^\circ$ while the force keeps the same
  magnitude and direction. Which best describes the graph of the rod's angular
  speed against time during this rotation?
choices:
  A: 'A straight line rising from zero'
  B: 'A curve rising from zero that gets steeper and steeper'
  C: 'A curve that rises from zero and then falls back toward zero'
  D: 'A curve rising from zero that gets less and less steep'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'A straight line means a constant angular acceleration, which needs a constant torque. As the rod turns, the angle between the rod and the force shrinks, and so does the torque.'
  B: 'The slope of this graph is the angular acceleration. It would get steeper only if the torque grew, but here the torque shrinks.'
  C: 'The torque gets smaller but stays in the same direction the whole time, so it never slows the rod down. The angular speed keeps rising.'
---

The slope of an angular speed–time graph is the angular acceleration, $\alpha = \tau / I$.

The force keeps a fixed direction, but the rod turns, so the angle between them changes. At the start the force is perpendicular to the rod, giving the largest torque:

$$
\tau = r F \sin 90^\circ = rF
$$

After the rod turns $60^\circ$ toward the force's direction, the angle between them is only $30^\circ$:

$$
\tau = r F \sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12 rF
$$

The torque stays in the same direction, so the angular speed keeps increasing. But the torque shrinks, so the slope of the graph gets smaller. The graph rises from zero and bends over, less and less steep.

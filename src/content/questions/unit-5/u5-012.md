stem: |
  A ceiling fan spins at a constant rate. Consider a ladybug sitting near the
  tip of one blade. Which of the following describes the ladybug's linear
  velocity and the blade's angular velocity?
choices:
  A: 'Linear velocity: constant. Angular velocity: constant.'
  B: 'Linear velocity: constant. Angular velocity: changing.'
  C: 'Linear velocity: changing. Angular velocity: constant.'
  D: 'Linear velocity: changing. Angular velocity: changing.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The ladybug''s speed is constant, but its direction of motion keeps turning as it goes around. A changing direction means a changing velocity.'
  B: 'This has the two swapped. The fan turns through the same angle every second about a fixed axis, so its angular velocity is the one that stays constant.'
  D: 'The fan spins at a constant rate about a fixed axis, so its angular velocity does not change.'
---

**Angular velocity:** the fan turns through the same angle every second, always in the same direction about the same axis. So its angular velocity is constant.

**Linear velocity:** the ladybug moves in a circle at a constant speed, $v = \omega r$. But velocity includes direction, and the ladybug's direction of motion is always tangent to the circle, which keeps changing. So the linear velocity is changing, even though its size is constant. That change is why the ladybug has a centripetal acceleration toward the center.

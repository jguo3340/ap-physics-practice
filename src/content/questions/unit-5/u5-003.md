stem: |
  A door of width $W$ starts at rest and is free to swing on frictionless
  hinges. In each case below, every force is horizontal and perpendicular to
  the door. In which case does the door's angular speed increase at the
  greatest rate?
choices:
  A: 'A force $2F$ applied a distance $\tfrac13 W$ from the hinges'
  B: 'A force $\tfrac12 F$ applied at $\tfrac12 W$ from the hinges and another $\tfrac12 F$ in the same direction at the outer edge'
  C: 'A force $3F$ applied at $\tfrac12 W$ from the hinges and a force $F$ in the opposite direction at the outer edge'
  D: 'A single force $F$ applied at the outer edge'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The torque is $(2F)(\tfrac13 W) = \tfrac23 FW$. Doubling the force doesn''t make up for pushing so close to the hinges.'
  B: 'The torques add to $\tfrac12 F \cdot \tfrac12 W + \tfrac12 F \cdot W = \tfrac34 FW$, less than $FW$.'
  C: 'The two torques oppose each other: $3F \cdot \tfrac12 W - F \cdot W = \tfrac12 FW$.'
---

The door has the same rotational inertia in every case, so by $\alpha = \tau_{net}/I$ the greatest angular acceleration comes from the greatest net torque. With each force perpendicular to the door, $\tau = rF$:

- (A) $(2F)(\tfrac13 W) = \tfrac23 FW$
- (B) $(\tfrac12 F)(\tfrac12 W) + (\tfrac12 F)(W) = \tfrac34 FW$
- (C) $(3F)(\tfrac12 W) - (F)(W) = \tfrac12 FW$
- (D) $(F)(W) = FW$

Case D has the largest net torque.

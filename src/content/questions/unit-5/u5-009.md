stem: |
  A student wants to find the weight of a rock without a scale. A uniform board
  rests on a fulcrum at its midpoint. The rock is placed on the board a
  distance $a$ from the fulcrum. A block of known mass $m$ is placed on the
  other side, a distance $b$ from the fulcrum, where $b > a$, and the board
  balances horizontally. What is the weight of the rock?
choices:
  A: '$\dfrac{mga}{b}$'
  B: '$\dfrac{mgb}{a}$'
  C: '$\dfrac{mg(a + b)}{a}$'
  D: '$\dfrac{mgb}{a + b}$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This has the ratio upside down. The rock is closer to the fulcrum, so it must be heavier than the block to balance it.'
  C: 'Each object''s torque uses its own distance from the fulcrum. The total length $a + b$ does not appear in the balance.'
  D: 'The distance $a + b$ is the separation of the two objects, not either one''s distance from the fulcrum.'
---

The board is uniform and supported at its midpoint, so its own weight acts at the fulcrum and adds no torque. For the board to balance, the two torques about the fulcrum must be equal:

$$
W_{\text{rock}}\,a = mg\,b
$$

$$
W_{\text{rock}} = \frac{mgb}{a}
$$

Since $b > a$, the rock is heavier than the block. That makes sense: the lighter block needs the longer lever arm.

stem: |
  Three identical uniform rods are each attached to a wall by a hinge at one
  end and held at rest by a cord. Rod I points $45^\circ$ above the horizontal,
  rod II is horizontal, and rod III points $45^\circ$ below the horizontal. Which
  of the following correctly ranks the magnitudes of the torques $\tau$ exerted
  by gravity on the rods about their hinges?
choices:
  A: '$\tau_{\text{I}} = \tau_{\text{II}} = \tau_{\text{III}}$'
  B: '$\tau_{\text{I}} > \tau_{\text{II}} > \tau_{\text{III}}$'
  C: '$\tau_{\text{I}} = \tau_{\text{III}} > \tau_{\text{II}}$'
  D: '$\tau_{\text{II}} > \tau_{\text{I}} = \tau_{\text{III}}$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The weights and lengths are the same, but the torque also depends on direction. Gravity pulls straight down, and only the horizontal distance from the hinge to the center of mass acts as the lever arm.'
  B: 'Pointing up or down by the same angle gives the same horizontal distance to the center of mass, so rods I and III have equal torques.'
  C: 'This has the ranking backward. A horizontal rod puts its center of mass farthest out from the wall, so it has the largest lever arm.'
---

Gravity acts at each rod's center, a distance $\tfrac12 L$ from the hinge, and pulls straight down. The lever arm is the **horizontal** distance from the hinge to the center:

$$
\tau = mg \cdot \tfrac12 L \cos\theta
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle from the horizontal.

- Rod II: $\cos 0^\circ = 1$, the largest torque.
- Rods I and III: $\cos 45^\circ \approx 0.71$ for both. Tilting up or down by the same angle brings the center of mass the same distance closer to the wall.

So $\tau_{\text{II}} > \tau_{\text{I}} = \tau_{\text{III}}$.

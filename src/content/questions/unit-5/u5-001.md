stem: |
  Two small spheres of mass $m$ and $3m$ are joined by a light rod. The rod is
  held level at the system's center of mass and released from rest near
  Earth's surface. As it falls, it does not start to rotate. Which of the
  following correctly explains why?
choices:
  A: 'Earth pulls equally hard on both spheres, so their torques about the center of mass cancel.'
  B: 'Earth pulls three times as hard on the $3m$ sphere, but that sphere is one-third as far from the center of mass, so the torques about the center of mass cancel.'
  C: 'Earth pulls three times as hard on the $3m$ sphere, but that sphere''s greater inertia cancels its extra torque.'
  D: 'No forces act on the rod itself, so nothing can exert a torque on the system about its center of mass.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'The gravitational force is proportional to mass, so Earth pulls three times as hard on the $3m$ sphere.'
  C: 'Inertia resists changes in motion, but it does not cancel a torque. The torques cancel because of where the forces act, not because of the masses resisting.'
  D: 'Gravity acts on both spheres, and they are attached to the rod. Each of those forces exerts a torque about the center of mass; the two just happen to cancel.'
---

The center of mass sits closer to the heavier sphere. If the rod has length $L$, the center of mass is $\tfrac14 L$ from the $3m$ sphere and $\tfrac34 L$ from the $m$ sphere, because $3m \cdot \tfrac14 L = m \cdot \tfrac34 L$.

Now compare the gravitational torques about the center of mass:

$$
\tau_{3m} = (3mg)\left(\tfrac14 L\right), \qquad \tau_{m} = (mg)\left(\tfrac34 L\right)
$$

They are equal in size and turn the rod in opposite directions, so the net torque about the center of mass is zero and the rod falls without rotating. This is true for any object: gravity near Earth exerts no net torque about the center of mass.

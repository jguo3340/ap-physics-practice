stem: |
  A uniform rod of length $L$ can rotate about a pivot at its left end. A force
  of magnitude $F$ is exerted at the rod's right end, at an angle of $30^\circ$
  to the rod. Which of the following forces, exerted on an identical rod with
  the same pivot, produces the same torque about the pivot?
choices:
  A: 'A force $F$ perpendicular to the rod, at the rod''s midpoint'
  B: 'A force $2F$ perpendicular to the rod, at the rod''s midpoint'
  C: 'A force $F$ at the right end, at $60^\circ$ to the rod'
  D: 'A force $2F$ at the right end, at $30^\circ$ to the rod'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'This gives $(2F)(\tfrac12 L) = FL$, twice the original torque of $\tfrac12 FL$.'
  C: 'A larger angle with the rod puts more of the force perpendicular to it. This gives $FL \sin 60^\circ \approx 0.87FL$, more than the original.'
  D: 'Doubling the force doubles the torque, to $FL$. The original torque is only $\tfrac12 FL$.'
---

Torque is the distance from the pivot times the part of the force perpendicular to the rod:

$$
\tau = r F \sin\theta
$$

where $\theta$ is the angle between the force and the rod.

**Original force:**

$$
\tau = L F \sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12 FL
$$

**Force $F$ perpendicular at the midpoint:**

$$
\tau = \left(\tfrac12 L\right) F \sin 90^\circ = \tfrac12 FL
$$

The two match. Pushing at an angle at the end and pushing straight at the middle both give half the torque of a straight push at the end.

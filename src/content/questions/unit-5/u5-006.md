stem: |
  A uniform meterstick turns freely on an axle through its 50 cm mark. A
  downward force of magnitude $F$ is exerted on the stick at its 90 cm mark.
  Where must an upward force of magnitude $4F$ be exerted to keep the stick
  level and at rest?
choices:
  A: 'At the 40 cm mark'
  B: 'At the 60 cm mark'
  C: 'At the 10 cm mark'
  D: 'At the 90 cm mark'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'An upward push to the left of the axle turns the stick the same way as the downward force on the right. The two torques would add instead of cancel.'
  C: 'An upward push on the left side turns the stick the same way as the downward force on the right, and 40 cm from the axle its torque is also four times too big.'
  D: 'At 40 cm from the axle, the $4F$ force makes four times the torque of $F$. It would tip the stick the other way.'
---

The stick is uniform and turns on an axle at its center, so its own weight exerts no torque. For the stick to stay at rest, the two forces must exert equal and opposite torques about the axle.

- The downward force $F$ acts 40 cm to the right of the axle: torque $F \times 40\ \text{cm}$, tipping the right end down.
- To oppose it, the upward force must lift the right side, so it goes to the **right** of the axle, at distance $d$ with $4F \times d = F \times 40\ \text{cm}$.

That gives $d = 10$ cm to the right of the 50 cm mark: the 60 cm mark.

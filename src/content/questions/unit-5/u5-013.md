stem: |
  Four forces are exerted on a disk of radius $R$ that is free to spin about its
  center, as shown. The forces have magnitudes $F_1 = F$, $F_2 = 2F$,
  $F_3 = 2F$, and $F_4 = F$. $F_1$ is tangent to the rim, $F_2$ is tangent to
  the dashed circle of radius $\tfrac12 R$, $F_3$ points straight out from the
  center, and $F_4$ makes a $30^\circ$ angle with the radius. Which two forces
  together exert zero net torque about the center?
choices:
  A: '$F_1$ and $F_2$'
  B: '$F_1$ and $F_3$'
  C: '$F_2$ and $F_4$'
  D: '$F_3$ and $F_4$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: '$F_3$ points straight out from the center, so it has no torque. That leaves $F_1$''s torque, $FR$, unbalanced.'
  C: '$F_4$ makes only a $30^\circ$ angle with the radius, so its torque is $FR \sin 30^\circ = \tfrac12 FR$. That cancels only half of $F_2$''s torque.'
  D: '$F_3$ has no torque, and $F_4$ has a torque of $\tfrac12 FR$, so together they still turn the disk.'
---

Torque is $\tau = rF\sin\theta$, where $\theta$ is the angle between the force and the line from the center to where the force acts. Take counterclockwise as positive.

- $F_1$: at the rim, tangent, turning the disk counterclockwise: $\tau_1 = +R(F) = +FR$.
- $F_2$: at $\tfrac12 R$, tangent, turning the disk clockwise: $\tau_2 = -\tfrac12 R(2F) = -FR$.
- $F_3$: along the radius, so $\theta = 0$ and $\tau_3 = 0$.
- $F_4$: at the rim, $30^\circ$ from the radius, turning counterclockwise: $\tau_4 = +R(F)\sin 30^\circ = +\tfrac12 FR$.

Only $F_1$ and $F_2$ cancel: $+FR - FR = 0$. Doubling the force while halving the distance keeps the torque the same size.

stem: |
  A uniform beam of mass $M$ and length $L$ is attached to a wall by a hinge at
  its left end and held horizontal by a vertical rope. A bucket, also of mass
  $M$, hangs from the beam. In which arrangement does the hinge exert no force
  on the beam?
choices:
  A: 'The rope is at the right end and the bucket hangs from the right end.'
  B: 'The rope and the bucket are both at the center of the beam.'
  C: 'The rope is at the right end and the bucket hangs from the center.'
  D: 'The rope is at the center and the bucket hangs from the right end.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Taking torques about the rope, the beam''s own weight (at the center, $\tfrac12 L$ from the rope) must be balanced by an upward hinge force of $\tfrac12 Mg$.'
  C: 'Taking torques about the rope, both weights act $\tfrac12 L$ to its left, so the hinge must push up with $Mg$.'
  D: 'The bucket hangs beyond the rope, so it tends to tip the beam. The hinge must pull down with $Mg$ to stop it.'
---

Take torques about the point where the rope is attached. The rope then exerts no torque, so the torque of the hinge force $F_h$ (at distance $d$ from the rope) must balance the torques of the two weights, each found from the weight times its distance from the rope.

When the rope and the bucket are both at the center, both weights act right at the rope: they exert no torque about it. Then $F_h d = 0$, so $F_h = 0$. The rope alone holds up the full $2Mg$, and the hinge is not needed at all.

In the other arrangements, at least one weight is off to one side of the rope, and the hinge has to push or pull to keep the beam level.

stem: |
  A 5.0 kg box rests on a level floor. The coefficient of static friction
  between the box and the floor is 0.60, and the coefficient of kinetic friction
  is 0.40. A student pushes horizontally on the box with a force of 25 N. What
  is the magnitude of the frictional force on the box?
choices:
  A: '0 N'
  B: '20 N'
  C: '25 N'
  D: '30 N'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The push is trying to slide the box, so static friction must act to hold it in place. With no friction, the 25 N push would accelerate the box.'
  B: 'This uses kinetic friction, $\mu_k N$. Kinetic friction only applies once the box is sliding, and this box never starts to slide.'
  D: 'This is the largest force static friction can supply, $\mu_s N$. Static friction only grows as large as it needs to, and here it needs to match 25 N.'
---

First check whether the box slides. The normal force is $N = mg = (5.0)(10) = 50\ \text{N}$, so the largest static friction force is

$$
f_{s,\max} = \mu_s N = (0.60)(50) = 30\ \text{N}
$$

The push (25 N) is less than 30 N, so the box **does not slide**. It stays at rest, and the net force on it is zero. Static friction matches the push:

$$
f_s = 25\ \text{N}
$$

The formula $\mu_s N$ gives only the maximum static friction, not its actual value.

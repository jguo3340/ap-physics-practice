stem: |
  Two identical satellites move in circular orbits around the same planet.
  Satellite 1's orbit has radius $r$, and satellite 2's orbit has radius $3r$.
  What is the ratio $K_2/K_1$ of their kinetic energies?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac19$'
  B: '$\tfrac13$'
  C: '$\tfrac{1}{\sqrt3}$'
  D: '$3$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is the ratio of the gravitational forces, which go as $1/r^2$. Kinetic energy goes as $1/r$.'
  C: 'That is the ratio of the speeds. Kinetic energy depends on speed squared, so square it: $(1/\sqrt3)^2 = 1/3$.'
  D: 'A wider orbit is slower, not faster. Gravity is weaker out there, so less speed is needed to keep the satellite on a circle.'
---

For a circular orbit, gravity supplies the centripetal force:

$$
\frac{GMm}{r^2} = \frac{mv^2}{r}
$$

Multiply both sides by $r$ and divide by 2:

$$
K = \tfrac12 mv^2 = \frac{GMm}{2r}
$$

So kinetic energy is proportional to $1/r$. Tripling the orbit radius gives one third of the kinetic energy:

$$
\frac{K_2}{K_1} = \frac{r}{3r} = \frac13
$$

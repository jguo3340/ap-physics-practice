stem: |
  A heavy truck and a small car collide head-on. During the collision, how do
  the sizes of the forces they exert on each other compare, and which vehicle
  has the larger acceleration?
choices:
  A: 'The truck exerts the larger force, and the car has the larger acceleration.'
  B: 'The forces are equal, and the car has the larger acceleration.'
  C: 'The forces are equal, and the accelerations are equal.'
  D: 'The car exerts the larger force, and the truck has the larger acceleration.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'The accelerations are right, but the forces are a third-law pair and must be equal in size.'
  C: 'The forces are equal, but the same force gives a smaller acceleration to a larger mass, so the car''s acceleration is larger.'
  D: 'Both parts are backward. The forces are equal, and the lighter car accelerates more.'
---

**Forces:** the truck's push on the car and the car's push on the truck are a Newton's third-law pair, equal in size and opposite in direction. The masses and speeds do not matter.

**Accelerations:** each vehicle feels the same size of force, so by Newton's second law,

$$
a = \frac{F}{m}
$$

the car, with the smaller mass, has the larger acceleration. That large acceleration is why people in the car have a much rougher collision than people in the truck, even though the forces are equal.

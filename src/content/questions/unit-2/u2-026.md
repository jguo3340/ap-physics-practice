stem: |
  For which of the following objects is the net force not zero?
choices:
  A: 'A book resting on a table'
  B: 'An elevator moving upward at a constant speed'
  C: 'A skydiver falling at a constant terminal speed'
  D: 'A car driving around a curve at a constant speed'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The book is at rest and stays at rest, so its velocity is not changing. The table''s upward push balances the book''s weight.'
  B: 'Constant speed in a straight line means constant velocity, so the acceleration is zero. The cable''s pull balances the elevator''s weight.'
  C: 'At terminal speed the skydiver falls in a straight line at a constant speed. Air resistance has grown to match the weight.'
---

By Newton's first law, the net force on an object is zero exactly when its **velocity** (speed and direction) is constant.

- The book: at rest the whole time, so constant velocity.
- The elevator: constant speed in a straight line, so constant velocity.
- The skydiver: constant speed in a straight line, so constant velocity.
- The car: constant speed, but its **direction** keeps changing as it goes around the curve. A changing direction means a changing velocity, so the car is accelerating toward the center of the curve.

Only the car needs a nonzero net force. Friction from the road provides it.

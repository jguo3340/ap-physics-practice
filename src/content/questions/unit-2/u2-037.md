stem: |
  Block A, of mass 6.0 kg, sits on a frictionless ramp tilted $30^\circ$ above
  the horizontal. A light string runs from block A up over a light,
  frictionless pulley at the top and down to block B, which sits on a second
  frictionless ramp tilted $45^\circ$ on the other side. What mass must block B
  have for the blocks to stay at rest?
choices:
  A: '3.0 kg'
  B: '4.2 kg'
  C: '6.0 kg'
  D: '8.5 kg'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This balances block A''s pull, 30 N, against block B''s full weight. Block B is on a ramp too, so only $m_B g \sin 45^\circ$ pulls on the string.'
  C: 'Equal masses would balance only if the ramps had equal angles. Block B''s steeper ramp gives it a larger pull for the same mass.'
  D: 'This has the ratio upside down. The block on the steeper ramp needs less mass, not more.'
---

Each block pulls on the string with the part of its weight that points down its ramp. For the blocks to stay at rest, those pulls must be equal (the tension is the same along a light string over a frictionless pulley).

$$
m_A g \sin 30^\circ = m_B g \sin 45^\circ
$$

$$
(6.0)(0.50) = m_B (0.71)
$$

$$
m_B = \frac{3.0}{0.71} \approx 4.2\ \text{kg}
$$

stem: |
  A puck slides north across frictionless, level ice. Two horizontal forces act
  on it: 12 N toward the south and 5 N toward the east. What single additional
  horizontal force would make the puck move at a constant velocity?
choices:
  A: '17 N, directed north of west'
  B: '13 N, directed south of east'
  C: '13 N, directed north of west'
  D: '7 N, directed north'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Forces at right angles do not add like ordinary numbers. Their combined size is found with the Pythagorean theorem: 13 N, not 17 N.'
  B: 'That is the net force the two forces already produce. To cancel it, the new force must point the opposite way.'
  D: 'Subtracting 5 from 12 treats the forces as if they pointed along the same line. They are perpendicular.'
---

For constant velocity the net force must be zero, so the new force must cancel the combined effect of the two forces already acting. The puck's direction of motion does not matter: Newton's first law needs only zero net force.

The two forces are perpendicular, so their resultant has a size of

$$
\sqrt{12^2 + 5^2} = \sqrt{169} = 13\ \text{N}
$$

and it points south and east. The force that cancels it is also 13 N, pointing the opposite way: 12 N worth to the north and 5 N worth to the west, which is north of west.

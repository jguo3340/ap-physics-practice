stem: |
  A 4 kg cart sits on a level, frictionless table. A light string tied to the
  cart passes over a light, frictionless pulley at the table's edge, and a 1 kg
  block hangs from the other end. The system is released from rest. The
  experiment is then repeated with a different cart and hanging block. Which
  combination gives the same acceleration as the original system?
choices:
  A: 'A 5 kg cart and a 2 kg hanging block'
  B: 'A 3 kg cart and a 2 kg hanging block'
  C: 'An 8 kg cart and a 2 kg hanging block'
  D: 'A 4 kg cart and a 2 kg hanging block'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Adding 1 kg to each object does not keep the ratio the same. Here the pull is 20 N on 7 kg, about $2.9\ \text{m/s}^2$.'
  B: 'This keeps the total mass at 5 kg but doubles the hanging block, so the pulling force doubles. That doubles the acceleration to $4\ \text{m/s}^2$.'
  D: 'Doubling only the hanging block increases the pulling force more than it increases the total mass. The acceleration rises to about $3.3\ \text{m/s}^2$.'
---

Treat the cart, string, and hanging block as one system. The only outside force that pulls the system along is the weight of the hanging block, $m_h g$. It accelerates the total mass $M + m_h$:

$$
a = \frac{m_h g}{M + m_h}
$$

**Original:** $a = \dfrac{(1)(10)}{4 + 1} = 2\ \text{m/s}^2$.

The acceleration depends only on the fraction of the total mass that hangs. In the original, $1/5$ of the mass hangs. Doubling both masses keeps that fraction:

$$
a = \frac{(2)(10)}{8 + 2} = 2\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

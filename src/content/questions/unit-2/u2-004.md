stem: |
  A planet has three times the mass of Earth and twice Earth's radius. What is
  the gravitational field strength at the planet's surface? (On Earth's
  surface it is 10 N/kg.)
choices:
  A: '3.8 N/kg'
  B: '7.5 N/kg'
  C: '15 N/kg'
  D: '120 N/kg'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This divides by the radius cubed. Surface gravity depends on $1/R^2$, so doubling the radius divides it by 4, not 8.'
  C: 'This divides by the radius instead of the radius squared.'
  D: 'This multiplies by the radius squared. A larger radius puts the surface farther from the center, which weakens gravity.'
---

The surface gravitational field of a planet is

$$
g = \frac{GM}{R^2}
$$

Compare with Earth by ratios. Tripling the mass multiplies $g$ by 3. Doubling the radius divides it by $2^2 = 4$.

$$
g = (10\ \text{N/kg}) \times \frac{3}{2^2} = 7.5\ \text{N/kg}
$$

Even with three times the mass, the planet's surface gravity is weaker than Earth's, because its surface is twice as far from its center.

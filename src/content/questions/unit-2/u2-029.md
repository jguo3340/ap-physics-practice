stem: |
  A 2 kg cart and a 6 kg cart sit at rest on a level track, not touching. A
  student pushes only the 2 kg cart, giving it an acceleration of
  $4\ \text{m/s}^2$, while the 6 kg cart stays at rest. What is the acceleration
  of the center of mass of the two-cart system?
choices:
  A: '$0$'
  B: '$1\ \text{m/s}^2$'
  C: '$2\ \text{m/s}^2$'
  D: '$4\ \text{m/s}^2$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'The center of mass of the system moves whenever any part of the system moves. One cart speeding up is enough to accelerate it.'
  C: 'This is a plain average of the two accelerations. The center of mass weights each cart by its mass, and the cart at rest is three times heavier.'
  D: 'That is the acceleration of the light cart alone. The heavy cart is part of the system too, and it is not moving.'
---

The center of mass acceleration is a mass-weighted average of the parts:

$$
a_{\text{cm}} = \frac{m_1 a_1 + m_2 a_2}{m_1 + m_2}
$$

$$
a_{\text{cm}} = \frac{(2)(4) + (6)(0)}{2 + 6} = 1\ \text{m/s}^2
$$

Another way to see it: the only outside horizontal force on the system is the student's push. That push is $F = (2)(4) = 8\ \text{N}$, and it acts on a system with a total mass of 8 kg, so $a_{\text{cm}} = 8/8 = 1\ \text{m/s}^2$.

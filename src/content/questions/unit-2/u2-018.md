stem: |
  A student of mass $m$ stands on a bathroom scale in an elevator. The elevator
  is moving downward and slowing down as it approaches a lower floor. How does
  the scale reading compare with $mg$?
choices:
  A: 'It is greater than $mg$, because the acceleration points upward.'
  B: 'It is equal to $mg$, because the student''s mass does not change.'
  C: 'It is less than $mg$, because the elevator is moving downward.'
  D: 'It is less than $mg$, because the elevator is slowing down.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The mass and the weight do not change, but the scale measures the normal force, which changes when the student accelerates.'
  C: 'The direction of motion does not decide the reading. The direction of the acceleration does.'
  D: 'Slowing down while moving down means the acceleration points up. That calls for a larger normal force, not a smaller one.'
---

Find the direction of the acceleration first. The elevator moves down but is slowing down, so its acceleration points **up** (opposite to the velocity).

The scale reads the normal force $F_N$ it exerts on the student. With up as positive,

$$
F_N - mg = ma \quad\Rightarrow\quad F_N = mg + ma > mg
$$

The scale reads more than the student's weight. It is the same feeling as an elevator starting to go **up**: in both cases the acceleration points up.

stem: |
  A block of mass $m_1$ sits on a table, pressed against a small fixed stop. A
  light string attached to the block passes over a light, frictionless pulley
  and holds a hanging block of mass $m_2$, as shown. Both blocks are at rest.
  What is the tension in the string?
choices:
  A: '$m_2 g$'
  B: '$(m_1 + m_2)\,g$'
  C: '$\dfrac{m_1 m_2}{m_1 + m_2}\,g$'
  D: '$m_1 g$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The string holds up only the hanging block. The block on the table is supported by the table, and the stop keeps it from sliding.'
  C: 'That is the tension when the two blocks accelerate together on a frictionless table. Here nothing moves.'
  D: 'The weight of the table block is balanced by the table''s normal force. It is not what the string supports.'
---

The hanging block is at rest, so the forces on it balance. Only two forces act on it: the string pulls up with tension $T$, and gravity pulls down with $m_2 g$.

$$
T - m_2 g = 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad T = m_2 g
$$

The block on the table does not change this. The string pulls it toward the pulley with force $T$, and the stop pushes back with an equal force so it stays put.

The trap is the formula for two blocks accelerating together, $\dfrac{m_1 m_2}{m_1+m_2}g$. The tension is less than $m_2 g$ only when the hanging block is accelerating downward.

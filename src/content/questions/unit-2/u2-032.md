stem: |
  A person walks forward at a constant speed on level ground, pulling a loaded
  sled behind them with a horizontal rope. Which of the following correctly
  describes the horizontal forces exerted on the person?
choices:
  A: 'A backward pull from the rope and a forward friction force from the ground, equal in size'
  B: 'A forward pull from the rope and a backward friction force from the ground'
  C: 'A backward pull from the rope and a larger forward push from the person''s own legs'
  D: 'Only a backward pull from the rope'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The rope pulls the sled forward, so by the third law it pulls the person backward. A rope can only pull toward the thing on its other end.'
  C: 'The legs are part of the person, so their push is an internal force and does not appear on the person''s free-body diagram. What the legs do is push back on the ground, so the ground pushes the person forward with friction.'
  D: 'With only a backward force, the person would slow down. Walking at constant speed means another horizontal force must balance the rope''s pull.'
---

Draw a free-body diagram for the person, looking only at horizontal forces exerted **on** the person by other objects.

- **The rope** is attached to the sled behind the person, so it pulls the person **backward**.
- **The ground:** the person's feet push backward on the ground, and by Newton's third law the ground pushes the person **forward**. This is static friction, since the planted foot does not slip.

The person moves at a constant velocity, so the net force is zero. The forward friction force must equal the backward pull of the rope.

The person's legs and muscles are not separate forces on the diagram. They are how the person gets the ground to push on them.

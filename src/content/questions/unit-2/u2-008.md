stem: |
  A diver leaves a springboard, pulls into a tuck, and spins several times
  before entering the water. Air resistance is negligible. Which of the
  following describes the acceleration of the diver's center of mass while
  she is in the air?
choices:
  A: 'It is greater than $g$ while she spins, because spinning adds to her motion.'
  B: 'It is zero at the top of her flight, where she stops rising.'
  C: 'It is less than $g$ while she is tucked, because her body is more compact.'
  D: 'It is $g$, directed downward, the whole time.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Spinning is caused by internal motion and the push from the board. In the air, the only external force is gravity, so the center of mass accelerates at exactly $g$.'
  B: 'At the top her vertical velocity is zero, but gravity is still acting, so her acceleration is still $g$ downward.'
  C: 'Changing body shape is internal. It changes how fast she spins, not how her center of mass accelerates.'
---

Treat the diver as a system. Once she leaves the board, the only external force on her is gravity, $mg$, so her center of mass accelerates at

$$
a_{cm} = \frac{mg}{m} = g \quad \text{downward}
$$

Her center of mass follows a simple projectile path, a parabola, no matter how she twists, tucks, or spins. Those motions are internal: they move her arms and legs around the center of mass, but they cannot change its path.

stem: |
  Three identical boxes rest without sliding on three boards. The boards are
  tilted at $15^\circ$, $25^\circ$, and $35^\circ$ above the horizontal. Which
  board exerts the largest frictional force on its box?
choices:
  A: 'The $15^\circ$ board'
  B: 'All three frictional forces are equal.'
  C: 'The $35^\circ$ board'
  D: 'It cannot be determined without the coefficient of static friction.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The $15^\circ$ board does push hardest with its normal force, but that only sets the maximum static friction. The actual friction is whatever it takes to hold the box, which is smallest here.'
  B: 'Each box has zero net force, but the forces being balanced are different. The part of the weight pulling down the board is larger on a steeper board.'
  D: 'Static friction does not have to equal $\mu_s N$. Since every box is at rest, friction exactly balances the downhill part of gravity, and that can be found without $\mu_s$.'
---

Each box is at rest, so the net force on it is zero. Along the board, only two forces act: the part of gravity pulling down the slope, $mg \sin\theta$, and static friction pulling up the slope. They must balance:

$$
f_s = mg \sin\theta
$$

The boxes are identical, so friction depends only on $\sin\theta$, which is largest for the steepest board:

- $\sin 15^\circ \approx 0.26$
- $\sin 25^\circ \approx 0.42$
- $\sin 35^\circ \approx 0.57$

The $35^\circ$ board exerts the largest frictional force. The coefficient of friction only tells you how steep a board can get before the box slips.

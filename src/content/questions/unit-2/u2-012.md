stem: |
  A student releases a cart from rest on a frictionless ramp inclined at an
  angle $\theta$. The student measures the distance $d$ the cart travels along
  the ramp at several times $t$, then plots $d$ on the vertical axis against
  $t^2$ on the horizontal axis. The points lie on a straight line. What does the
  slope of the line equal?
choices:
  A: '$g\sin\theta$'
  B: '$\tfrac12 g\cos\theta$'
  C: '$2g\sin\theta$'
  D: '$\tfrac12 g\sin\theta$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is the cart''s acceleration. Distance from rest is $\tfrac12 a t^2$, so the slope is half of the acceleration.'
  B: 'The acceleration along a frictionless ramp comes from gravity''s component along the ramp, $g\sin\theta$. The cosine part is balanced by the normal force.'
  C: 'This doubles the acceleration instead of halving it. Distance from rest is $\tfrac12 a t^2$, so the slope is half of $g\sin\theta$.'
---

On a frictionless ramp, the component of gravity along the ramp gives the cart an acceleration

$$
a = g\sin\theta
$$

Starting from rest with constant acceleration, the distance traveled is

$$
d = \tfrac12 a t^2 = \left(\tfrac12 g\sin\theta\right) t^2
$$

This has the form $y = (\text{slope})\,x$ with $y = d$ and $x = t^2$, so the slope is $\tfrac12 g\sin\theta$. Plotting against $t^2$ instead of $t$ is what turns the curve into a straight line, and the student can find $g$ from the slope: $g = \dfrac{2(\text{slope})}{\sin\theta}$.

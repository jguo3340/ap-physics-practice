stem: |
  A ball on a string is swung in a vertical circle. The figure shows the ball at
  the moment it is level with the center of the circle, moving upward. Air
  resistance is negligible. Which forces act on the ball at this moment?
choices:
  A: 'The tension in the string only, toward the center'
  B: 'Gravity, the tension, and a centripetal force'
  C: 'Gravity, the tension, and a force in the direction of motion'
  D: 'Gravity and the tension only'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Gravity acts on the ball at every point of the circle, including this one.'
  B: 'Centripetal force is not a separate force. It is the name for the part of the net force that points toward the center, which here is the tension.'
  C: 'Nothing pushes the ball along its path. It keeps moving because of its inertia, not because of a force.'
---

Only two objects touch or pull on the ball: the string and Earth.

- **Tension** pulls along the string, toward the center of the circle (horizontally, at this moment).
- **Gravity** pulls straight down.

There is no extra "centripetal force". That term describes the role a real force plays. Here, the tension points toward the center and provides the centripetal acceleration, while gravity, which is perpendicular to the string at this point, slows the ball as it rises.

The net force points down and toward the center, so it is not aimed at the center of the circle. That is fine: the ball is not moving at a constant speed in a vertical circle.

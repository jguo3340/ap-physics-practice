stem: |
  Three moons, X, Y, and Z, orbit the same planet. The table gives each moon's
  mass and orbit radius in terms of $m$ and $r$.

  $\begin{array}{c|c|c} \text{Moon} & \text{Mass} & \text{Orbit radius} \\ \hline X & m & 2r \\ Y & 3m & 3r \\ Z & 4m & 4r \end{array}$

  Which of the following correctly ranks the magnitudes of the gravitational
  forces $F$ that the planet exerts on the moons?
choices:
  A: '$F_Y > F_X = F_Z$'
  B: '$F_Y = F_Z > F_X$'
  C: '$F_Z > F_Y > F_X$'
  D: '$F_X > F_Y > F_Z$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'This divides each mass by the radius, not the radius squared. Gravity falls off as $1/r^2$.'
  C: 'This ranks by mass alone and ignores the distance. Z is the most massive, but it is also the farthest away.'
  D: 'This ranks by distance alone and ignores the masses. A heavier moon feels a larger force at the same distance.'
---

The force from the planet on each moon is $F = \dfrac{GMm_{\text{moon}}}{R^2}$. The planet's mass $M$ is the same for all three, so compare $m_{\text{moon}}/R^2$:

$$
X:\ \frac{m}{(2r)^2} = \frac{m}{4r^2} = 0.25\,\frac{m}{r^2}
$$

$$
Y:\ \frac{3m}{(3r)^2} = \frac{3m}{9r^2} \approx 0.33\,\frac{m}{r^2}
$$

$$
Z:\ \frac{4m}{(4r)^2} = \frac{4m}{16r^2} = 0.25\,\frac{m}{r^2}
$$

So $F_Y > F_X = F_Z$.

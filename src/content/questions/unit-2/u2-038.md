stem: |
  Blocks A and B sit on a level, frictionless surface, connected by a spring of
  negligible mass. A student pulls block A to the right with a constant force of
  magnitude $F$, and the blocks accelerate to the right together, with the
  spring steadily stretched and not oscillating. The spring exerts a force of
  magnitude $F_A$ on block A and $F_B$ on block B. Which of the following is
  correct?
choices:
  A: '$F > F_A = F_B$'
  B: '$F = F_A = F_B$'
  C: '$F = F_A > F_B$'
  D: '$F > F_A > F_B$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'If the spring pulled back on block A as hard as the student pulls forward, block A would have no net force and could not accelerate.'
  C: 'A spring with negligible mass pulls equally hard on both ends. Otherwise the massless spring would have a net force on it and an impossible acceleration.'
  D: 'The spring is light, so the forces at its two ends must be equal. Only block masses can make forces differ along the chain.'
---

**The spring's two ends:** a spring with negligible mass needs no net force to accelerate, so it pulls equally on whatever is at each end:

$$
F_A = F_B
$$

**Block A:** the student pulls it forward with $F$, and the spring pulls it backward with $F_A$. Block A accelerates forward, so the forward force must be larger:

$$
F - F_A = m_A a > 0 \quad\Rightarrow\quad F > F_A
$$

**Block B:** the spring is the only horizontal force on it, so $F_B = m_B a$. Only part of the student's pull reaches block B, because some of it is used to accelerate block A.

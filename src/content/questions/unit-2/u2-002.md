stem: |
  A block of mass $m$ is on a frictionless ramp inclined at an angle $\theta$
  above the horizontal. A horizontal force of size $F$ pushes on the block, as
  shown, and the block slides up the ramp. What is the net force on the block
  along the ramp, taking up the ramp as positive?
choices:
  A: '$F\cos\theta - mg\sin\theta$'
  B: '$F - mg\sin\theta$'
  C: '$F\sin\theta - mg\cos\theta$'
  D: '$F\cos\theta - mg\cos\theta$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The push is horizontal, not along the ramp. Only its component along the ramp, $F\cos\theta$, pushes the block up the slope.'
  C: 'The sine and cosine are swapped for both forces. Check $\theta = 0$, a flat floor: the net force should be just $F$, but this gives $-mg$.'
  D: 'Gravity''s component along the ramp is $mg\sin\theta$. The $mg\cos\theta$ part points into the ramp and is balanced by the normal force.'
---

Use axes along and perpendicular to the ramp, and find each force's component along the ramp.

- **Gravity:** its component down the slope is $mg\sin\theta$.
- **The push:** the horizontal push makes an angle $\theta$ with the ramp surface, so its component up the slope is $F\cos\theta$.
- **Normal force:** perpendicular to the ramp, so it has no component along it.

$$
F_{\text{net}} = F\cos\theta - mg\sin\theta
$$

Check the extremes: at $\theta = 0$ this gives $F$ (a push on a flat, frictionless floor), and at $\theta = 90°$ it gives $-mg$ (a horizontal push against a vertical wall does nothing to lift the block).

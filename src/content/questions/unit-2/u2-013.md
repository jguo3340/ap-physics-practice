stem: |
  A box sits on the floor of an elevator that is moving downward and speeding
  up. Let $N$ be the force the floor exerts on the box, $P$ the force the box
  exerts on the floor, and $W$ the box's weight. Which of the following
  correctly compares the sizes of these forces?
choices:
  A: '$N = P = W$'
  B: '$N = P < W$'
  C: '$N < P = W$'
  D: '$N = P > W$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'If $N$ equaled $W$, the net force on the box would be zero. The box is accelerating, so the forces on it cannot balance.'
  C: '$N$ and $P$ are a third-law pair, so they are always equal. The box does not push on the floor with its full weight while it accelerates downward.'
  D: 'A floor push larger than the weight would accelerate the box upward. Speeding up while moving down means the acceleration points down.'
---

**Direction of acceleration:** the elevator moves down and is speeding up, so its acceleration (and the box's) points **down**.

**Forces on the box:** only $W$ (down) and $N$ (up). For the net force to point down, the weight must be larger:

$$
W - N = ma \quad\Rightarrow\quad N = W - ma < W
$$

**Third law:** $N$ (floor on box) and $P$ (box on floor) are a third-law pair, so $N = P$ always.

Together, $N = P < W$. That is why you feel lighter for a moment when a downward elevator starts moving: a bathroom scale under you would read less than your weight.

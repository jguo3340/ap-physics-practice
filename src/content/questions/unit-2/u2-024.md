stem: |
  A space probe has weight $W_0$ when it sits on the surface of a planet of
  radius $R_0$. The probe is then carried to a point $2R_0$ above the planet's
  surface. What is the gravitational force the planet exerts on the probe
  there?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac14 W_0$'
  B: '$\tfrac19 W_0$'
  C: '$\tfrac13 W_0$'
  D: '$\tfrac12 W_0$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'This uses the height above the surface, $2R_0$, as the distance in the force law. Gravity depends on the distance from the planet''s center, which is $3R_0$.'
  C: 'This uses the right distance, $3R_0$, but forgets to square it. Gravity falls off as $1/r^2$.'
  D: 'This uses the height $2R_0$ as the distance and also forgets to square it.'
---

Newton's law of gravitation uses the distance between **centers**, $F = GMm/r^2$.

- On the surface: $r = R_0$, so $W_0 = GMm/R_0^2$.
- At a height of $2R_0$ above the surface: $r = R_0 + 2R_0 = 3R_0$.

$$
F = \frac{GMm}{(3R_0)^2} = \frac19 \cdot \frac{GMm}{R_0^2} = \frac19 W_0
$$

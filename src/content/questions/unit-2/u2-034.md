stem: |
  Box P sits on top of box Q, which rests on a rough floor. A student pushes
  horizontally to the right on box Q only, and both boxes move together to the
  right at a constant speed, with P not slipping on Q. Which of the following
  describes the horizontal forces exerted on box P?
choices:
  A: 'A friction force to the right from box Q'
  B: 'A friction force to the left from box Q'
  C: 'The student''s push, passed through box Q to the right'
  D: 'There are no horizontal forces on box P.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'A forward friction force would make box P speed up. Box P moves at a constant velocity, so it needs no horizontal force at all.'
  B: 'A backward friction force on box P would slow it down. Nothing is trying to make P slip relative to Q, so friction between them is zero.'
  C: 'The student touches only box Q. A force is exerted by one object on another that it touches, and the student does not touch box P.'
---

Look at box P by itself. It moves at a **constant velocity**, so the net force on it is zero.

- Vertical: gravity down, and box Q's normal force up. These balance.
- Horizontal: the only thing touching box P is box Q. If Q exerted a friction force on P, that would be the only horizontal force, and P would accelerate.

So friction between the boxes must be zero. Static friction only appears when something tries to make two surfaces slip, and at constant velocity nothing does.

Box Q is different. It has the student's push to the right and friction from the floor to the left, and they balance.

stem: |
  Two asteroids of different masses orbit each other while, as a pair, they
  orbit a distant planet. Consider the two asteroids as one system and ignore
  the gravity of everything except the planet and the asteroids. Which way does
  the acceleration of the system's center of mass point?
choices:
  A: 'Toward the planet'
  B: 'Toward the more massive asteroid'
  C: 'Along the direction the pair is moving'
  D: 'The center of mass does not accelerate, because the asteroids'' pulls on each other cancel.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The pull between the asteroids is internal to the system. Internal forces cannot accelerate the center of mass.'
  C: 'The pair''s path curves around the planet, so its acceleration points toward the center of the curve, not along the motion.'
  D: 'The internal pulls do cancel, but the planet''s gravity is an external force on the system, and it is not balanced.'
---

The acceleration of a system's center of mass depends only on the **external** forces:

$$
\vec a_{cm} = \frac{\sum \vec F_{\text{ext}}}{m_{\text{total}}}
$$

The asteroids pull on each other, but those forces are internal: equal and opposite, so they cancel in the sum. The only external force is the planet's gravity on each asteroid, and both pulls point toward the planet.

So the center of mass accelerates toward the planet, which is just what it needs to follow its orbit. The asteroids can tumble around each other in any way, and the center of mass still follows the same curved path.

stem: |
  A book rests on a board that is tilted at an angle $\theta$, and the book does
  not slide. Consider the total force the board exerts on the book: the normal
  force and static friction together. What are its size and direction?
choices:
  A: 'Size $mg\cos\theta$, directed perpendicular to the board'
  B: 'Size $mg$, directed straight up'
  C: 'Size $mg\sin\theta$, directed up along the board'
  D: 'Size greater than $mg$, directed straight up'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is only the normal force. Friction also acts on the book, and the total force includes both.'
  C: 'That is only the friction force. The board also pushes on the book with a normal force.'
  D: 'The book is at rest, so the board''s total force must exactly balance gravity, no more.'
---

Only two objects exert forces on the book: Earth (gravity, $mg$ down) and the board. The book is at rest, so the net force is zero. That means the board's **total** force must exactly cancel gravity:

$$
\vec F_{\text{board}} = mg \text{ upward}
$$

You can check it with the pieces. The normal force is $mg\cos\theta$ and friction is $mg\sin\theta$, and they are perpendicular, so the combined size is

$$
\sqrt{(mg\cos\theta)^2 + (mg\sin\theta)^2} = mg
$$

Its direction is straight up, whatever the tilt.

stem: |
  A block of mass $m$ rests on a rough ramp inclined at an angle $\theta$. A
  student pushes on the block with a force of size $P$ directed down the ramp,
  parallel to its surface, and the block stays at rest. What is the size of the
  static friction force on the block?
choices:
  A: '$\mu_s mg\cos\theta$'
  B: '$mg\sin\theta$'
  C: '$mg\sin\theta + P$'
  D: '$P - mg\sin\theta$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'That is the largest static friction can possibly be. Static friction is only as big as it needs to be to hold the block, which may be less.'
  B: 'That would hold the block if nobody were pushing. The push down the ramp adds to what friction has to resist.'
  D: 'Gravity''s component and the push both point down the ramp, so they add. They do not oppose each other.'
---

Look at the forces along the ramp. Two of them point down the slope:

- gravity's component, $mg\sin\theta$
- the student's push, $P$

The block stays at rest, so the net force along the ramp is zero. Static friction must point up the ramp and balance both:

$$
F_s = mg\sin\theta + P
$$

The expression $\mu_s F_N$ is only the **maximum** static friction. Unless the block is just about to slip, the actual friction is whatever the other forces require.

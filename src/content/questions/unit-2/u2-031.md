stem: |
  A student pulls a crate across a rough, level floor with a horizontal force
  $F$, and the crate speeds up with acceleration $a$. The student then pulls
  the same crate on the same floor with a horizontal force $2F$. Which of the
  following describes the crate's new acceleration?
choices:
  A: 'It is still $a$, because friction grows to match the larger pull.'
  B: 'It is between $a$ and $2a$.'
  C: 'It is exactly $2a$.'
  D: 'It is greater than $2a$.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Kinetic friction does not grow with the pull. It depends on the normal force, which has not changed.'
  B: 'Friction stays the same size, so all of the extra pull goes into acceleration. That gives more than double, not less.'
  C: 'The acceleration would double only if the net force doubled. Doubling the pull more than doubles the net force, because friction does not double.'
---

Kinetic friction $f$ depends only on the normal force, so it is the same in both cases.

**First pull:**

$$
F - f = ma \quad\Rightarrow\quad a = \frac{F - f}{m}
$$

**Second pull:**

$$
a' = \frac{2F - f}{m}
$$

Compare with $2a$:

$$
2a = \frac{2F - 2f}{m}
$$

The numerator of $a'$ is larger by $f$, so

$$
a' = 2a + \frac{f}{m} > 2a
$$

For example, if $F = 30\ \text{N}$ and $f = 10\ \text{N}$, the net force goes from 20 N to 50 N, which is 2.5 times as large.

stem: |
  A soccer ball is kicked at an angle above level ground and flies through the
  air. Air resistance is negligible. At the highest point of its path, how is
  the net force on the ball directed compared with the ball's velocity?
choices:
  A: 'In the same direction as the velocity'
  B: 'Opposite to the velocity'
  C: 'Perpendicular to the velocity'
  D: 'There is no net force at the highest point.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The only force is gravity, which points down. At the top the ball is moving horizontally, so the force cannot point along the velocity.'
  B: 'The force would be opposite the velocity only if the ball were moving straight up. At the top its velocity is horizontal.'
  D: 'Only the vertical velocity is zero at the top. Gravity still acts there, with the same strength as everywhere else in the flight.'
---

With no air resistance, the only force on the ball during the whole flight is gravity, $mg$, straight down.

At the highest point the ball has stopped rising but has not started falling, so its vertical velocity is zero. It is still moving horizontally, though, so its velocity points horizontally.

A downward force and a horizontal velocity are perpendicular. This is the only point in the flight where that is true.

stem: |
  After a quick shove, a box slides up a rough ramp tilted $30^\circ$ above the
  horizontal. The gravitational force on the box is 50 N, and the kinetic
  frictional force on it is 6 N. What is the net force on the box while it
  slides up the ramp?
choices:
  A: '19 N down the ramp'
  B: '25 N down the ramp'
  C: '31 N down the ramp'
  D: '56 N down the ramp'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'This has friction pointing up the ramp. Kinetic friction opposes the sliding, and the box slides up, so friction points down the ramp.'
  B: 'This is only the part of gravity along the ramp. Friction also acts along the ramp while the box slides.'
  D: 'This uses the whole weight. Only the part of gravity parallel to the ramp, $mg \sin 30^\circ$, acts along the ramp. The rest is balanced by the normal force.'
---

Once the shove is over, the forces on the box are gravity, the normal force, and kinetic friction.

**Perpendicular to the ramp:** the normal force balances the perpendicular part of gravity, so nothing is left over in that direction.

**Along the ramp:**

- Gravity: $mg \sin 30^\circ = (50)(0.5) = 25\ \text{N}$ down the ramp.
- Friction: the box slides **up**, so friction points **down** the ramp, 6 N.

Both point the same way:

$$
F_{\text{net}} = 25 + 6 = 31\ \text{N down the ramp}
$$

That is why a box slows down faster going up a rough ramp than it speeds up coming down. On the way down, friction would point up the ramp and the net force would be only 19 N.

stem: |
  A rope pulls a box horizontally across a rough, level floor with a constant
  tension $F_T$. The box's speed increases by 3.0 m/s every second. The rope
  is then released, and the box's speed decreases by 1.0 m/s every second
  until it stops. What is the size of the friction force on the box?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac14 F_T$'
  B: '$\tfrac13 F_T$'
  C: '$\tfrac34 F_T$'
  D: '$F_T$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'This compares the two accelerations directly, as if the tension alone caused the 3.0 m/s² acceleration. Friction was acting during the pull too.'
  C: 'That is the net force during the pull, $F_T - F_f$. Friction is the smaller part.'
  D: 'Friction equals the tension only when the box moves at a constant speed. Here it speeds up, so the tension is larger.'
---

Friction acts the whole time, with the same size, because the box slides on the same floor at all times.

**After release:** friction is the only horizontal force, and it slows the box at 1.0 m/s²:

$$
F_f = m(1.0)
$$

**During the pull:** the net force is the tension minus friction, and the box speeds up at 3.0 m/s²:

$$
F_T - F_f = m(3.0)
$$

$$
F_T = m(3.0) + m(1.0) = m(4.0)
$$

So $F_f = m(1.0) = \tfrac14 F_T$.

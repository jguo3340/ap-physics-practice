stem: |
  A 5.0 kg box and a 3.0 kg box sit on a rough, level floor, joined by a light
  horizontal rope. A student pulls horizontally on the 5.0 kg box, and both boxes
  speed up together at $1.5\ \text{m/s}^2$. The coefficient of kinetic friction
  between each box and the floor is 0.20. What is the magnitude of the
  student's pull?
choices:
  A: '12 N'
  B: '16 N'
  C: '22 N'
  D: '28 N'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'This is only the force needed to accelerate the boxes, $(8.0)(1.5)$. The student also has to overcome friction on both boxes.'
  B: 'This is only the total friction. The boxes are speeding up, so the pull must be larger than friction by $ma$.'
  C: 'This includes friction on the 5.0 kg box only. The 3.0 kg box drags on the floor too, and the student pulls it through the rope.'
---

Treat both boxes and the rope as one system of mass $8.0\ \text{kg}$. The rope's pulls are internal, so the horizontal forces from outside are the student's pull $F$ (forward) and friction on each box (backward).

Total friction:

$$
f = \mu_k (m_1 + m_2) g = (0.20)(8.0)(10) = 16\ \text{N}
$$

Newton's second law for the system:

$$
F - f = (m_1 + m_2)\,a
$$

$$
F = 16 + (8.0)(1.5) = 16 + 12 = 28\ \text{N}
$$

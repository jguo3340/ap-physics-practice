stem: |
  A car can just make it around a flat, circular curve of radius $R$ at speed
  $v$ without skidding. If the car instead travels at speed $2v$, what is the
  smallest radius of curve it can take on the same road without skidding?
choices:
  A: '$\tfrac14 R$'
  B: '$R$'
  C: '$2R$'
  D: '$4R$'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'Going faster requires a gentler curve, not a tighter one. A tighter curve needs even more friction.'
  B: 'Friction can only supply so much force. At twice the speed, the same curve would need four times as much.'
  C: 'The force needed depends on $v^2$, so doubling the speed requires four times the radius, not twice.'
---

On a flat curve, static friction supplies the centripetal force. The road can supply at most a fixed amount, $F_{\max} = \mu_s mg$, no matter how fast the car goes.

$$
\frac{mv^2}{r} \le F_{\max} \quad\Rightarrow\quad r_{\min} = \frac{mv^2}{F_{\max}}
$$

The smallest radius is proportional to $v^2$. Doubling the speed multiplies $v^2$ by 4, so the smallest radius becomes $4R$.

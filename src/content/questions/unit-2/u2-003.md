stem: |
  A uniform ladder leans against a smooth, frictionless wall. Its bottom rests
  on a rough floor, and the ladder is at rest. How does the size of the normal
  force from the floor on the ladder compare with the ladder's weight?
choices:
  A: 'It is less than the weight, because the wall holds up part of the ladder.'
  B: 'It is equal to the weight.'
  C: 'It is greater than the weight, because the wall pushes the ladder down into the floor.'
  D: 'It depends on the angle of the ladder.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'A frictionless wall can only push straight out, perpendicular to its surface. A horizontal push cannot hold anything up.'
  C: 'The wall''s push is horizontal, so it does not push the ladder downward.'
  D: 'The angle changes the horizontal forces (the wall''s push and floor friction), but the vertical forces are always just the weight and the floor''s normal force.'
---

The ladder is at rest, so the net force on it is zero in both directions. Look at the vertical forces only.

- The **wall** is frictionless, so it exerts only a normal force, perpendicular to the wall. That force is horizontal.
- The **floor** exerts a normal force (up) and static friction (horizontal).
- **Gravity** pulls down.

The only vertical forces are the floor's normal force and gravity, so they must balance:

$$
F_N = mg
$$

The horizontal forces balance separately: floor friction pushes toward the wall and the wall pushes back the other way.

stem: |
  A worker pushes a crate of mass $m$ up a rough ramp inclined at an angle
  $\theta$, pushing parallel to the ramp so that the crate moves at a constant
  speed. The coefficient of kinetic friction is $\mu_k$. What is the size of the
  friction force on the crate?
choices:
  A: '$\mu_k mg\cos\theta$'
  B: '$\mu_k mg$'
  C: '$mg\sin\theta$'
  D: '$\mu_k mg\sin\theta$'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'On a ramp the normal force is less than the weight. It balances only the perpendicular part of gravity, $mg\cos\theta$.'
  C: 'Friction would balance gravity''s component only if nothing else were pushing along the ramp. Here the worker''s push is what balances both.'
  D: 'Friction depends on the normal force, which balances the part of gravity perpendicular to the ramp: $mg\cos\theta$, not $mg\sin\theta$.'
---

Kinetic friction is $F_f = \mu_k F_N$, so first find the normal force.

Perpendicular to the ramp, the crate does not accelerate. The worker pushes parallel to the ramp, so only gravity and the normal force act in that direction:

$$
F_N = mg\cos\theta \quad\Rightarrow\quad F_f = \mu_k mg\cos\theta
$$

The constant speed is a distraction. It tells you the worker's push balances friction plus $mg\sin\theta$, but it does not change the size of the friction force itself.

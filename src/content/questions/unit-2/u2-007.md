stem: |
  A car starts from rest and speeds up along a level road without its tires
  slipping. Which force is responsible for the car's forward acceleration?
choices:
  A: 'The force of the engine on the car'
  B: 'The normal force of the road on the tires'
  C: 'Static friction from the road on the tires, pointing forward'
  D: 'Kinetic friction from the road on the tires, pointing forward'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'The engine is part of the car, so its forces are internal. An internal force cannot accelerate the car as a whole; an outside object has to push it.'
  B: 'The normal force is vertical, so it cannot speed the car up horizontally.'
  D: 'Kinetic friction acts only when surfaces slide against each other. The tires are not slipping, so the friction is static.'
---

To accelerate the car, a force from **outside** the car must act on it. The engine only turns the wheels; it is part of the car.

As a drive wheel turns, the bottom of the tire pushes backward on the road. By Newton's third law, the road pushes forward on the tire. That forward push is friction, and because the tire grips the road without slipping, it is **static** friction.

This is why cars struggle on ice: the engine works fine, but the road can't supply much friction, so the wheels just spin.

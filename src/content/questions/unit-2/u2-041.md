stem: |
  A box is given a quick push so that it slides down a rough ramp. A student
  wants to predict whether the box will then slide at a constant velocity. What
  is the smallest set of information that is enough to decide?
choices:
  A: 'The box''s mass and the ramp''s angle'
  B: 'The box''s mass, the ramp''s angle, and the coefficient of kinetic friction'
  C: 'The ramp''s angle and the coefficient of kinetic friction'
  D: 'The coefficient of kinetic friction and the value of $g$'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'Without the coefficient of friction there is no way to know how strong friction is compared with the pull down the ramp.'
  B: 'This is enough, but it is not the smallest set. The mass cancels out, so it is not needed.'
  D: 'Without the angle you cannot compare the downhill pull with friction. And $g$ cancels out, just like the mass.'
---

The box slides at a constant velocity if the forces along the ramp balance: the downhill part of gravity equals kinetic friction.

$$
mg \sin\theta = \mu_k N = \mu_k\, mg \cos\theta
$$

The mass $m$ and $g$ appear on both sides and cancel:

$$
\tan\theta = \mu_k
$$

So the angle and the coefficient of kinetic friction are all you need. If $\tan\theta = \mu_k$, the box keeps a constant velocity; if $\tan\theta > \mu_k$ it speeds up; if $\tan\theta < \mu_k$ it slows down.

stem: |
  Block 1 sits on a level, frictionless table and is tied by a light string
  that runs over a light, frictionless pulley to block 2, which hangs. A student
  releases the blocks and measures their acceleration $a$. The student then adds
  mass to the two-block system and repeats the trial several times. The table
  shows the total mass $M$ of the two blocks and the acceleration for each
  trial.

  $\begin{array}{c|c} M\ (\text{kg}) & a\ (\text{m/s}^2) \\ \hline 2.0 & 2.50 \\ 4.0 & 1.25 \\ 5.0 & 1.00 \\ 8.0 & 0.625 \end{array}$

  Which of the following best describes how the mass was added?
choices:
  A: 'Mass was added only to block 1.'
  B: 'Mass was added only to block 2.'
  C: 'Mass was added equally to block 1 and block 2.'
  D: 'The data cannot tell which block the mass was added to.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'Adding mass to the hanging block increases the force pulling the system. The acceleration would rise toward $g$, not fall.'
  C: 'Adding to block 2 increases the pulling force, so the product $Ma$ would grow from trial to trial. In the data it stays at 5.0 N.'
  D: 'The product $Ma$ equals the weight of block 2, so the data do reveal block 2''s mass. It stays at 0.50 kg in every trial.'
---

For the whole system, the only outside force pulling it along is the weight of the hanging block, $m_2 g$:

$$
m_2 g = M a
$$

Multiply $M$ by $a$ for each trial:

$$
(2.0)(2.50) = (4.0)(1.25) = 5.0\ \text{N}
$$

$$
(5.0)(1.00) = (8.0)(0.625) = 5.0\ \text{N}
$$

The product is the same every time, so the hanging weight never changed:

$$
m_2 = \frac{5.0\ \text{N}}{10\ \text{m/s}^2} = 0.50\ \text{kg}
$$

All of the added mass must have gone onto block 1, on the table. That made the system heavier without increasing the force that pulls it, so the acceleration dropped.

stem: |
  A spacecraft drifts through deep space, far from any planet or star. It fires
  a thruster that pushes it with a force of constant size, always directed
  perpendicular to its velocity. What path does the spacecraft follow?
choices:
  A: 'A straight line, speeding up'
  B: 'A circle, at a constant speed'
  C: 'A spiral that widens as it speeds up'
  D: 'A parabola, like a thrown ball'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'To speed up in a straight line, the force would have to point along the velocity. A perpendicular force changes only the direction.'
  C: 'A force perpendicular to the velocity does no work, so the speed cannot increase.'
  D: 'A thrown ball follows a parabola because gravity always points the same way. Here the force turns as the spacecraft turns, always staying perpendicular.'
---

A force perpendicular to the velocity cannot speed an object up or slow it down; it only changes the direction of motion. So the speed stays constant.

The force has a constant size and always points sideways to the motion, so the spacecraft turns at a steady rate. Constant speed plus a constant-size force that always points perpendicular to the velocity is exactly uniform circular motion. The force points toward the center of the circle, with

$$
F = \frac{mv^2}{r}
$$

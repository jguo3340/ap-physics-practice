stem: |
  A pendulum is made from a small steel ball on a light string. The steel ball is
  replaced with a plastic ball of the same size but much smaller mass, and the
  pendulum is released from the same small angle as before. Compared with the
  original pendulum, how do the new pendulum's period, maximum speed, and
  maximum kinetic energy compare?
choices:
  A: 'Period the same; maximum speed the same; maximum kinetic energy smaller'
  B: 'Period shorter; maximum speed greater; maximum kinetic energy the same'
  C: 'Period the same; maximum speed smaller; maximum kinetic energy smaller'
  D: 'Period longer; maximum speed the same; maximum kinetic energy smaller'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'A pendulum''s period, $2\pi\sqrt{\ell/g}$, doesn''t depend on the mass at all. And with less mass, the same speed means less kinetic energy, not the same.'
  C: 'The maximum speed comes from $mgh = \tfrac12 mv^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2gh}$. The mass cancels, and the ball drops the same height, so the maximum speed is the same.'
  D: 'Mass does not appear in the period of a pendulum, $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\ell/g}$. The string length and $g$ are unchanged, so the period is unchanged.'
---

Look at each quantity in turn. Only the mass changes: the string length, $g$, and the release angle (so the height the ball drops) stay the same.

- **Period:** $T = 2\pi\sqrt{\ell/g}$ has no mass in it, so the period is the same.
- **Maximum speed:** at the bottom, all the potential energy has become kinetic: $mgh = \tfrac12 m v^2$, so $v = \sqrt{2gh}$. The mass cancels, so the maximum speed is the same.
- **Maximum kinetic energy:** $\tfrac12 m v^2$ with the same $v$ and a smaller $m$ is smaller.

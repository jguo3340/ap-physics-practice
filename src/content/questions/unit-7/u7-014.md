stem: |
  A block oscillating on a spring and a simple pendulum swinging at a small
  angle both have a period of 1.0 s. Which single change increases the period of
  the block–spring system but leaves the period of the pendulum unchanged?
choices:
  A: 'Replacing the block and the pendulum bob with objects of twice the mass'
  B: 'Taking both to a planet with a larger gravitational field'
  C: 'Doubling the amplitude of both, keeping the pendulum''s angle small'
  D: 'Using a stiffer spring and a longer pendulum string'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'A block–spring period does not depend on $g$, and a pendulum''s period gets shorter where $g$ is larger. This changes the pendulum only, and in the wrong way.'
  C: 'Neither period depends on amplitude, as long as the pendulum''s angle stays small. Neither period changes.'
  D: 'A stiffer spring shortens the block–spring period, and a longer string lengthens the pendulum''s period. Both effects go the opposite way from what is needed.'
---

Compare the two period formulas:

$$
T_{\text{spring}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}} \qquad T_{\text{pendulum}} = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{L}{g}}
$$

The block–spring period depends on the mass. The pendulum's period does not depend on the bob's mass at all, because a heavier bob has both more weight pulling it and more inertia to move, and the two effects cancel.

Doubling both masses makes the block–spring period $\sqrt2$ times longer, about 1.4 s, while the pendulum stays at 1.0 s.

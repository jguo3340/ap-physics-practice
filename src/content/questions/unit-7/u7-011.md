stem: |
  A block of mass $m_0$ on a frictionless, level surface is attached to a spring
  with spring constant $k_0$. The block is pulled a distance $A_0$ from
  equilibrium and released from rest, and the block–spring system oscillates
  with total mechanical energy $E_0$. Which combination would give the system a
  total mechanical energy of $\tfrac12 E_0$?
choices:
  A: 'Spring constant $\tfrac14 k_0$, pulled $2A_0$, mass $m_0$'
  B: 'Spring constant $\tfrac12 k_0$, pulled $A_0$, mass $2m_0$'
  C: 'Spring constant $2k_0$, pulled $\tfrac12 A_0$, mass $m_0$'
  D: 'Spring constant $k_0$, pulled $\tfrac12 A_0$, mass $2m_0$'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'Doubling the stretch multiplies the energy by 4, which cancels the factor of $\tfrac14$ from the spring constant. The energy stays $E_0$.'
  C: 'Halving the stretch divides the energy by 4, and doubling the spring constant only brings back a factor of 2. That gives $\tfrac14 E_0$.'
  D: 'The mass does not affect the energy, and halving the stretch divides the energy by 4, giving $\tfrac14 E_0$.'
---

The block starts at rest, so all of the energy is stored in the spring when it is released:

$$
E = \tfrac12 k A^2
$$

The mass does not appear. It changes how fast the block oscillates, but not the energy. Check each choice:

- $\tfrac12\left(\tfrac14 k_0\right)(2A_0)^2 = \tfrac12 k_0 A_0^2 = E_0$
- $\tfrac12\left(\tfrac12 k_0\right)(A_0)^2 = \tfrac12 E_0$
- $\tfrac12 (2k_0)\left(\tfrac12 A_0\right)^2 = \tfrac14 E_0$
- $\tfrac12 (k_0)\left(\tfrac12 A_0\right)^2 = \tfrac14 E_0$

Only the second combination gives $\tfrac12 E_0$.

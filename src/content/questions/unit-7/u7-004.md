stem: |
  Blocks P and Q are attached to identical ideal springs and oscillate on a
  horizontal frictionless surface. The graphs show the position $x$ of each
  block as a function of time $t$. Which statement comparing block Q with
  block P is correct?
choices:
  A: 'Q has twice the mass of P, because Q''s period is twice as long.'
  B: 'Q has four times the mass of P, because the period of a block on a spring is proportional to the square root of its mass.'
  C: 'Each cycle takes Q longer because Q travels a greater distance in each cycle.'
  D: 'Q''s maximum speed is greater than P''s, because Q''s amplitude is greater.'
answer: B
why_wrong:
  A: 'The period goes as the square root of the mass. Doubling the period takes $2^2 = 4$ times the mass.'
  C: 'The period of a block on a spring, $2\pi\sqrt{m/k}$, does not depend on the amplitude. Pulling a block farther makes it move faster, not take longer.'
  D: 'Q goes twice as far out, but it oscillates at half the angular frequency, so $v_{\max} = A\omega$ comes out the same: about 13 cm/s for both.'
---

From the graphs: P has amplitude 4 cm and period 2 s; Q has amplitude 8 cm and period 4 s.

The period of a block on a spring is

$$
T = 2\pi\sqrt{\frac{m}{k}}
$$

The springs are identical, so $k$ is the same and $T \propto \sqrt{m}$. Q's period is 2 times P's, so

$$
\frac{m_Q}{m_P} = \left(\frac{T_Q}{T_P}\right)^2 = 2^2 = 4
$$

The amplitude plays no part in the period. It does affect the maximum speed, $v_{\max} = A\omega = 2\pi A / T$, but here the larger amplitude and the longer period cancel: $2\pi(4\ \text{cm})/(2\ \text{s})$ and $2\pi(8\ \text{cm})/(4\ \text{s})$ are both about 13 cm/s.

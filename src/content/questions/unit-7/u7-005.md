stem: |
  A cart attached to a spring oscillates on a track. A motion sensor records the
  cart's position $x$ relative to equilibrium, and part of the slightly noisy
  data is shown. Which of the following is the best way to find the cart's
  velocity at $t = 0.50$ s?
choices:
  A: 'Draw a smooth curve that best fits the data, and find the slope of the line tangent to that curve at $t = 0.50$ s.'
  B: 'Read the value of the graph at $t = 0.50$ s.'
  C: 'Find the change in position from $t = 0$ to $t = 0.50$ s and divide it by 0.50 s.'
  D: 'Find the slope of the straight line from the origin of the graph to the data point at $t = 0.50$ s.'
answer: A
why_wrong:
  B: 'The value of a position graph is the position, not the velocity. Velocity is how fast the position is changing.'
  C: 'That gives the average velocity over the first half second, during which the cart turned around. It is not the velocity at the instant $t = 0.50$ s.'
  D: 'That is not a slope of the motion at all: the line from the origin skips over everything the cart did in between.'
---

Velocity at an instant is the slope of the position–time graph at that instant, which is the slope of the tangent line there.

With noisy data, a tangent drawn to the jagged readings would wobble with the noise. Fitting a smooth curve through the data first averages out the noise, and then the tangent to the smooth curve at 0.50 s gives the instantaneous velocity.

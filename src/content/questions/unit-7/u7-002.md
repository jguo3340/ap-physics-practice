stem: |
  A block attached to a horizontal spring slides back and forth on a frictionless
  surface between $x = -A$ and $x = +A$. Which statement about the total
  mechanical energy of the block–spring system is correct?
choices:
  A: 'It is greatest at $x = 0$, where the block moves fastest.'
  B: 'It is greatest at $x = \pm A$, where the spring is stretched or squeezed the most.'
  C: 'It is smallest halfway between $x = 0$ and $x = A$.'
  D: 'It is the same at every position of the block.'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'At $x = 0$ the kinetic energy is greatest, but the spring energy is zero there. The total is no bigger than anywhere else.'
  B: 'At $x = \pm A$ the spring energy is greatest, but the block is momentarily at rest, so the kinetic energy is zero. The total is the same as everywhere else.'
  C: 'Halfway out, the energy is split between kinetic and spring energy, but the two still add up to the same total.'
---

With no friction, nothing outside the block–spring system does work on it, so its total mechanical energy stays constant. What changes is how the energy is split:

- At $x = \pm A$ it is all spring energy: $\tfrac12 k A^2$.
- At $x = 0$ it is all kinetic energy: $\tfrac12 m v_{\max}^2$.
- Everywhere in between it is part of each, always adding up to the same total, $\tfrac12 k A^2$.

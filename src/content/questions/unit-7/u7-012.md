stem: |
  A block on a frictionless, level surface is attached to a spring whose other
  end is fixed to a wall. The block is pulled away from the wall, stretching the
  spring, and released from rest at $t = 0$. Take the direction away from the
  wall as positive. Which describes the force $F$ the spring exerts on the block
  during one period $T$?
choices:
  A: '$F$ starts at zero, is most positive at $\tfrac14 T$, zero at $\tfrac12 T$, and most negative at $\tfrac34 T$.'
  B: '$F$ starts at its most positive value, is zero at $\tfrac14 T$, and most negative at $\tfrac12 T$.'
  C: '$F$ starts at its most negative value, is zero at $\tfrac14 T$, and most positive at $\tfrac12 T$.'
  D: '$F$ starts at zero, is most negative at $\tfrac14 T$, zero at $\tfrac12 T$, and most positive at $\tfrac34 T$.'
answer: C
why_wrong:
  A: 'At $t = 0$ the spring is stretched as far as it will go, so its force is largest then, not zero.'
  B: 'A stretched spring pulls the block back toward the wall, which is the negative direction. The force starts out negative.'
  D: 'The force is zero when the block passes through equilibrium, which first happens at $\tfrac14 T$, not at $t = 0$.'
---

The spring force is $F = -kx$, so it always points opposite the block's displacement.

- $t = 0$: the block is at $x = +A$, as far from the wall as it gets. The force is $-kA$, its most negative value.
- $t = \tfrac14 T$: the block passes through equilibrium, $x = 0$, so $F = 0$.
- $t = \tfrac12 T$: the block is at $x = -A$, closest to the wall. The spring is compressed and pushes outward, $F = +kA$.
- $t = \tfrac34 T$: back through equilibrium, $F = 0$.
- $t = T$: back at $x = +A$, $F = -kA$.

So the force graph is an upside-down cosine curve: it starts at its most negative value and is zero at $\tfrac14 T$.

stem: |
  A block attached to a horizontal spring oscillates on a frictionless surface
  between $x = -A$ and $x = +A$, with $x = 0$ at the equilibrium position. The
  graph shows some quantity $Q$ for the block against the block's position $x$.
  Which quantity could $Q$ be?
choices:
  A: 'The elastic potential energy of the spring'
  B: 'The kinetic energy of the block'
  C: 'The speed of the block'
  D: 'The magnitude of the net force on the block'
answer: D
why_wrong:
  A: 'The elastic potential energy is $\tfrac12 kx^2$, which curves upward like a parabola. This graph is made of straight lines.'
  B: 'The kinetic energy is largest at $x = 0$, where the block moves fastest, and zero at the turning points. That is the opposite of this graph.'
  C: 'The block moves fastest at $x = 0$ and stops at $\pm A$, so the speed is largest in the middle. This graph is zero in the middle.'
---

The graph is zero at $x = 0$ and rises in straight lines to its largest values at $x = \pm A$, with the same shape on both sides.

The net force on the block is the spring force, $F = -kx$. Its magnitude is

$$
|F| = k|x|
$$

which is zero at equilibrium and grows in proportion to the distance from it. That is exactly a V shape.

The other choices fail:

- $U = \tfrac12 kx^2$ is a parabola, which is curved, not straight.
- Kinetic energy and speed are both largest at $x = 0$ and zero at $x = \pm A$, so they peak in the middle.
